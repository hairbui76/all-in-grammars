# Ability With Can And Could

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Can` and `could` express ability, but for a single successful occasion in the past English uses `was/were able to`, `managed to` or `succeeded in doing` instead of `could`.

## Form

`S + can / could + V(bare)` — general ability
`S + was / were able to + V` · `S + managed to + V` · `S + succeeded in + V-ing` — one specific past success
`S + couldn't + V(bare)` — negative, in every situation

## Examples — general ability

- I **can speak** English.
- What **could** you **do** when you were six months old?
- I **could lie** on my front.
- I **could cycle / ride** a bicycle when I was 8.

## Examples — one specific past occasion

- The fire spread quickly but everybody **managed to escape** from the building.
  (not *could escape*)

## Notes & exceptions

- `could` describes ability in general over a period of the past. In one particular situation it must be replaced by `was / were able to`, `managed to` or `succeeded in doing`.
- In the **negative**, `couldn't` may be used in all situations, general or specific.
- `be able to` also supplies the forms `can` lacks: *I have **been able to** swim since I was 5.* / *Will you **be able to** come tomorrow?*
