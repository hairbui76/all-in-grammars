# Active and Passive Voice

> [!abstract] In one line
> The active sentence puts the doer of the action in subject position; the passive puts the receiver there and makes the doer optional.

## Form

- Active: `S (doer) + V + Object`
- Passive: `S (receiver) + be + P2 (past participle) + (by + Agent)`

The general passive rule is always `to be + past participle`, with `be` carrying the tense of the original active verb.

## How to convert an active sentence

1. Identify the grammatical tense of the active verb — the passive `be` must take that same tense.
2. Move the object of the active sentence into subject position.
3. Put the active subject after `by` (or drop it if it is unknown or unimportant).
4. If the verb has two objects, two passive sentences are possible; normally the personal object is chosen as the passive subject.

## When to use

- Active: when the doer of the action is known and relevant.
- Passive: when the doer is unknown, or when the speaker wants to stress the receiver of the action rather than the doer.

## Examples

- My grandfather **bought** this house 20 years ago. → This house **was bought** by my grandfather 20 years ago.
- Newton **invented** the theory of gravitation. → The theory of gravitation **was invented** by Newton.
- Mr John **teaches** us English. → We **are taught** English by Mr John.

## Notes & exceptions

- `by + agent` is normally omitted when the agent is `they`, `people`, `someone`, or otherwise obvious.
- Only transitive verbs (verbs that take an object) can be made passive.
