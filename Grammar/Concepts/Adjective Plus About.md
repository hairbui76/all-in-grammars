# Adjective Plus About

> [!abstract] In one line
> `About` follows adjectives of emotion when what follows is the **subject or topic** that causes the feeling.

## Form

`be + adjective + about + noun / V-ing`

## List

| Combination | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| careless **about** | not taking care over | He is **careless about** his spelling. |
| concerned **about** | worried about | Parents are **concerned about** the exams. |
| confused **about** | muddled about | I am **confused about** the timetable. |
| excited **about** | eagerly looking forward to | She is **excited about** the trip. |
| happy **about** | glad about | We are **happy about** the decision. |
| sad **about** | unhappy about | He is **sad about** leaving. |
| serious **about** | not joking about | Are you **serious about** resigning? |
| upset **about** | distressed about | She was **upset about** the remark. |
| worried **about** | anxious about | I am **worried about** my results. |
| anxious **about** | uneasy about | They were **anxious about** the flight. |
| disappointed **about** sth | let down by a thing | We were **disappointed about** the score. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `disappointed **about**` is used for a thing, `disappointed **with**` for a person → see [[Adjective Plus With]].
- `concerned about` = worried; `concerned with` = connected to a subject.
- `anxious about` a danger, but `anxious for` a result you want → see [[Adjective Plus For]].
- Any verb after `about` takes the `-ing` form: `excited **about** going` → see [[Gerund After Prepositions]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I am worried for my results. → ✅ I am **worried about** my results.
- ❌ She is excited for going. → ✅ She is **excited about going**.

## Contrast with

- [[Adjective Plus At]] — an immediate reaction rather than a topic
- [[Adjective Plus Of]] — feelings of fear, pride and shame
