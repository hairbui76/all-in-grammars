# Adjective Plus To-Infinitive

> [!abstract] In one line
> Many adjectives are completed by a `to + V` phrase: *glad to see you*, *hard to explain*.

## Form

`S + be + adjective + to + V(bare)`
`S + be + adjective + (for + object) + to + V`
`It + be + adjective + to + V`

## When to use

- **Feelings and reactions**: `glad, happy, pleased, delighted, sorry, sad, surprised, astonished, disappointed, lucky, proud, relieved, ashamed`.
  - *I'm **glad to hear** the news.*
- **Willingness and readiness**: `ready, willing, eager, keen, reluctant, determined, prepared`.
  - *She is **ready to leave**.*
- **Ease and difficulty** — the subject is the **object** of the infinitive, so no object pronoun follows:
  - `easy, hard, difficult, simple, dangerous, expensive, impossible, pleasant`
  - *This problem is **easy to solve**.* (not *to solve it*)
- **Likelihood**: `likely, unlikely, certain, sure, bound, due, apt`.
  - *He is **likely to win**.*
- **Ordinals and superlatives**: `the first, the last, the next, the only, the best`.
  - *She was **the first to arrive**.*
- After `too` and `enough` → see [[Too With Infinitive]] and [[Enough With Infinitive]].

## Examples

- We were **sorry to hear** about your accident.
- The instructions are **difficult to follow**.
- They are **unlikely to accept** the terms.
- He was **the only student to finish** on time.
- I'm **happy to help** you at any time.

## Notes & exceptions

- With `easy / hard / difficult` type adjectives, do **not** repeat the object: ❌ *English is easy to learn it.*
- Some adjectives take a preposition + gerund instead: `interested in doing`, `good at doing`, `afraid of doing` → see [[Adjective Preposition Combinations]] and [[Gerund After Prepositions]].
- `afraid`, `sure`, `ashamed` allow both patterns with a meaning difference: *afraid **to ask*** (unwilling) vs *afraid **of failing*** (fear of something).
- The `for + object` variant introduces a different doer: *It is important **for students to arrive** on time.*

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I am interested to learn Japanese. → ✅ I am **interested in learning** Japanese.
- ❌ This book is easy to read it. → ✅ This book is **easy to read**.
- ❌ He is likely winning. → ✅ He is **likely to win**.
