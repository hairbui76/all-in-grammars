# Adjectives and Adverbs With the Same Form

> [!abstract] In one line
> A set of common words keeps exactly the same shape whether it modifies a noun (adjective) or a verb (adverb) — no `-ly` is added.

## The list

`back`, `deep*`, `direct*`, `early`, `enough`, `fast`, `hard*`, `high*`, `ill`, `just`, `kindly`, `late`, `left`, `little`, `long`, `low`, `much / more / most*`, `near*`, `pretty*`, `right*`, `still`, `straight`, `well`, `wrong`

Also the time words `daily`, `weekly`, `monthly`.

(* = these words also have an `-ly` form with a **different** meaning → see [[Adverbs With Two Forms]].)

## Examples

| Used as an adjective | Used as an adverb |
| --- | --- |
| the **back** door | Come **back** soon. |
| the most **direct** route | You can dial some **direct**. |
| a **fast** train | The train went **fast**. |
| The work is **hard**. | They worked **hard**. (= energetically) |
| You look **ill / well**. | an **ill**-made road |
| the **right** answer | Turn **right** here. |
| a **straight** line | She went **straight** home. |
| This is the **wrong** way. | He led us **wrong**. |

Time words:

- Tom was **late**. (adjective) — Tom came **late**. (adverb)
- It's a **weekly** paper. (adjective) — We buy it **weekly**. (adverb)

## Common mistakes

- ❌ The train went fastly. → ✅ The train went **fast**.
- ❌ They worked hardly. → ✅ They worked **hard**. (`hardly` means "almost not")
