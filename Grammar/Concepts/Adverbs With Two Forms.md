# Adverbs With Two Forms

> [!abstract] In one line
> Some adjectives give **two** adverbs — a bare form with the literal meaning and an `-ly` form with a completely different, usually figurative, meaning.

## The pairs

| Adjective | Adverb (same form) | Adverb in `-ly` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hard | hard = with effort | **hardly** = almost not, scarcely |
| late | late = not on time | **lately** = recently |
| high | high = at a great height | **highly** = very much, with great esteem |
| near | near = close by | **nearly** = almost |
| deep | deep = far down | **deeply** = intensely, profoundly |
| direct | direct = without stopping | **directly** = immediately / indirectly |
| pretty | — | **prettily** = in a pretty way (`pretty` alone = fairly, quite) |
| right | right = correctly, straight | **rightly** = justly, correctly (with a past participle) |
| just | just = only, exactly | **justly** = fairly, in a just way |
| most | most = to the greatest degree | **mostly** = chiefly, mainly |

## Examples

- I've **hardly** got any clean clothes left. (= almost none)
- They worked **hard** all day. (= with effort)
- I haven't been to the cinema **lately**. (= recently)
- Have you seen him **lately**?
- He was **deeply** offended.
- He'll be here **directly**. (= at once)
- The new regulations will affect us **directly / indirectly**.
- They spoke very **highly** of him.
- He was a **highly** paid official.
- I'm **nearly** ready.
- Her little girls are always **prettily** dressed.
- The exam was **pretty** difficult. (= fairly, quite)
- He was **rightly / justly** punished.
- I was **rightly / correctly** informed.

## Notes & exceptions

- `hardly`, `scarcely` and `barely` are negative in meaning: a sentence containing them takes no second negative.
- `rightly` and `justly` are typically used with a past participle: *rightly punished*, *rightly informed*.
- `pretty` as an adverb of degree means "fairly / rather", which is unrelated to the adjective `pretty` (= attractive).

## Common mistakes

- ❌ He works hardly. (meaning "he works a lot") → ✅ He works **hard**.
- ❌ I saw him late. (meaning "recently") → ✅ I saw him **lately**.
- ❌ I don't hardly know him. → ✅ I **hardly** know him.
