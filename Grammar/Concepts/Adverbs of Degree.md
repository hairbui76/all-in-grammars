# Adverbs of Degree

> [!abstract] In one line
> Adverbs of degree say **how much** or **to what extent**, and modify a verb, an adjective or another adverb.

## Form

```
S + V + degree adverb          (very much, a lot, a bit, a little)
degree adverb + adjective/adverb  (very, quite, rather, fairly, too)
```

Common members: `fairly, quite, rather, hardly, too, very, very much, a lot, a bit, a little, extremely, pretty`.

## Examples

Modifying a verb — end position:

- We don't like this novel **very much**.
- They talked **a lot** on the way home.
- I like it **very much**.

Modifying an adjective or another adverb — before the word:

- The exam was **pretty** difficult.
- The door **badly** needs a coat of paint.
- He was **badly** wounded.
- I'd like the steak **well** done.

## Notes & exceptions

- With `very much`, the whole phrase stays together and goes at the end: ✅ *I like it **very much*** — ❌ *I like very much it*, ❌ *I very like it*.
- `hardly` is a degree adverb with negative meaning (= almost not) → see [[Adverbs With Two Forms]].
- `pretty` as a degree adverb means "fairly / rather"; it is not related to the adjective `pretty` meaning "attractive".
- `well` and `badly` act as degree adverbs when placed **before** a verb or past participle, but as manner adverbs when placed after → see [[Adverbs of Manner]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I like very much it. → ✅ I like it **very much**.
- ❌ I very like it. → ✅ I like it **very much**.
