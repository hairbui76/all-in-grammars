# Adverbs of Frequency

> [!abstract] In one line
> Adverbs that say how often something happens, forming a scale from 100% to 0%.

## The scale

| Adverb | Rough frequency |
|---|---|
| always | 100% |
| usually | 80% |
| often | 65% |
| sometimes | 50% |
| occasionally | 30% |
| seldom / hardly ever / rarely / barely | 10% |
| never | 0% |

## When to use

- Typically with [[Present Simple]] to describe habits and routines.

## Examples

- My mother **usually** stays up late.
- We **usually** learn English on Thursday afternoons.
- Tom hardly **ever** goes to parties.
- I have **never** been to England.

## Notes & exceptions

- They stand after `be` but before an ordinary main verb: *He **is always** late* / *He **always** comes late*.
- `never, seldom, rarely, hardly ever, barely` already carry a negative meaning, so the clause counts as negative — a following question tag is positive (see [[Question Tags With Negative Adverbs]]) and fronting them causes inversion (see [[Inversion with Negative Adverbs]]).
- Longer time phrases of frequency (*every day, once a week, twice a month*) go at the beginning or the end of the clause, not in mid-position.
