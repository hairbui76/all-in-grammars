# Adverbs of Place

> [!abstract] In one line
> Adverbs of place say **where** the action happens and normally stand after the verb, or after the verb's object.

## Form

```
S + V + place adverb
S + V + object + place adverb
```

Common members: `away, abroad, everywhere, nowhere, somewhere, anywhere, here, there, upstairs, near, up, down, out`.

## Examples

After the verb when there is no object:

- She went **away**.
- He lives **abroad**.
- Bill is **upstairs**.
- The accident happened **there**.

After the object when there is one:

- She sent him **away**.
- I can't find my key **anywhere**.

## Notes & exceptions

- `somewhere` and `anywhere` follow the ordinary rules for `some` and `any`:
  - I've seen that man **somewhere**.
  - Are you going **anywhere** tonight?
- `here` and `there` can be followed by `be`, `come` or `go` + a noun subject:
  - **Here is** Tom. / **There's** Ann.
  - **Here comes** the train. / **There goes** our bus.
  - But if the subject is a personal pronoun it comes first, as usual: **There he is.** / **Here it comes.**
- `away, down, in, off, out, over, round, up` can be followed by a verb of movement + a noun subject:
  - **Away went** the runners.
  - **Down fell** a dozen apples.
  - **Out sprang** the cuckoo.
  - **Round and round flew** the plane.
  - With a pronoun subject there is no inversion: *Away **they went**. / Round and round **it flew**.*
- Full adverbial phrases of place (preposition + noun) can also be fronted with an inverted verb of position (`crouch, hang, lie, sit, stand`), movement, or `be, born, die, live` → see [[Inversion after Adverbials of Place]]:
  - From the rafters **hung strings of onions**.
  - In the doorway **stood a man with a gun**.
  - On a perch beside him **sat a blue parrot**.
- The same idea can be expressed with a participle plus `be`:
  - **Hanging** from the rafters **were** strings of onions.
  - **Standing** in the doorway **was** a man with a gun.
  - **Sitting** on a perch beside him **was** a blue parrot.
