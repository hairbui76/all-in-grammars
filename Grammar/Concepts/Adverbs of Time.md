# Adverbs of Time

> [!abstract] In one line
> Adverbs of time say **when** something happens and go at the beginning or, more usually, at the end of the clause.

## Form

```
Time adverb + S + V + ...
S + V + ... + time adverb
```

Common members: `afterwards, eventually, lately, now, recently, soon, then, today, tomorrow, yesterday`; adverb phrases such as `at once`, `since then`, `till Monday`.

## Examples

Front or end position, both acceptable:

- **Eventually** he came. / He came **eventually**.
- **Yesterday** I had my car serviced. / I had my car serviced **yesterday**.
- **Then** we went home. / We went home **then**.

Other examples:

- I'm leaving for Hanoi **tomorrow**.
- The accident happened **yesterday**.

## Notes & exceptions

- End position is the normal choice with imperatives: *Write **today**.* / *I'll wait till **tomorrow**.*
- Front position gives the time slight emphasis or sets the scene for the sentence.
- When a time adverb and a place adverb both appear at the end, place comes first:
  - He works in the garage **on Tuesdays**. [not: ~~on Tuesdays in the garage~~]
  - She sang very well at the concert **last night**. [not: ~~last night at the concert~~]

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Let's go early to bed. → ✅ Let's go to bed **early**.
- ❌ Can you do now your work? → ✅ Can you do your work **now**?
