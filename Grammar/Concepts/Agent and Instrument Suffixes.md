# Agent and Instrument Suffixes

> [!abstract] In one line
> One set of endings turns a verb or noun into a word for the **person** who does it, and another turns it into the **thing** that does it.

## Nouns for people

`verb / noun + -er, -or, -ess, -ist, -ant, -ee, -ar, -ian` → person

| Root | Person | Root | Person |
|---|---|---|---|
| teach | teacher | act | actor |
| wait | waitress | science | scientist |
| account | accountant | train | trainee |
| beg | beggar | library | librarian |

- We're very glad to have a new **teacher** of English.
- The film was played by a famous **actor**.
- **Scientists** have devoted their life to the world people.
- She'd like to become an **accountant** when she leaves school.
- Daisy worked as a **waitress** in her summer holiday.

## Less common person endings: -ent, -ive, -aire, -al

| Root | Person |
|---|---|
| study | student |
| detect | detective |
| million | millionaire |
| crime | criminal |

- Mary is a very good **student**.
- The police have tried to look for the **criminal**.

## Nouns for things and machines

`verb + -er / -or` → the instrument that performs the action

| Root | Instrument | Root | Instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| cook | cooker | calculate | calculator |
| wipe | wiper | record | recorder |

- My father bought a new **cooker** yesterday.
- Tom always brings his **calculator** to school.

## Notes & exceptions

- The same ending covers both people and machines, so context decides: a `cooker` is an appliance while a `cook` is a person; a `recorder` can be a machine or a person keeping records.
- `-ee` marks the person who **receives** the action, `-er / -or` the one who **performs** it: employer vs employee, interviewer vs interviewee, trainer vs trainee.
- `-er`, `-or`, `-ist` and `-ant` are stress-neutral, but `-ee`, `-eer` and `-ian` affect stress → see [[Suffixes That Take Stress]].
