# Agreeing and Disagreeing

> [!abstract] In one line
> Signalling that you share, or do not share, the opinion just expressed — including the short-answer echoes `So do I` and `Neither do I`.

## Useful patterns

### Agreeing

| Strength | Expression |
|---|---|
| Strong | `You can say that again.` · `Exactly.` · `Absolutely.` |
| Neutral | `I agree.` · `I think so, too.` · `I'm with you there.` |
| Echoing a positive statement | `So do I.` / `So am I.` / `So have I.` |
| Echoing a negative statement | `Neither do I.` / `Nor do I.` |
| Confirming a past comment | `It certainly was.` · `Yes, it really was.` |
| Reluctant | `I'm afraid so.` · `I suppose so.` · `Yes, maybe a little.` |

### Disagreeing

| Strength | Expression |
|---|---|
| Softened | `Do you? I think it's boring.` · `Well, I'm not so sure.` |
| Neutral | `I don't think so.` · `No, not really.` |
| Direct | `I can't agree with you.` · `I'm against it.` |

## When to use

- To agree with a **negative** statement, use `Neither do I`, not `So do I`. The auxiliary must match the one in the original statement.
- `Do you?` before your own opinion is a very common softener: it acknowledges the other view before contradicting it.
- `I'm afraid so` agrees with something unwelcome; `I'm afraid not` disagrees with something hoped for.

## Examples

- "I don't think we should exercise late at night." — "**Neither do I.**"
- "I think golf is really great." — "**Do you? I think it's boring.**"
- "That was a boring book." — "**It certainly was.**"
- "I think the new resort will ruin the environment." — "**I'm afraid so.**"
- "Do you support the proposal to build a new airport?" — "**No, not really.**"

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "I don't think we should exercise late at night." — "So do I."
- ❌ "I don't think we should exercise late at night." — "I don't, neither."
- ❌ "Do you support the proposal?" — "Yes, I'm against it." (the two halves contradict)
