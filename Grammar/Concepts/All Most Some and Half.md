# All Most Some and Half

> [!abstract] In one line
> `all, most, some` and `half` stand directly before a noun for a general meaning, but need `of` before a determiner or pronoun for a specific meaning.

## Form

```
all / most / some / no + plural countable noun      (general)
all / most / some / no + uncountable noun           (general)
all / most / some / half + of + the/these/those/my/her/our + noun   (specific)
all / most / some / half + of + object pronoun
```

## Examples

### With a plural countable noun

- **All plants** need water.
- **Most people** agree with my attitude.
- You've got **some interesting jazz records**.
- We have got **no Thursdays** free this term.

### With an uncountable noun

- My brother likes **all music**.
- **Most meat** is expensive.
- Would you like **some more beer**?
- Sorry — there's **no time** to talk now.

### With `of` + determiner + noun (specific)

- **All of my friends** at school like English.
- **Most of the food** at this restaurant is very delicious.
- **Some of his books** are written in French.
- **Half of the money** is mine.

### With `of` + object pronoun

- **All of them** don't approve of my plan.
- **Some of you** have made careless mistakes in your tests.
- **Most of us** feel the same about the war.

## Notes & exceptions

- `of` may be dropped after `all` and `half` when a determiner + noun follows, but never before a pronoun:
  - `all of the clothes` = `all the clothes`
  - `all of us` ✅ / ~~all us~~ ❌
  - `half of our group` = `half our group`
  - `half of us` ✅ / ~~half us~~ ❌
- Without `of`, the meaning is general (all plants everywhere); with `of`, it is limited to a defined group.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ All of plants need water. → ✅ **All plants** need water.
- ❌ Most of people agree. → ✅ **Most people** agree. / **Most of the people** here agree.
