# Anticipatory It As Object

> [!abstract] In one line
> When a verb's real object is an infinitive or a clause, `it` fills the object slot and the real object moves to the end.

## Form

`S + V + it + adjective/noun + to + V ...`
`S + V + it + adjective/noun + that + clause`

Verbs that take this pattern: `find, think, consider, believe, feel, make, take, owe, leave, deem, judge`.

## When to use

- To avoid a heavy infinitive or `that`-clause sitting directly after the verb.
- Standard after `find`, `think`, `consider`, `make` + evaluative adjective.
- With `make it + adj + for sb + to V` to state what a situation causes.

## Examples

- I **find it difficult to concentrate** in this room.
- We **consider it essential that** every member **attend** the meeting.
- The noise **made it impossible to sleep**.
- She **thinks it a waste of time to argue** with him.
- I **owe it to my parents that** I succeeded.
- The rain **made it hard for us to see** the road.

## Notes & exceptions

- The `it` is obligatory — it cannot be dropped: ❌ *I find difficult to concentrate.*
- `take it for granted that ...` and `leave it to sb to do sth` are fixed expressions of this type.
- After `consider / think it essential / vital / necessary that`, the `that`-clause often takes the base-form subjunctive → see [[It Is Essential That Clause]] and [[Subjunctive in That Clauses]].
- The subject-position twin is `It + be + adj + to V` → see [[Infinitive As Subject]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I think important to study English. → ✅ I think **it important** to study English.
- ❌ He made impossible for me to refuse. → ✅ He made **it impossible** for me to refuse.
