# Apologising and Responding

> [!abstract] In one line
> Saying sorry, and reassuring someone who has apologised to you.

## Useful patterns

### Apologising

| Register | Expression | Following form |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral | `I'm sorry (that) ...` | clause |
| Neutral | `I'm sorry for + V-ing` | `-ing` |
| Softening bad news | `I'm afraid ...` | clause |
| Softening a refusal | `I'm sorry I can't.` · `I'm afraid not.` | — |
| Formal | `I do apologise for ...` | noun / `-ing` |

### Responding to an apology

| Register | Expression |
|---|---|
| Neutral | `That's all right.` · `Never mind.` |
| Reassuring | `Don't worry. I'm all right.` · `No problem.` |
| Formal | `Please don't apologise.` |

### Getting attention or interrupting

| Expression | Use |
|---|---|
| `Excuse me.` | before you speak to a stranger, ask a question or leave |
| `Sorry?` (rising tone) | you did not hear and want a repeat |

## When to use

- `Excuse me` comes **before** the possible trouble (stopping someone in the street); `Sorry` comes **after** it.
- `I'm afraid` is not a real apology — it is a softener attached to unwelcome information or a refusal.
- Not knowing an answer is apologised for, not simply denied: `I'm sorry, I don't know.`

## Examples

- "**Excuse me.** I'm your new neighbour. I just moved in." — "Oh, nice to meet you."
- "Could I speak to Ann?" — "**I'm sorry. Ann's not in.**"
- "Can you come over after the show?" — "**I'm sorry I can't.**"
- "Let me drive you home." — "**Don't worry. I'm all right.**"

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Using a bare `No` where `I'm afraid not` is expected.
- ❌ I'm sorry for come late. → ✅ I'm sorry for **coming** late.
