# Appositives

> [!abstract] In one line
> An appositive is a noun or noun phrase placed next to another noun to explain or identify it, and it has the same grammatical function as that noun.

## Form

`Noun, + noun phrase, + ...` (explaining — commas)
`Noun + noun phrase + ...` (identifying — no commas)

## When to use

- **With commas** when the appositive merely explains a noun that is already identified.
- **Without commas** when the appositive is needed to identify the noun.

## Examples

- Manchester, **a big city in Northwest England**, has a very good football team. (`a big city in Northwest England` explains `Manchester` and shares its role as subject of `has`)
- Everybody visits the White House, **the house of the president**. (`the house of the president` explains `the White House` and shares its role as object of `visits`)
- The great English dramatist **Shakespeare** was born in 1564. (identifying — no comma)
- His father **Mr John** will give a lecture on wildlife. (identifying — no comma)

## Contrast with

- [[Non-Defining Relative Clauses]] — the same explaining function, but with a finite clause
- [[Reduced Relative Clauses]] — reduction to a participle rather than to a noun phrase
