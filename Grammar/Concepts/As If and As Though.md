# As If and As Though

> [!abstract] In one line
> `as if` and `as though` introduce a comparison; a past form after them signals that the comparison is unreal, improbable or doubtful.

## Form

```
S1 + V(present or past) + as if/as though + S2 + V(past simple)     -> unreality now
S1 + V(present or past) + as if/as though + S2 + were + V-ing       -> imaginary action now
S1 + V(present or past) + as if/as though + S2 + had + V3           -> unreality in the past
S1 + V + as if/as though + S2 + V(normal tense)                     -> the comparison is real
```

There is no difference in meaning between `as if` and `as though`.

## When to use

- Past simple after `as if`: the situation compared is untrue, probably untrue, or unknown at the moment of speaking
- Past continuous: an imaginary action happening at the time of speaking
- Past perfect: a real or imaginary action earlier than the main verb
- Normal tense: when the speaker treats the comparison as genuinely likely

## Examples

Unreal present:

- He speaks / spoke **as if** he **were** my father.
- He behaves **as if** he **owned** the place. (but he doesn't)
- He talks **as though** he **knew** where she was.
- The old lady dresses **as if** it **were** winter even in the summer.
- He behaves **as though** he **were** the owner. (but he is not the owner)
- He orders me about **as if** I **were** his wife. (but I am not)

Imaginary action in progress:

- I feel **as if** I **were sitting** on a fire.
- I didn't see anybody but I felt **as if** I **were being watched**.

Earlier action:

- Jeff looked **as though** he **had seen** a ghost.
- He talks about Rome **as though** he **had been** there himself.
- The grass looks / looked **as if** it **hadn't been cut** for a long time.
- She talked about the contest **as if** she **had won** the grand prize.
- He looks / looked **as though** he **hadn't had** a decent meal for a month.
- She talked about the accident **as if** she **had seen** it with her own eyes.

Real comparison, ordinary tense:

- It looks **as if** it **is going** to rain.
- It sounds **as if** they **are talking** about something secret.
- It looks **as if** the light **has been burning** all night.

## Notes & exceptions

- Putting the main verb into the past does **not** change the tense after `as if`: *He acts / acted as though he **were** speaking in public.*
- `as if` clauses are not backshifted in reported speech: *"He treats me as if I were a slave," she said → She said he treated her as if she **were** a slave.*
