# Asking For and Giving Directions

> [!abstract] In one line
> Stopping a stranger to find a place, and giving the route back in imperatives and distance phrases.

## Useful patterns

### Asking

| Expression | Note |
|---|---|
| `Excuse me. How can I get to ...?` | always open with `Excuse me` |
| `Could you tell me the way to ...?` | indirect, very polite |
| `Where's the nearest ...?` | for a type of place |
| `Is it far from here?` | checking distance |
| `What's the best place to ...?` | asking for a recommendation |

### Giving

| Expression | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Go straight on / Go two blocks and then you are there.` | route |
| `Turn left / right at the traffic lights.` | route |
| `Take the second turning on the left.` | route |
| `It's at the corner of the street.` | position |
| `It's two blocks away.` | distance |
| `You can see it from the back.` | position |
| `I'm sorry, I don't know. I'm a stranger here myself.` | cannot help |

## When to use

- Directions are given in the **imperative**: `Go ...`, `Turn ...`, `Take ...` — no subject.
- After receiving directions, close the exchange with `OK, thanks.` or `Thanks a lot.`
- If you cannot help, apologise rather than simply saying no.

## Examples

- "Excuse me. **How can I get there?**" — "**Go two blocks and then you are there.**" — "OK, thanks."
- "What's the best place to eat lunch?" — "**There's a great restaurant at the corner of the street.**"
- "Where's the view? The advertisement said this place has a great view of the sea." — "**You can see it from the back.**"
- "What does it cost to get to Manchester?" — "**It depends on how you go.**"

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "Go two blocks and then you are there." — "No, thanks." (the speaker has just been helped, so thank them)
- ❌ Answering a `How can I get to ...?` question with an opening time instead of a route.
