# Backshift of Tenses

> [!abstract] In one line
> When the reporting verb is in the past, the verb of the quoted words moves one step further back in time.

## Form

| Direct speech | Indirect speech |
|---|---|
| Simple present | Simple past |
| Present continuous | Past continuous |
| Present perfect | Past perfect |
| Present perfect continuous | Past perfect continuous |
| Simple past | Past perfect (or unchanged simple past) |
| Past continuous | Past perfect continuous (or unchanged past continuous) |
| `be going to` | `was/were going to` (future in the past) |
| Simple future (`will`) | Conditional (`would`) |
| Future continuous (`will be + V-ing`) | Conditional continuous (`would be + V-ing`) |
| Future perfect (`will have + V3`) | Conditional perfect (`would have + V3`) |

## When to use

- Only when the reporting verb is in a past tense (`said`, `told`, `explained`, `asked`)
- Not when the reporting verb is present or future, and not for permanent truths

## Examples

- "I never eat dog meat," he explained → He explained that he **never ate** dog meat.
- "I'm waiting for Mary now," he said → He said he **was waiting** for Mary **then**.
- "I have never ridden a camel," he said → He said he **had never ridden** a camel.
- "I have been playing the guitar for ages," she said → She said she **had been playing** the guitar for ages.
- "I didn't go anywhere last night," George said → George said he **hadn't gone** anywhere the previous night.
- "I was cooking dinner when the light went out," Mrs Pitt said → Mrs Pitt said she **had been cooking** dinner when the light **had gone** out.
- "We are going to sell our house next month," they said → They said they **were going to sell** their house the following month.
- "I will be in Paris on Monday," she said → She said she **would be** in Paris on Monday.
- "I will be using the car myself on the 25th," she said → She said she **would be using** the car herself on the 25th.
- "I will have saved enough money by the end of this year," she said → She said she **would have saved** enough money by the end of that year.

## Notes & exceptions

- Simple past and past continuous may stay unchanged when no confusion of time can arise.
- Past perfect and past perfect continuous cannot go back any further, so they stay as they are.
- Facts that are still true at the moment of reporting need not be backshifted.
