# Bare Infinitive

> [!abstract] In one line
> The bare infinitive is the infinitive without `to`; it is used after all the central modal verbs and after a few special verbs.

## Form

`modal + V(bare)`

## Where the bare infinitive is used

| After | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `will / shall` | future simple | There **will be** a change in the weather in the next few days. |
| `must` | obligation, certainty | It's too late. I **must go** now. |
| `can / could` | ability | I **can speak** English. |
| `should / had better` | advice | I think you **should work** harder. |
| `may as well / might as well` | no reason not to | The last bus has left, we **may as well walk** home. |
| `needn't` | no necessity now | She **needn't worry** about money, need she? |
| `used to / would` | past habit | I **used to live** in Yen Bai. |
| `would rather / would sooner` | preference | I'd **rather go** out for a change now. |
| `make / let / have + object` | causative | The boss **made** the woman **work** very hard during the day. |
| verbs of perception + object | seeing/hearing the whole action | We watched the little girl **walk** across the street. |

## Examples

- China **will host** the 16th Asian Games.
- I have a lot of work to do tonight. I think I **must stay** up late.
- What could you **do** when you were six months old?
- Smoking is harmful to your health. You'd better **give** it up.
- You'd better **not ask** your parents for money.
- We **may as well delay** the game because it's raining heavily.
- Let me **hear** from you soon.

## Notes & exceptions

- `had better` is negated as `had better not + V`, never *hadn't better*.
- `help` allows both: *He helps me (to) learn English.*
- With `be going to`, `be able to`, `have to`, `ought to` and `used to` the `to` is part of the expression and is kept.
