# Be Going To

> [!abstract] In one line
> `am/is/are going to + V` — a plan already decided, or a prediction based on present evidence.

## Form

`(+) S + am/is/are + going to + V(bare)`
`(-) S + am/is/are + not + going to + V(bare)`
`(?) Am/Is/Are + S + going to + V(bare) ?`

Past form: `was/were going to + V` = an intention that was not carried out.

## When to use

- An **intention or plan decided before the moment of speaking**.
- A **prediction based on visible present evidence**.
- With `was/were going to` for unfulfilled past intentions.

## Examples

- We **are going to buy** a house next year. (already decided)
- Look at those clouds — it **is going to rain**.
- She **is not going to accept** the offer.
- I **was going to call** you, but I fell asleep.
- What **are** you **going to do** after graduation?

## Notes & exceptions

- With `go` and `come`, the plain [[Present Continuous]] is preferred: *I'm going to the cinema* rather than *I'm going to go to the cinema*.
- In informal speech `going to` is reduced to `gonna` — never write this in an exam.
- Future time markers (`tomorrow`, `next week`, `soon`) work with both `be going to` and [[Future Simple]]; the difference is the **speaker's attitude**, not the time word.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I am going to buy a car tomorrow — I have just decided now. → ✅ I **will buy** a car. (spontaneous decision)
- ❌ He is going buy a car. → ✅ He **is going to buy** a car.
- ❌ Are you going to be a doctor when you will grow up? → ✅ ... when you **grow up**.

## Contrast with

- [[Future Simple]] — spontaneous decisions, promises, opinion-based predictions
- [[Present Continuous]] — a fixed arrangement with a time and place already set
- [[Present Simple]] — timetables and fixed schedules
- [[Future Continuous]] — an action in progress at a future moment
