# Because and Because Of

> [!abstract] In one line
> `because` is a conjunction and needs a full clause after it; `because of` is a preposition and needs a noun phrase.

## Form

```
because + S + V
because of + noun / noun phrase / gerund
```

## Examples

| Conjunction (`because` + clause) | Preposition (`because of` + noun) |
| --- | --- |
| I came to school late **because** it rained heavily. | I came to school late **because of** the heavy rain. |
| He couldn't go to work **because** he was ill. | He couldn't go to work **because of** his illness. |

## Notes & exceptions

- Converting between the two normally means turning a verb into a noun: *it rained heavily* → *the heavy rain*; *he was ill* → *his illness*.
- The same conjunction/preposition split runs through the whole system of adverbial linking:

| Meaning | Conjunction (+ clause) | Preposition (+ noun) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| reason | because, as, since | because of, due to, owing to |
| concession | although, though, even though | despite, in spite of |

## Common mistakes

- ❌ because of it rained heavily → ✅ **because** it rained heavily / **because of** the heavy rain
- ❌ because his illness → ✅ **because of** his illness
