# Both Either and Neither

> [!abstract] In one line
> These three determiners are used only for two people or two things: `both` = the two, `either` = one or the other, `neither` = not one and not the other.

## Form

```
both + plural noun
either / neither + singular noun + singular verb
both of / either of / neither of + object pronoun / the / these / those / possessive + plural noun
```

## Examples

- I asked two people the way to the station but **neither of them** knew.
- **Both his parents** are from Europe.
- Come on Tuesday or Wednesday. **Either day** is OK.
- **Both of us** were tired.
- **Neither of the books is/are** interesting.
- **Both (of) her parents** are from London.
- **Both of us** like playing badminton.

## Notes & exceptions

- `of` is optional before a determiner + noun after `both`: *Both (of) her parents ...*
- `of` is obligatory before a pronoun: ❌ ~~Both us~~ → ✅ **Both of us**.
- After `neither of`, the verb may be singular (formal) or plural (informal): *Neither of the books **is/are** interesting.*
- `both` takes a plural verb; `either` and `neither` with a singular noun take a singular verb.
- For three or more items use `all`, `any` and `none` instead.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Both us were tired. → ✅ **Both of us** were tired.
- ❌ Neither of the two books are interesting to me. (informal only) → ✅ **Neither of the two books is** interesting to me.
- ❌ Neither of my three brothers came. → ✅ **None of** my three brothers came.
