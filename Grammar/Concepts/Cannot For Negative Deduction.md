# Cannot For Negative Deduction

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Can't` and `couldn't` express the speaker's certainty that something is **not** true — they are the negative counterpart of deductive `must`.

## Form

| Time reference | Structure | Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| now, in progress | `S + can't be + V-ing` | `I'm sure + negative present continuous clause` |
| past, completed | `S + can't / couldn't have + PII` | `I'm sure + negative past simple clause` |
| past, in progress | `S + can't / couldn't have been + V-ing` | `I'm sure + negative past continuous clause` |

## Examples — present

- It's Sunday. They **can't be working** in the office. = I'm sure they are not working in the office.
- He looks serious. He **can't be joking**. = I'm sure he is not joking.
- The children **can't be studying** at school now.
- She **can't be earning** a lot of money.
- She **can't be having** a good time as the weather is rather bad.

## Examples — past

- It **couldn't have been** Huong whom you saw in the supermarket this morning because she has gone to Canada and won't come back until next month.
- The ground is dry. It **can't have rained** last night.
- I **can't have mistaken** her for anyone else.
- They lost five successive matches. They **can't have played** well.
- Peter didn't say hello to me when I saw him in the street. He **couldn't have seen** me. = I'm sure Peter didn't see me.
- He **can't have stolen** the money as he wasn't there at that time.

## Examples — past continuous

- Jack walked straight into the wall. He **can't have been looking** where he was going.
- The children **couldn't have been studying** at school at 10 p.m. yesterday. = I'm sure the children weren't studying.

## Notes & exceptions

- Do not use `mustn't` for negative deduction; `mustn't` means prohibition.
- `Couldn't` is used in **all** situations in the negative, whereas positive `could` is limited to general past ability → see [[Ability With Can And Could]].

- [[Must For Deduction]] — certainty that something is true
- [[May And Might For Possibility]] — uncertainty rather than certainty
