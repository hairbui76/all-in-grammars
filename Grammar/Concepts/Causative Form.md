# Causative Form

> [!abstract] In one line
> `have/get + object + past participle` says that you arranged for somebody else to do the job, or that something (usually unpleasant) happened to something of yours.

## Form

- Passive causative: `S + have/get + something + P2 + (by sb)`
- Active causative: `S + have + sb + do smth` / `S + get + sb + to do smth`

`have` and `get` change for tense and aspect; the past participle never changes.

## When to use

**1. To say that you arranged for another person to do the work rather than doing it yourself.**

**2. To say that something unpleasant happened to something you own** (the possessor becomes the subject instead of the possessed thing).

## Examples

Arranged action:

- Jill repaired the roof. (= she did it herself) / Jill **had the roof repaired**. (= she arranged for somebody else to do it)
- I have cut my hair. (= myself) / I **have had my hair cut**. (= somebody cut it for me)
- I hate taking photographs. / I hate **having my photograph taken**.
- Did Ann make the dress herself, or did she **have it made**?
- How often do you **have your hair done**?
- I've lost my key. I'll have to **have another key made**.
- Why did you take your car to the garage? To **have it serviced**.
- I'm **having a house built** at the moment.
- I'm going to **have a telephone installed** in my house next month.
- They are **having a pond dug** in the garden.
- You should **have/get your eyes tested** (by an oculist). = You should have an oculist **test** your eyes. / You should get an oculist **to test** your eyes.

Unpleasant event:

- George's nose was broken in a fight. → George **had his nose broken** in a fight.
- Jack's passport was stolen a few weeks ago. → Jack **had his passport stolen** a few weeks ago.
- The hurricane blew off the roof of the house. → The house **had its roof blown off** by the hurricane.
- They **have had their house broken into** twice this month.
- Mr Brown **had his hat blown off** in the wind.

## Notes & exceptions

- Note the word order: the object comes **before** the past participle — `have the roof repaired`, never `have repaired the roof`.
- With `have sb do` the following verb is a bare infinitive; with `get sb to do` it takes `to`.
