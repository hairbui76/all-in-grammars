# Clauses of Concession

> [!abstract] In one line
> A concessive clause introduced by `although`, `though` or `even though` states a fact that would normally prevent the main clause, but does not.

## Form

```
Although / Though / Even though + S + V, + Main clause
Main clause + although / though / even though + S + V
```

## Examples

- **Although** the weather was bad, they went camping. (= The weather was bad **but** they went camping.)
- **Even though** they are poor, they live in harmony. (= They are poor **but** they live in harmony.)
- He can't answer my questions **though** he is intelligent.
- **Although** my salary is low, I love my job teaching.

## Notes & exceptions

- `even though` is stronger and more emphatic than `although`; `though` is the most informal.
- In spoken English, `though` is often placed at the end of a sentence as an adverb meaning "however":
  - The house isn't very nice. I like the garden **though**.
  - I see him every day. I've never spoken to him **though**.
- These conjunctions introduce a clause (S + V). Before a noun or gerund, use `despite` / `in spite of` instead → see [[Despite and In Spite Of]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Although the weather was bad, but they went camping. → ✅ **Although** the weather was bad, they went camping.
- ❌ Although the bad weather, they went camping. → ✅ **Despite** the bad weather, they went camping.

## Contrast with

- [[Despite and In Spite Of]] — prepositions, followed by noun or gerund
- [[Concessive Inversion With As and Though]] — `Rich as he is, ...`
- [[However and No Matter]] — concession with a wh-word
- [[Even If and Whether Or Not]] — concession about a hypothetical or open case
