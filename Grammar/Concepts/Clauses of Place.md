# Clauses of Place

> [!abstract] In one line
> A clause of place says **where** the action of the main clause happens and is introduced by `where` or `wherever`.

## Form

```
Main clause + where / wherever + S + V
```

- `where` = at / to the particular place that
- `wherever` = at / to any place that, no matter where

## Examples

- I looked **where** he pointed.
- He told me **where** he had seen the money.
- Sit **wherever** you want.
- I will go **wherever** you go.
- In Bali, you will see these ceremonies **wherever** you go.

## Notes & exceptions

- No preposition is needed before `where`: ❌ *I looked at where he pointed* is unnecessary in this pattern.
- The verb in a `where` / `wherever` clause is never in the future tense when the main clause refers to the future: *I will go **wherever you go***, not ~~wherever you will go~~.
- `where` also functions as a **relative adverb** attached to a noun of place (*the town **where** I was born*); that is a relative clause, not an adverb clause → see [[Relative Adverbs]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I will go wherever you will go. → ✅ I will go **wherever you go**.
- ❌ Sit where do you want. → ✅ Sit **wherever you want**.

## Contrast with

- [[Relative Adverbs]] — `where` modifying a noun instead of the whole clause
- [[Adverbs of Place]] — a single adverb rather than a clause
- [[Clauses of Time]] — when, not where
- [[However and No Matter]] — the `no matter` family that `wherever` belongs to
