# Clauses of Reason

> [!abstract] In one line
> A clause of reason explains **why** the main clause happens, and is introduced by `because`, `as`, `since`, `seeing that` or `now that`.

## Form

```
Main clause + because + S + V
As / Since / Seeing that / Now that + S + V, + Main clause
```

## Examples

- Tom took the train to Auckland **because** he hated flying.
- **As** it was getting late, we decided to come home.
- **Since** he was late, I decided to go without him.
- **Seeing that** I have no money, I can't buy the book.
- **Now that** you're grown up, you must stop this childish behaviour.
- He couldn't go to work **because** he was ill.

## Notes & exceptions

- **Position and emphasis.** A `because` clause usually comes **after** the main clause, which puts the emphasis on the reason. Clauses with `as`, `since`, `seeing that` and `now that` usually come **first**, which puts the emphasis on the main clause.
- `because` is a conjunction and takes a clause; `because of` is a preposition and takes a noun phrase → see [[Because and Because Of]].
- A reason clause states the cause; a result clause states the consequence. The same facts can be expressed either way:
  - I felt hungry, **so** I ate two cakes. (`so` + result)
  - I ate two cakes **because** I felt hungry. (`because` + reason)

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Because he was ill, so he couldn't go to work. → ✅ **Because** he was ill, he couldn't go to work. / He couldn't go to work **because** he was ill.
- ❌ He couldn't go to work because of he was ill. → ✅ ... **because** he was ill / **because of** his illness.
