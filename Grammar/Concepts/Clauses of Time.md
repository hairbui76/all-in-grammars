# Clauses of Time

> [!abstract] In one line
> A time clause says **when** the action of the main clause happens, and it never uses a future tense.

## Form

```
Time conjunction + S + V, + Main clause
Main clause + time conjunction + S + V
```

Time conjunctions: `when`, `as` (when/while), `before`, `after`, `since`, `until / till`, `as soon as`, `while`, `once`, `whenever`, `as long as`, `just as`.

## Examples

- **When** I lived in Darwin, I experienced hot weather.
- **After** he had done his homework, he watched TV.
- I will lend you some money **whenever** you need.
- He phoned me **as soon as** he came back from his holiday.
- **Once** I lived in Darwin, I knew what a real cyclone was.
- I'll wait here **till** the bus arrives.

## Notes & exceptions

- **Tense agreement.** The verb in the time clause must fit the main verb and the meaning of the conjunction:
  - **Until** I lived in Vietnam, I **had never known** hot weather. (`until` points to a state existing before another past event, so the past perfect is used)
  - **While** I **was walking** down the street I **saw** Joe driving a Toyota. (`while` = during, so a continuous tense is natural)
- **No future tense in a time clause.** Use the present simple for future reference, and the present perfect for a completed future action:
  - I'll remember that day **as long as I live**. [not: ~~as long as I'll live~~]
  - I'll tell you about that **when I come**. [not: ~~when I'll come~~]
  - I'd like to go for a walk once the rain **has stopped**.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I'll telephone you after I will arrive. → ✅ I'll telephone you after I **arrive / have arrived**.
- ❌ As long as I'll live → ✅ As long as I **live**
