# Cleft Sentences

> [!abstract] In one line
> A sentence is split in two so that one element is highlighted between `It is / was` and `that`.

## Form

```
It is / was + emphatic part + that + rest of the clause
```

Any element except the verb can be put in the emphatic slot: subject, object, time, place, manner.

## Examples

Base sentence: *Shakespeare wrote Hamlet.*

- **It was Shakespeare that wrote** Hamlet.

Base sentence: *England won the world cup in 1966.*

- **It was England that** won the world cup in 1966. (emphasises the subject)
- **It was in 1966 that** England won the world cup. (emphasises the time)
- **It was the world cup that** England won in 1966. (emphasises the object)

Further examples:

- **It is** the flexibility of function of individual words, the assimilation of borrowings and the spontaneous creation of new words **that have made** English an effective medium of international communication.
- **It is** new English words **that cause** me a lot of difficulty.
- **It was** his return **that made** her happy.
- **It was** his bad exam result **that made** his parents sad.

## Notes & exceptions

- `who` may replace `that` when the highlighted element is a person.
- The tense of `be` follows the tense of the original sentence: present → `It is`, past → `It was`.
- `It is/was` stays singular however plural the emphasised element is, but the verb inside the `that`-clause agrees with that element: `It is new English words that **cause** ...`
- `It was not until ... that ...` is a very common cleft used to avoid inversion — see [[Inversion with Not Until]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ It was in 1966 when England won the world cup that ... → ✅ **It was in 1966 that** England won the world cup.
- ❌ It was England won the world cup. → ✅ It was England **that** won the world cup.
