# Compound Nouns With Gerund

> [!abstract] In one line
> A gerund combines with a noun in either order to build compound nouns, and the order changes the relationship between the two parts.

## Form

```
Gerund + Noun = Compound Noun   (the noun is used FOR that activity)
Noun + Gerund = Compound Noun   (the noun is the OBJECT of the activity)
```

## Examples

### Gerund + Noun

a swimming pool · writing paper · wrapping paper · cooking oil · cooking apple · building materials · fishing rod · looking glass · teaching plan · teaching method · fighting cock · reading lamp · washing machine · sewing machine · ploughing machine · walking stick · diving board

### Noun + Gerund

weight-lifting · surf-riding · coal-mining · bird-watching · lorry-driving · fruit picking · wheat producing · fund-raising

## Notes & exceptions

- In `Gerund + Noun` compounds the stress normally falls on the gerund: a **SWIM**ming pool.
- `Noun + Gerund` compounds are often written with a hyphen (`weight-lifting`), though usage varies.
- These compounds behave as ordinary nouns: they can be pluralised and take articles.

## Contrast with

- [[Participles]] — `a swimming man` is a participle describing the man, not a compound noun
