# Concessive Inversion With As and Though

> [!abstract] In one line
> Moving an adjective, adverb or verb to the front of the clause before `as` or `though` gives an emphatic concessive meaning.

## Form

```
Adj / Adv + as / though + S + V, + Main clause
Verb + as + S + will / would / may / might, + Main clause
```

## Examples

### Adjective or adverb fronted

- **Rich as he is**, he never helps anybody.
- **Carefully as he drives**, he often meets with accidents.
- **Much as I admire** his achievements, I think he is a fool.
- **Tired as I am**, I am trying to work.

### Verb fronted with a modal

- **Work as he will**, he can't support/feed his family.
- **Work as he would**, he couldn't make both ends meet.
- **Study as she would**, she didn't pass the exam.

## Notes & exceptions

- The pattern is emphatic and rather literary; the plain equivalent is `Although he is rich, ...`.
- `as` and `though` are interchangeable in the adjective pattern: *Rich as/though he is, ...*.
- The verb pattern requires `will / would / may / might` and expresses persistent but unsuccessful effort.
- No article is used with a fronted noun-like adjective phrase in this pattern.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Although rich as he is, he never helps anybody. → ✅ **Rich as he is**, he never helps anybody.
