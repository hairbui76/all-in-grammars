# Connective Relative Clauses

> [!abstract] In one line
> A connective relative clause continues the sentence after the object of a verb, typically with `quantifier + of whom/of which`.

## Form

`..., quantifier + of whom / of which + clause`

Quantifiers used: `all of` · `most of` · `none of` · `neither of` · `any of` · `either of` ·
`some of` · `many of` · `much of` · `(a) few of` · `both of` · `half of` · `each of` ·
`one of` · `two of` ...

Use `of whom` for persons and `of which` for things. The pronoun is never `that`, and commas are obligatory.

## When to use

- To join a second sentence that says something about a part or all of a previously mentioned group.
- Instead of repeating `them`/`some of them` in a separate sentence.

## Examples

- I have a car **which was made in Japan**.
- They have two cars. They rarely use one of them. → They have two cars, **one of which they rarely use**.
- Mr Brown has two daughters. Both of them are doctors. → Mr Brown has two daughters, **both of whom are doctors**.
- Mr Brown has two daughters. They are doctors. → Mr Brown has two daughters **who are doctors**.
- Mary has three brothers. All of them are married. → Mary has three brothers, **all of whom are married**.
- They asked me a lot of questions. I couldn't answer most of them. → They asked me a lot of questions, **most of which I couldn't answer**.
- Tom tried on three jackets, **none of which fitted him**.
- Two men, **neither of whom I had ever seen before**, came into my office.
- They have got three cars, **two of which they rarely use**.
- She has got a lot of friends, **many of whom she was at school with**.

## Notes & exceptions

- The quantifier keeps `of`: ❌ `all whom`, ✅ `all of whom`.
- Compare `both of whom are doctors` (all information known) with the plain defining clause `who are doctors`, which restricts the set.
