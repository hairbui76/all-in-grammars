# Connectors of Alternative

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Alternatively`, `otherwise` and `instead` all offer a second option, but each answers a different question: another choice, the bad consequence of not choosing, or the replacement actually chosen.

## Alternatively — "or else you could ..."

Introduces another possible choice.

- If the goods are faulty, we can replace them. **Alternatively**, we can give you a refund.
- Let's take the train. **Alternatively**, we could go by car.

## Otherwise — "if not, then ..."

States what will happen if the first sentence is not carried out. The comma after `otherwise` is usually omitted.

- You have to press the button. **Otherwise** the machine won't work.
- We must run, **otherwise** we'll be too late.
- Tom will have to work harder. **Otherwise** he'll fail.

## Instead — "in place of that"

Names the thing that replaces the rejected option. It can stand at the beginning or at the end of the clause.

- She didn't go to Greece. **Instead**, she went to America.
- We have no coffee. Would you like tea **instead**?
- Typewriters are no longer used. **Instead**, most writing is done on a word processor.

## Notes & exceptions

- `otherwise` implies a condition and can usually be rewritten with `if ... not` → see [[Implied Conditionals]].
- `instead of` + noun / V-ing is a preposition, not a connector: *She went to America **instead of** Greece.*

## Common mistakes

- ❌ We must run. Instead we'll be too late. → ✅ We must run, **otherwise** we'll be too late.
