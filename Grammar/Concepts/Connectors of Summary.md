# Connectors of Summary

> [!abstract] In one line
> `All in all` and `in short` close a passage by compressing everything just said into one final statement.

## The group

| Connector | Note |
|---|---|
| `all in all` | weighs everything up and gives an overall verdict |
| `in short` | states the same content far more briefly |
| `in brief` / `to sum up` | same function, more formal |

## Examples

- He was a kind husband, generous to friends, and considerate to other people. **All in all**, he was a good man.
- He was a kind husband, generous to friends, and considerate to other people. **In short**, he was a good man.

## Notes & exceptions

- A summary connector must follow at least two ideas — it cannot summarise a single sentence.
- The summarising clause normally repeats no new information; if it does, use [[Connectors of Addition]] instead.
- Punctuation follows the general rule → see [[Sentence Connector Punctuation]].

## Contrast with

- [[Connectors of Explanation and Example]] — restates or illustrates one point rather than closing a passage
- [[Connectors of Result]] — states a consequence, not a summary
