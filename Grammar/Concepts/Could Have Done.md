# Could Have Done

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Could have + past participle` has two meanings: "perhaps it happened" and "it was possible but it did not happen".

## Meaning a — past possibility (= may / might have + PII)

`S + could have + PII` = `Perhaps + past simple clause`

- They **might / could have taken** this road. = Perhaps they took this road.

## Meaning b — an opportunity that was not used

`S + could have + PII` — the ability or chance existed, but nothing was done.

- Why didn't you tell me that you were short of money? I **could have lent** you some.
- You needn't have cooked this food. We **could have eaten** it now.
- I had free time but I went to bed early. I **could have done** all my homework.

## Notes & exceptions

- `would / could / might have + PII` is the main clause of a [[Third Conditional]]:
  - Perhaps I didn't get a better job because I didn't study hard enough. → I **might have got** a better job if I had studied hard enough.
- Meaning b often carries an implied reproach or regret, close to `should have + PII` but without stating that the action was a duty.

## Contrast with

- [[May And Might For Possibility]] — only meaning a
- [[Should Have Done]] — states a past obligation, not just an opportunity
- [[Ability With Can And Could]] — `could` for ability that was actually used
