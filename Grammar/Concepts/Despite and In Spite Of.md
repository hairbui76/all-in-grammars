# Despite and In Spite Of

> [!abstract] In one line
> `despite`, `in spite of` and `notwithstanding` are prepositions of concession, so a noun, a gerund or `the fact that + clause` must follow — never a bare clause.

## Form

```
Despite / In spite of / Notwithstanding + noun, + Main clause
Despite / In spite of / Notwithstanding + V-ing, + Main clause
Despite / In spite of / Notwithstanding + the fact that + S + V, + Main clause
```

## Examples

- **In spite of / Despite** the bad weather, they went camping.
- Although the traffic was bad, I arrived on time. ⇔ **In spite of** the (bad) traffic, I arrived on time.
- I couldn't sleep although I was very tired. ⇔ I couldn't sleep **despite being** very tired.
- Although the play received good notices, not many people went to see it. ⇔ **Despite** good notices the play received, not many people went to see it.
- Although Christopher was stronger of the two, his attacker soon overpowered him. ⇔ **Despite** his (superior) strength, Christopher was soon overpowered by his attacker.
- Although he was not guilty, they executed him. ⇔ **In spite of** his innocence, they executed him. / **In spite of** his **not being** guilty, he was executed by them.

## Notes & exceptions

- When rewriting `although + S + be + adjective` as a preposition phrase, turn the adjective into a noun (`guilty → his innocence`, `strong → his strength`) or use `being + adjective` (`despite being very tired`).
- `for all` is another concessive preposition with the same meaning:
  - **For all** his wealth, he never helps anybody. (= Although he is wealthy, he never helps anybody.)
  - **For all** you say, I still believe him.
- `notwithstanding` is formal and may also follow its noun: *his objections notwithstanding*.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Despite of the rain, we went out. → ✅ **Despite** the rain / **In spite of** the rain, we went out.
- ❌ Despite it was raining, we went out. → ✅ **Despite the fact that** it was raining, we went out.
- ❌ In spite the noise, the children slept well. → ✅ **In spite of** the noise, the children slept well.
