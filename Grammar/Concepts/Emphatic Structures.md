# Emphatic Structures

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Do / does / did` can be added before the bare infinitive to insist that an action really is or was performed.

## Form

```
S + do/does/did + V(bare)
Do + V(bare) ...          (emphatic imperative)
```

The auxiliary carries the tense, so the main verb returns to its bare form.

## When to use

- To contradict a doubt or an accusation
- To make an invitation or request warmer and more insistent
- To stress that something really happened

## Examples

- Come in, please. → **Do come in**, please.
- I went out last night. → I **did go** out last night.
- He taught English at school this morning. → He **did teach** English at school this morning.

## Notes & exceptions

- Only affirmative sentences take emphatic `do`; negatives and questions already use the auxiliary.
- The tense marker moves to `do`: `He did **teach**`, never *He did taught*.
- In speech, `do / does / did` is stressed.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I did went out last night. → ✅ I **did go** out last night.
- ❌ He does teaches English. → ✅ He **does teach** English.

## Contrast with

- [[Cleft Sentences]] — emphasis by fronting a phrase with `It is / was ... that ...`
- [[Inversion]] — emphasis by changing word order
