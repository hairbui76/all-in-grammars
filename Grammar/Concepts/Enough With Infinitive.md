# Enough With Infinitive

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Enough` follows an adjective or adverb and, with a following infinitive, means "sufficiently ... to be able to".

## Form

`S + be / V + adj / adv + enough + (for sb) + to V`

`enough + noun + to V` (when `enough` modifies a noun it comes **before** it)

## The infinitive refers to the subject

- She isn't **old enough to get married**.
- She isn't **young enough to have** a child.
- I'm not **rich enough to buy** a small flat in the suburb, let alone a villa in the city centre.
- He isn't **strong enough to carry** the heavy case.
- Is my English **good enough for me to work** as a translator?

## The infinitive refers to the object of a verb

- This cup of tea isn't **cool enough for me to drink**.
- Your question isn't **easy enough for me to answer**.
- The house isn't **cheap enough for them to buy**.
- The car isn't **reliable enough for us to take** for a long journey.

## The infinitive refers to the object of a preposition

- The grass isn't **dry enough for us to sit on**.
- The ice isn't **thick enough for us to walk on**.
- The light isn't **strong enough for me to read by**.
- The water isn't **warm enough for us to swim in**.
- He isn't **good enough for me to make friends with**.

## Enough as adjective and as pronoun

- Before a noun `enough` is an adjective: I have got **enough books** to study. / He doesn't have **enough money** to study abroad.
- Alone it is a pronoun: He doesn't earn **enough** to live on.

## Notes & exceptions

- `have + enough + abstract noun` can be replaced by `have + the + abstract noun`:
  - I don't have **enough / the patience** to wait any longer.
  - She didn't have **enough / the sense** to make up her mind.
  - He didn't have **enough / the courage** to admit his mistake.
