# Every and Each

> [!abstract] In one line
> Both take a singular noun and a singular verb, but `every` looks at the group as a whole while `each` looks at the members one by one.

## Form

```
every / each + singular noun + singular verb
each + of + object pronoun / the / these / possessive + plural noun
pronoun + each
```

## When to use

- `every` implies "all of them together"; it is used when the group is seen as a complete set.
- `each` implies "one by one"; it is used when the members are considered separately.
- Only `each` can stand alone without a noun, and only `each` combines with `of`.

## Examples

- **Every/Each room has** a number. (= all the rooms in the house)
- I go for a walk **every day**. (all days, habitually)
- **Each day** seems to pass very slowly. (one day at a time)
- There are six flats. **Each** has its own entrance.
- **Each of** the six flats **has** its own entrance.
- They gave **us each** an English book.
- **Each of us** can have our own desk.

## Notes & exceptions

- `every` cannot stand alone and cannot take `of`:
  - ❌ Every has its own entrance. → ✅ **Each** has its own entrance.
  - ❌ Every of the six flats ... → ✅ **Each of** the six flats ...
- `each` can follow a pronoun or the subject: *They gave us **each** a book.*
- ❌ Each us → ✅ **Each of us**.
- The verb after `each of + plural noun` stays singular: *Each of the flats **has** ...*

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Every students has a book. → ✅ **Every student has** a book.
- ❌ Each of the boys have a bike. → ✅ **Each of the boys has** a bike.

## Contrast with

- [[Both Either and Neither]] — used for exactly two
- [[All Most Some and Half]] — `all` takes a plural noun and a plural verb
