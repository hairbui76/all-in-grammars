# Expressing Sympathy

> [!abstract] In one line
> Noticing that something is wrong, asking about it, and reacting with concern rather than with facts.

## Useful patterns

### Asking what is wrong

| Expression | Register |
|---|---|
| `Are you feeling OK?` | informal, caring |
| `What's the matter?` | neutral |
| `Is there anything wrong?` | neutral |
| `What's wrong with it?` | about a thing, not a person |
| `Are you all right?` | informal |

### Reacting to bad news

| Strength | Expression |
|---|---|
| Strong | `Oh, I'm so sorry to hear that.` · `That's terrible.` |
| Neutral | `Oh dear.` · `What a pity.` · `That's a shame.` |
| Reassuring | `Don't worry.` · `Never mind.` · `It'll be all right.` |
| Following up with advice | `Maybe you should take a rest.` · `You'd better see a doctor.` |

## When to use

- React **before** you advise: sympathy first (`Oh dear.`), then the suggestion (`Maybe you should ...`).
- `I'm sorry to hear that` expresses sympathy; it is not an apology — see [[Apologising and Responding]].
- Answer `Are you feeling OK?` honestly and briefly: `No, I'm not. I feel awful.`

## Examples

- "I have a terrible headache." — "**Maybe you should take a rest.**"
- "**Are you feeling OK?**" — "**No, I'm not. I feel awful.**"
- "I think the vase is broken." — "**What's wrong with it?**"
- "Sorry, I've lost your umbrella." — "**Never mind.** It doesn't matter."

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "I have a terrible headache." — "Not very well. Thanks." (answers a *how are you* question instead)
- ❌ Giving advice with no sympathetic reaction first — it sounds cold.

## Contrast with

- [[Apologising and Responding]] — sorry for what *you* did, not for what happened to someone else
- [[Suggestions and Advice]] — the advice that usually follows sympathy
- [[Greetings and Small Talk]] — routine `How are you?` rather than genuine concern
