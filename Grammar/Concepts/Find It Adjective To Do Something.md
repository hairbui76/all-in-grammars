# Find It Adjective To Do Something

> [!abstract] In one line
> After verbs such as `find`, `think` and `consider`, an empty `it` stands in for the real object, which is a to-infinitive placed at the end.

## Form

`S + find / think / consider / believe / make + it + adjective + to-infinitive`

## When to use

- When the real object of the verb is a whole infinitive phrase, which would be too heavy immediately after the verb.
- Very common with `difficult`, `easy`, `hard`, `impossible`, `necessary`, `important`.

## Examples

- I **find it very difficult to learn** about English.
- They **found it easy to overcome** that problem.
- We **consider it necessary to check** every answer.
- The noise **made it impossible to concentrate**.
- She **thought it wise to say** nothing.

## Notes & exceptions

- The `it` cannot be left out: *I find difficult to learn English* is ungrammatical.
- A noun phrase may replace the adjective: *I find it a pleasure to work with you*.
- A `that`-clause can also be the real object: *I find it strange **that** he never phoned*.
- The same preparatory `it` appears in subject position with a different pattern → see [[Infinitive As Subject]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I find very difficult to speak English. → ✅ I **find it** very difficult **to speak** English.
- ❌ They found easy overcoming the problem. → ✅ They **found it easy to overcome** the problem.

## Contrast with

- [[It Is Adjective For Somebody To Do]] — preparatory `it` as subject, not object
- [[It Is Adjective Of Somebody To Do]] — judges the person, not the task
- [[Infinitive As Subject]] — the infinitive placed first instead
