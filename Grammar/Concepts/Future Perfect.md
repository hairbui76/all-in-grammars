# Future Perfect

> [!abstract] In one line
> `will have + V3` — an action that will already be finished before a given time in the future.

## Form

`(+) S + will have + V3/V-ed`
`(-) S + will not (won't) have + V3/V-ed`
`(?) Will + S + have + V3/V-ed ?`

## When to use

- An action **completed before a future time**, usually with `by`, `by the time`, `before`, `by then`.
- An action completed **before another future action** (the second verb is in [[Present Simple]]).
- To state a **total reached** by a future point: *By June I will have worked here for ten years.*

## Examples

- By this time next month I **will have finished** my thesis.
- She **will have left** by the time you get there.
- **Will** you **have completed** the report before Friday?
- By 2030 the company **will have opened** fifty branches.
- We **won't have received** the results before Monday.

## Notes & exceptions

- Typical markers: `by + future time`, `by the time + present tense clause`, `before`, `by then`.
- `by` = not later than; `until` = up to and including — do not swap them → see [[Until and By]].
  - ❌ I will have finished until Friday. → ✅ I will have finished **by** Friday.
- Passive: `will have been + V3` → see [[Passive Voice in Nine Tenses]].
- `must have + V3` looks similar but expresses deduction about the past → see [[Must For Deduction]] and [[Modal Perfect Infinitive]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ By the time he will arrive, we will have eaten. → ✅ By the time he **arrives**, we will have eaten.
- ❌ I will have went. → ✅ I **will have gone**.

## Contrast with

- [[Future Simple]] — the action simply happens at a future time
- [[Future Continuous]] — in progress **at** the future point, not finished before it
- [[Future Perfect Continuous]] — stresses the **duration** up to the future point
- [[Past Perfect]] — the same "before a reference point" logic, located in the past
- [[Present Perfect]] — completed before **now**
