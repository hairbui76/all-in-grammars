# Gerund After Prepositions

> [!abstract] In one line
> Any verb that follows a preposition must be in the gerund (`-ing`) form, never the infinitive.

## Form

`preposition + V-ing`

Common prepositions involved: `besides, to, without, at, of, against, like, for, in, on, by, with`.

## Examples

- What can you do besides **typing**?
- She is afraid of **living** on her own in a big city.
- Touch your toes without **bending** your knees!
- I'm not keen on **gambling**. I'm too afraid of **losing**.
- I'm against **saying** anything / I'm for **saying** nothing.
- I'm tired of **arguing**.
- This is a tool for **opening** tins.
- After **swimming** I felt cold.
- What about **leaving** it here and **collecting** it later?
- I'm sorry for **keeping** you waiting.
- We had difficulty in **finding** a parking place.
- In spite of **starting** late he arrived in time.
- There's no point in **waiting**.
- They argued for the sake of **arguing**.
- I have no objection to **hearing** your story again.
- He is good at **diving**. She is fond of **climbing**.
- He was fined for **being** drunk in charge of a car.
- I'm fed up with **waiting**. (colloquial)
- Do you feel like **going** out?
- She disapproves of **jogging**.
- He is thinking of **emigrating**.
- They escaped by **sliding** down a rope.
- You should be ashamed of yourself for **behaving** so badly.
- Aren't you interested in **making** money?

## Notes & exceptions

- Many phrasal verbs end in a preposition and therefore take the gerund: `be for/against`, `care for` (= like, look after), `give up`, `keep on` (= continue), `leave off` (= stop), `look forward to`, `put off` (= postpone), `see about` (= attend to), `take to`, `go on` (= continue).
  - I don't care for **standing** in queues.
  - I am looking forward to **meeting** her.
  - He took to **ringing** us up in the middle of the night.
  - Eventually the dogs left off **barking**.
  - He put off **making** a decision till he had more information.
- The commonest mistake is with `to`, which can be either a preposition or part of an infinitive → see [[To As Preposition]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I'm looking forward to meet you. → ✅ I'm looking forward to **meeting** you.
- ❌ He is good at to dive. → ✅ He is good at **diving**.
