# Gerunds

> [!abstract] In one line
> A gerund is a verb form ending in `-ing` that keeps its verbal meaning but works as a noun in the sentence.

## Form

`V + -ing`

Examples of the form: working, being, knowing, learning.

The gerund has exactly the same shape as the present participle; only its function (noun-like) tells them apart.

## When to use

- **As subject**, when the action is viewed in a general sense
- **As complement of `be`**, naming a hobby, dream, passion or habit
- **After prepositions** → see [[Gerund After Prepositions]]
- **After certain verbs** → see [[Verbs Followed By Gerund]]

## Examples

### Gerund as subject

- **Reading** French is easier than **speaking** it. (= It is easier to read French than to speak it.)
- **Teaching** is a noble job.
- **Smoking** is harmful to your health.
- **Dancing** made him tired.
- **Travelling** by train is cheaper than **going** by plane.

### Gerund as complement of `be`

- I must say that my great passion in life is **studying**.
- My dream is **becoming** a good teacher.
- Her hobby is **painting**.
- My mother's habit is **staying** up late and **getting** up early.

## Notes & exceptions

- Like the infinitive, the gerund can be the subject of a clause after `believe, consider, discover, expect, find, think, wonder`. After `find`, `that` and `be` may be dropped:
  - He found that **parking was** difficult. → He found **parking** difficult.
- Gerund vs infinitive after `find` changes the meaning:
  - He found **parking** difficult. (a general, habitual judgement)
  - He found it difficult **to park**. (one particular occasion)
- The gerund is used in short prohibition notices: `No smoking.` `No waiting.` `No fishing.`
- The gerund is used in proverbs: **Seeing** is **believing**.

## Contrast with

- [[Infinitives]] — `to + V`, often for a specific occasion or a purpose
- [[Participles]] — same `-ing` shape, but adjectival/adverbial function
