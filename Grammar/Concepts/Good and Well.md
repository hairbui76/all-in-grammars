# Good and Well

> [!abstract] In one line
> `good` is the adjective, `well` is its irregular adverb — but `well` is also an adjective meaning "in good health".

## Form

```
good  = adjective (describes a noun)
well  = adverb of manner from `good` (describes a verb)
well  = adjective meaning "healthy" (after a linking verb only)
```

There is no adverb ~~goodly~~.

## Examples

`good` as an adjective:

- He's a **good** worker.
- The food looks **good**.

`well` as the adverb of `good`:

- He works **well**. [not: ~~He works goodly~~]
- She speaks Japanese **well**.
- He reads **well**.
- They speak English **well**.

`well` as an adjective meaning "healthy":

- How are you? — Very **well**, thank you.
- The boy isn't **well**. He looks **ill**.

## Notes & exceptions

- `well` in the "healthy" sense is predicative only; before a noun say *a healthy man*, not ~~a well man~~ → see [[Predicative-Only Adjectives]].
- `well` and `badly` are also used as adverbs of degree, in which case they stand **before** the verb or past participle:
  - The door **badly** needs a coat of paint.
  - He was **badly** wounded.
  - I'd like the steak **well** done.
- As adverbs of manner they follow a passive verb or an object: *He paid her **badly*** / *She was **badly** paid* / *The trip was **well** organised*.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ He speaks English good. → ✅ He speaks English **well**.
- ❌ He works goodly. → ✅ He works **well**.
