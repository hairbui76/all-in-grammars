# In Case and Lest

> [!abstract] In one line
> `in case` and `lest` express a precaution — something done because of a possible future problem, not because the problem has happened.

## Form

```
Main clause + in case + S + V(present simple)   → present / future reference
Main clause + in case + S + V(past simple)      → past reference
Main clause + lest + S + should + V(bare)
In case of + noun + , + main clause             (= If there is ...)
```

## Examples

- Geoff is a football referee. He usually wears two watches **in case** one of them **stops**.
- Ann might phone tonight. I don't want to go out **in case she phones**. (= because it is possible she will phone)
- I'll make a cake **in case** someone **drops** in at the weekend.
- I carry a spare wheel **in case** I **have** a puncture.
- I always keep candles handy **in case** there **is** a power cut.
- I always kept candles handy **in case** there **was** a power cut.
- We bought some more food **in case** Tom **came**.
- We rang the bell again **in case** they **hadn't heard** it the first time.
- He doesn't/didn't dare to leave the house **lest** someone **should recognize** him.

## Notes & exceptions

- No future tense is used after `in case`: use the present simple for present/future situations, and the past simple for past situations.
- `just in case` marks a possibility the speaker thinks is less likely:
  - I don't think it will rain but I'll take an umbrella **just in case**.
- `in case of + noun` is a different structure meaning `if there is ...`:
  - **In case of** fire, please leave the building as quickly as possible.
  - **In case of** emergency, telephone this number. (= If there is an emergency ...)
- `lest` is formal and literary; it is followed by `should + bare infinitive`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I'll take an umbrella in case it will rain. → ✅ ... **in case it rains**.
- ❌ In case of it rains, stay at home. → ✅ **In case of** rain, stay at home. / **If it rains**, stay at home.

## Contrast with

- [[Clauses of Purpose]] — a deliberate aim, not a precaution
- [[Purpose Phrases With Gerund]] — `for fear of / for fear that` also express avoidance
- [[First Conditional]] — `if` states a condition; `in case` states an advance precaution
