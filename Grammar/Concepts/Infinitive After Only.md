# Infinitive After Only

> [!abstract] In one line
> `only + to-infinitive` expresses an unexpected and disappointing result of the action in the main clause.

## Form

`S + V ..., only + to + V`

## Examples

- He survived the aircrash **only to die** on the desert.
- He rushed to the station **only to find** that the train had left.
- He hurriedly decided to get married **only to divorce** a few months later.

## Notes & exceptions

- The result is always something the subject did not want or expect; the tone is ironic.
- This is not the infinitive of purpose — the subject was not trying to bring the result about.

## Contrast with

- [[Clauses of Purpose]] — `to V` / `in order to V` expresses intention, not an unwanted outcome
- [[Clauses of Result]] — a finite alternative with `so ... that`
