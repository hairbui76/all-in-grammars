# Infinitive In Reduced Relative Clauses

> [!abstract] In one line
> A relative clause can be reduced to a full infinitive after ordinal numbers, `only`, superlatives, and when the clause expresses obligation or duty.

## Form

`N + first / second / last / next / only / superlative + to + V`

`N + to + V` (when the clause expresses a duty or something that must be done)

## Case a — after ordinals, `only` and superlatives

- She was the last person **who left** the conference. → She was the last person **to leave** the conference.
- He is always the last **to arrive**.
- He was the only person **to survive** the air crash.
- The best film **to see** is "Titanic".
- He loves parties. He is always the first **to come** and the last **to leave**.

## Case b — when the clause expresses duty or obligation

- I have a lot of exercises **which I have to do** today. → I have a lot of exercises **to do** today.
- English is an important language **to master**.
- She has two piles of dirty clothes **to wash**.

## Notes & exceptions

- The infinitive replaces the whole relative clause including the relative pronoun.
- The reduction is possible only when the relative pronoun is the subject of the clause (case a) or when the clause carries a modal meaning of necessity (case b).
