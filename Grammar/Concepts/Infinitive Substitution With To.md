# Infinitive Substitution With To

> [!abstract] In one line
> To avoid repeating a verb already mentioned, only the particle `to` is kept and the verb is left out.

## Form

`... V + to` (the rest of the infinitive is understood from the previous clause)

## Verbs and auxiliaries used this way

want · would like · would love · mean · try · hate · hope · intend · plan ·
have · need · ought · used to · be going to

## Examples

- Would you like to go to the cinema with me tonight? — Yes, I'd like **to**.
- Why did you have to catch a taxi? — I had **to** because I was late.
- He sings better than he used **to**.
- Have you repaired the car? — No, but I'm going **to**.

## Notes & exceptions

- `to` is never dropped in this pattern: ❌ *Yes, I'd like.* → ✅ *Yes, I'd like to.*
- After `can`, `must`, `will` and other bare-infinitive modals nothing is left at all: *Can you come? — Yes, I can.*
