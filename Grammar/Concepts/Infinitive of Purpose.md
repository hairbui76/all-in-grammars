# Infinitive of Purpose

> [!abstract] In one line
> Purpose is most commonly expressed by a `to`-infinitive, which can be expanded to `in order to` or `so as to`.

## Form

```
S + V + to + V(bare)
S + V + in order (not) to + V(bare)
S + V + so as (not) to + V(bare)
in order for + O + to + V(bare)
```

`in order to` suggests the subject wants to perform the action; `so as to` suggests the subject wants the action to happen. `in order to` is the more common of the two.

## Examples

- He went to France **to learn** French.
- **To be** there in time, we must leave now.
- We come here **to learn** English.
- **To pass** the exam, you must study much harder.
- She took off her shoes and jumped in **to save** him.
- He sent Tom to the shop **to buy** bread.

## When to use `in order to` / `so as to`

**1. With a negative infinitive** (this is obligatory — plain `not to` is not used alone):

- He left his gun outside **in order/so as not to frighten** us.
- We are trying our best to study **so as not to fail** in the exam.
- I always drive carefully **in order not to have** an accident.

**2. With `to be` and `to have`:**

- She left work early **in order/so as to be** at home when he arrived.
- She gave up work **in order/so as to have** more time with the children.

**3. When the purpose is less immediate:**

- He is studying maths **in order/so as to qualify** for a better job.
- **In order to learn** English in a short time, learners have no way but to practise as much as they can.
- She learnt typing **in order to help** her husband with his work.

**4. In longer sentences, to make clear that the infinitive expresses purpose:**

- He was accused of misrepresenting the facts **in order/so as to make** the scheme seem feasible.
- He took much more trouble over the figures than he usually did **in order/so as to show** his new boss what a careful worker he was.

**5. When there is a personal object but the infinitive must refer to the subject:**

- He sent his sons to a boarding school **in order/so as to have** some peace. (the father wants peace)
- Compare: He sent his sons to a boarding school **to learn** to live in a community. (the sons learn)

## Notes & exceptions

- When the main verb has a personal object, a plain infinitive normally refers to that object: *He sent Tom to the shop **to buy** bread.* (Tom buys the bread.)
- `in order for + object + to + V` names a different doer explicitly:
  - I gave her my address **in order for her to write** to me.
  - I am speaking slowly and clearly **in order for my students to understand** what I mean.
  - **In order for a film to be** entertaining, it has to have an interesting story.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ He left his gun outside not to frighten us. → ✅ ... **so as not to frighten** us.
- ❌ I study English for improve my job. → ✅ I study English **to improve** my job.
