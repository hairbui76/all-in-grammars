# Infinitives

> [!abstract] In one line
> The infinitive is the base form of a verb, used either with `to` (full infinitive) or without it (bare infinitive), and it has six different forms.

## Form

An infinitive has two parts: `to + V`. Without `to` it is called the **bare infinitive**.

## The six forms of the infinitive

| # | Name | Form | Examples |
|---|------|------|----------|
| 1 | Present infinitive | `to + V` | to do, to make, to work |
| 2 | Present continuous infinitive | `to be + V-ing` | to be doing, to be making |
| 3 | Perfect infinitive | `to have + PII` | to have done, to have made |
| 4 | Perfect continuous infinitive | `to have been + V-ing` | to have been doing, to have been making |
| 5 | Passive present infinitive | `to be + PII` | to be done, to be made |
| 6 | Passive perfect infinitive | `to have been + PII` | to have been done, to have been made |

## Examples

- It is easy **to do** this exercise.
- Do you think you will still **be doing** this job in ten years' time?
- I **must have dropped** it somewhere in this room.
- By the end of this year, I will **have been teaching** for 12 years.
- Do you want **to be examined** by the doctor?
- America is thought **to have been discovered** by Columbus before others.

## Notes & exceptions

- Each of the six forms combines with modal verbs to express time and aspect in deduction: `must + V` (now), `must be V-ing` (now, in progress), `must have PII` (past), `must have been V-ing` (past, in progress).
- The infinitive is a non-finite verb form: it carries no person or number agreement.
