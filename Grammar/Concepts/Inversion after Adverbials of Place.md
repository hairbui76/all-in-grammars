# Inversion after Adverbials of Place

> [!abstract] In one line
> When a sentence opens with an adverbial of place or direction, an intransitive verb can be moved in front of its subject — a stylistic device of formal and literary writing.

## Form

```
Adverbial of place/direction + Verb + Subject
```

No auxiliary is added: the main verb itself moves in front of the subject.

## Examples

- David began to open the three parcels. Inside the first **was a dictionary** from his uncle.
- Above the fireplace **was a portrait of Lenin**.
- In an armchair **sat his mother**.
- Into the room **walked a man with a gun**.

## Notes & exceptions

- The verb is normally `be`, or a verb of position or movement: `climb, come, fly, go, hang, lie, run, sit, stand`.
- The verb is usually in the **past simple**.
- Unlike negative-adverb inversion, this pattern does **not** use `do / does / did`; the lexical verb is placed directly before the subject.
- The pattern is blocked when the subject is a **pronoun**:
  - ❌ In an armchair sat she. → ✅ In an armchair **she sat**.
- Typical of formal or literary style, especially in descriptive narration.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Above the fireplace did hang a portrait. → ✅ Above the fireplace **hung a portrait**.
- ❌ Into the room walked he. → ✅ Into the room **he walked**.
