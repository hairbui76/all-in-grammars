# Inversion in Conditionals

> [!abstract] In one line
> `If` can be dropped and the auxiliary `should`, `were` or `had` moved to the front of the clause — a formal equivalent of the ordinary if-clause.

## Form

```
Type 1:  Should + S + V(bare), main clause
Type 2:  Were + S (+ to + V), main clause
Type 3:  Had + S + (not) + V3, main clause
```

The negative is **not** contracted after inversion: `Had she not ...`, `Were it not ...`.

## When to use

- Formal or written English, especially notices, business letters and academic prose
- To make a condition sound more remote, tentative or polite

## Examples

Type 1 with `should` (a less likely possibility):

- **Should** he **call**, please take a message. (= If he calls, ...)
- **Should** she **work** hard, she will be promoted.
- **Should there be** a change in the weather, they will postpone all the flights.
- If you should hear the fire alarm, leave the building at once. → **Should you hear** the fire alarm, leave the building at once.
- If he has cheated, he will have to be punished. → **Should he have cheated**, he will have to be punished.

Type 2 with `were`:

- **Were I** you, I would not give up such a good job.
- **Were he** rich, he would help the poor.
- **Were I to know** the answer, I would tell you immediately.
- If Alan were to eat breakfast, he wouldn't overeat at lunch. → **Were Alan to eat** breakfast, he wouldn't overeat at lunch.
- If he worked more slowly, he wouldn't make many mistakes. → **Were he to work** more slowly, he wouldn't make many mistakes.

Type 3 with `had`:

- **Had she not insisted** on kissing everybody goodbye, she wouldn't have missed the train.
- **Had it not been for** her insistence, she wouldn't have missed the train.
- If they had realized the danger, they would have done it differently. → **Had they realized** the danger, they would have done it differently.

## Notes & exceptions

- Inversion happens **only in the if-clause**; the main clause keeps normal word order.
- `Were + S + to + V` replaces a past-simple if-clause and pushes the hypothesis into the future. It is the pattern used when the if-clause verb is **not** `be`.
- `Were it not for + noun` and `Had it not been for + noun` are the inverted equivalents of `If it weren't for` / `If it hadn't been for`.
- In a **negative** condition, `not` goes after the subject and is never contracted:
  - If you don't believe what I say, ask your mother. → **Should you not believe** what I say, ask your mother.
  - If she were not shy, she would enjoy parties. → **Were she not shy**, she would enjoy parties.
  - If I hadn't seen it with my own eyes, I wouldn't have believed it. → **Had I not seen** it with my own eyes, I wouldn't have believed it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Hadn't she insisted ... → ✅ **Had she not** insisted ...
- ❌ Should you to hear the alarm ... → ✅ **Should you hear** the alarm ...
- ❌ Were I to be you, ... → ✅ **Were I you**, ...
