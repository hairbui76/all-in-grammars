# Inversion with Negative Phrases

> [!abstract] In one line
> Fronting a noun phrase or prepositional phrase containing `no` forces question word order in the main clause.

## Form

```
No + Noun + Auxiliary + S + V(bare)
Not any + Noun + Auxiliary + S + V(bare)
At no time / On no account / Under no circumstances ... + Auxiliary + S + V(bare)
```

## Common fronted phrases

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `At no time` | never, at no point |
| `On no condition` | absolutely not |
| `On no account` | not for any reason |
| `Under / In no circumstances` | in no situation whatsoever |
| `For no reason` | for no reason at all |
| `In no way` | by no means |
| `No longer` | not any more |

## Examples

- **No money shall I lend** you from now on. (= Not any money shall I lend you from now on.)
- **No time will he spend** reading these books.
- **Not a tear did she shed** when the story ended in tragedy.
- **At no time did he know** she came from a rich family.
- **No longer do I work** for them. (= I no longer work for them.)
- **Under no circumstances are passengers permitted** to open the doors themselves.
- **On no account must you touch** the switch.
- **On no condition shall we accept** their proposal.
- **For no reason must you cheat** in your exam.

## Notes & exceptions

- These transformations rewrite an ordinary negative sentence:
  - The outcome of the election was never in doubt. → **At no time was** the outcome of the election in doubt.
  - You shouldn't phone the police in any circumstances. → **Under no circumstances should** you phone the police.
  - He never suspected that his wife was a spy. → **At no time did** he suspect that his wife was a spy.
- `Not any` in the original becomes `no` when fronted: `I am not interested in politics any longer` → **No longer am I** interested in politics.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Under no circumstances you should phone the police. → ✅ Under no circumstances **should you** phone the police.
- ❌ At no time he knew the truth. → ✅ At no time **did he** know the truth.
