# Inversion with Not Only But Also

> [!abstract] In one line
> When `not only` opens the sentence, the first clause inverts; the second clause keeps normal word order and adds `but ... also / as well`.

## Form

```
Not only + Auxiliary + S + V(bare) + but + S + also + V ...
```

Variants of the second half: `but ... also`, `but ... as well`, `but ... too`.

## Examples

- He is not only good at English but he can also draw very well.
  → **Not only is he** good at English **but** he can draw very well.
- **Not only does he sing** well **but** he **also** plays musical instruments perfectly.
- **Not only does he sing** well **but** he plays musical instruments perfectly **as well**.
- **Not only did he spend** all his money **but** he **also** borrowed some from his friends.
- **Not only did the patient refuse** to take the tablets **but** he **also** hit the doctor.
- They not only came but they also stayed for dinner. → **Not only did they come but** they **also** stayed for dinner.

## Notes & exceptions

- Only the first clause is inverted. The clause after `but` uses ordinary subject + verb order.
- `also` sits in its normal mid-position (after the auxiliary or before the main verb); `as well` and `too` go at the end.
- Without fronting, no inversion is needed: `He is not only good at English but he can also draw.`

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Not only he did spend all his money ... → ✅ Not only **did he** spend all his money ...
- ❌ Not only did he spend all his money but also he borrowed some. → ✅ ... **but he also** borrowed some.
