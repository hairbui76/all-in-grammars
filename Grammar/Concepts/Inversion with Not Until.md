# Inversion with Not Until

> [!abstract] In one line
> Fronting `not until / not till + time expression` inverts the main clause and means "only at that moment did it happen".

## Form

```
Not until/till + clause          + Auxiliary + S + V(bare)
Not until/till + then/later/adverb of time + Auxiliary + S + V(bare)
```

## Examples

- He couldn't speak until he was 8.
  → **Not until he was eight could he speak.**
  → It was not until he was 8 **that** he could speak. (cleft alternative)
- I didn't know that I had lost my key until I got home.
  → **Not until I got home did I know** that I had lost my key.
- He didn't return to his country until last June.
  → **Not till last June did he return** to his country.
- The noise next door didn't stop until midnight.
  → **Not until midnight did the noise next door stop.**

## Notes & exceptions

- The clause after `not until` keeps normal word order; only the **main** clause inverts.
- Two ways to rewrite the same idea:
  - `It was not until + time/clause + **that** + normal word order` (no inversion)
  - `Not until + time/clause + **auxiliary + subject + verb**` (inversion)
- The original negative disappears in the inverted clause — the negation is carried by `not until`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Not until I got home I knew that I had lost my key. → ✅ Not until I got home **did I know** ...
- ❌ Not until midnight did the noise next door didn't stop. → ✅ Not until midnight **did the noise next door stop**.
- ❌ It was not until he was 30 did he get married. → ✅ It was not until he was 30 **that** he got married.
