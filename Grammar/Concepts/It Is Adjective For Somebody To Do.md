# It Is Adjective For Somebody To Do

> [!abstract] In one line
> `It` stands in for a to-infinitive subject; `for + somebody` names the person concerned, and the adjective judges the **action**, not the person.

## Form

```
It + be + adjective + (for + (pro)noun) + to-infinitive
```

`It` is a dummy subject; the real subject is the to-infinitive phrase at the end.

## Adjective groups

| Meaning | Adjectives |
| --- | --- |
| feasibility | possible, impossible, easy, not easy, hard, difficult |
| frequency / custom | usual, not usual, normal, rare, customary, a common practice, the custom, the rule |
| importance / necessity | important, vital, necessary, advisable, fitting, essential |

## Examples

Feasibility:

- **It's not easy for an inexperienced man to get** a good job.
- **It is difficult for men to fulfil** domestic duty.
- **It is impossible for teachers to test** students' progress without examination.

Frequency and custom:

- **It was the rule for men and women to sit** apart.
- **It is not usual for him to be late** for work. He is always punctual.
- **It is customary for young people to help** parents of old age.

Importance and necessity:

- **It's necessary for you to practise** English every day.
- **It is important for parents to prevent** children from sniffing up heroin.
- **It is advisable for both husband and wife to spend** time with children.

## Extraposing a gerund subject

A gerund subject can be rewritten with anticipatory `it`:

```
V-ing ... + be + adjective  ⇔  It + be + adjective + to-infinitive
```

- Learning English is very easy. ⇔ **It is very easy to learn** English.
- Mastering a foreign language is necessary. ⇔ **It is necessary to master** a foreign language.
- Swimming across that rough sea is impossible. ⇔ **It is impossible to swim** across that rough sea.
- Telling her the news now is unwise. ⇔ **It is unwise to tell** her the news now.

## Notes & exceptions

- Use `for` when the adjective judges the action or its difficulty; use `of` when it judges the person's character → see [[It Is Adjective Of Somebody To Do]].
- With adjectives of necessity, a `that`-clause with `should` (or the bare subjunctive) is a formal alternative → see [[It Is Essential That Clause]].
- The `for + noun` part is dropped when the person is general or obvious.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ It is necessary you to practise English. → ✅ It is necessary **for you to practise** English.
- ❌ Is easy to learn English. → ✅ **It** is easy to learn English.
