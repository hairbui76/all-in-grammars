# It Is Essential That Clause

> [!abstract] In one line
> After `It is essential/important/vital/necessary that ...`, the verb in the `that`-clause stays in the **bare infinitive**.

## Form

`It + be + adjective of urgency + that + S + V(bare)`
`It + be + adjective + that + S + should + V(bare)` (British variant)

Adjectives used in this pattern:

`essential · important · vital · necessary · imperative · crucial · urgent · advisable · desirable · compulsory · obligatory · best · better`

## When to use

- To express **necessity, urgency or a recommendation** in formal or written English.
- The pattern is fixed: the verb takes **no `-s`**, **no `to`**, and does **not** change for tense.
- The negative is `that + S + not + V(bare)`.
- The passive is `that + S + be + V3`.

## Examples

- It is essential that every candidate **arrive** on time.
- It is vital that he **be** informed immediately.
- It is necessary that she **not miss** the deadline.
- It is imperative that the documents **be signed** today.
- It was important that the news **remain** secret.
- It is advisable that students **should register** early. (British variant)

## Notes & exceptions

- The base form does not change even when the main clause is past: *It **was** essential that he **go**.*
- Some of these adjectives also allow an infinitive paraphrase: *It is essential **for every candidate to arrive** on time* → see [[It Is Adjective For Somebody To Do]].
- The same subjunctive appears after verbs of demand and suggestion (`demand, suggest, insist, request, propose, recommend`) → see [[Subjunctive in That Clauses]].
- Do **not** confuse with `It is + adjective of judgement + of somebody to do` → see [[It Is Adjective Of Somebody To Do]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ It is essential that he arrives on time. → ✅ It is essential that he **arrive** on time.
- ❌ It is vital that she is told. → ✅ It is vital that she **be told**.
- ❌ It is necessary that he doesn't go. → ✅ It is necessary that he **not go**.
