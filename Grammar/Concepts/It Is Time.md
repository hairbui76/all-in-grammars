# It Is Time

> [!abstract] In one line
> `It's time` takes an infinitive for a neutral statement, but a past subjunctive when the speaker feels the action is overdue.

## Form

```
It's time (for sb) + to + V(bare)
It's (high / about) time + S + V(past simple)
```

`high` and `about` add a note of criticism: the action should already have happened.

## When to use

- Infinitive pattern: simply saying the right moment has come
- Past subjunctive pattern: complaining that something is late or has not been done

## Examples

Infinitive:

- **It's time for us to go** home.
- **It's time to have** breakfast.
- **It's time for them to start** their lesson.

Past subjunctive:

- **It's time we went** home.
- Jack is a great talker. But **it's high time he did** something instead of just talking.
- You are very selfish. **It's about time you realised** that you're not the most important person in the world.
- It's 10 o'clock and he is still in bed. **It's time he got** up.
- **It's time we were leaving.**
- **It's high time we left.**

## Notes & exceptions

- The past tense here refers to present time; it is a subjunctive, not past time.
- The pattern is not backshifted in reported speech.
- The two structures differ slightly: `it is time + infinitive` merely states that the correct moment has arrived, while `it is time + subject + past subjunctive` implies it is already a little late. `high` emphasises this idea.
- `It is time + I/he/she/it` cannot be followed by `were`: ✅ *It's time I **was** going.*
