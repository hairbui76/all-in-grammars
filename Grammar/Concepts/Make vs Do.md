# Make vs Do

> [!abstract] In one line
> Rule of thumb: use `do` for performing an action, task or job; use `make` for producing or creating a result.

## The rule of thumb

| Choose | When the meaning is | Test question |
|---|---|---|
| `do` | carrying out an action, activity, task, duty or work | *What activity is being performed?* |
| `make` | building, creating, producing or bringing something into existence | *What new thing results?* |

`do` = the process · `make` = the product.

## Quick decision guide

- Household chores and duties → `do`: `do the washing up`, `do the ironing`, `do your homework`, `do your duty`.
- Vague or unspecified activity, especially with `something / nothing / anything / everything` → `do`: `I'm not **doing anything** today.`
- Food, drink and physical objects → `make`: `make a cup of tea`, `make a dress`, `make breakfast`.
- Speech acts and mental acts that produce something → `make`: `make a speech`, `make a decision`, `make a promise`, `make a suggestion`, `make a remark`.
- Effects that come into being → `make`: `make a noise`, `make a mess`, `make a difference`, `make a profit`.

## Confusable pairs

| `do` | `make` |
|---|---|
| `do business` (carry it on) | `make money / make a profit` (produce it) |
| `do your best` | `make an effort` |
| `do harm` | `make trouble` |
| `do research` | `make an enquiry` |
| `do a favour` | `make a promise` |
| `do your hair` | `make your bed` |

## Examples

- Every student must **do** his duty, but the principal will **make** a speech at the meeting.
- He's **making** a big mistake in **doing** business with such a big firm.
- There's nothing **doing** here. Let's **make** our way out.
- We've got a lot to **do**, so it's time we **made** a start.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ do a decision → ✅ **make** a decision
- ❌ make your homework → ✅ **do** your homework
- ❌ do a phone call → ✅ **make** a phone call
- ❌ make the washing up → ✅ **do** the washing up
