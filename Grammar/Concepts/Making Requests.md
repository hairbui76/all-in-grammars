# Making Requests

> [!abstract] In one line
> Asking another person to do something for you, and the fixed ways of agreeing or refusing.

## Useful patterns

| Register | Request | Typical response |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral | `Can you ...?` / `Could you ...?` | `Sure, I'll do it now.` |
| Polite | `Could you do me a favour?` | `Of course. What is it?` |
| Polite | `Would you mind + V-ing ...?` | `Not at all.` / `Sure, I'll do it now.` |
| Very tentative | `I wonder if you could do something for me.` | `It depends on what it is.` |
| Softening add-on | `..., if you don't mind.` | — |
| Refusing | — | `I'm sorry I can't.` / `I'm afraid not.` |

## When to use

- `Could` and `would` are more distant and therefore more polite than `can` and `will`.
- `I wonder if you could ...` is the most tentative opening; it leaves the other person free to ask what the favour is before committing.
- A request is normally accepted with a short willing formula (`Sure`, `Of course`, `No problem`), not with a bare `Yes`.

## Examples

- "**Could you do me a favour?**" — "Yes. Do you have any shirts?"
- "**I wonder if you could do something for me.**" — "**It depends on what it is.**"
- "**Would you mind sending** those flowers to Mr Brown?" — "**Sure, I'll do it now.**"

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "Would you mind sending those flowers?" — "No, I can handle it myself." (that answers an *offer*, not a request)
- ❌ Answering `Would you mind ...?` with `Yes` when you intend to agree — `mind` means *object to*, so agreement is negative.
