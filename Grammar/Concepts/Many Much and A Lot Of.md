# Many Much and A Lot Of

> [!abstract] In one line
> `many` and `much` express large quantity mainly in negatives and questions, while `a lot of` and `lots of` are preferred in affirmative sentences.

## Form

| Expression | Goes with | Typical sentence type |
|---|---|---|
| `many` | plural countable noun | negative, question |
| `much` | uncountable noun | negative, question |
| `a lot of` / `lots of` | plural or uncountable | affirmative |
| `a large number of` | plural countable noun | affirmative |
| `a great deal of` | uncountable noun | affirmative |

## Examples

### Negative and interrogative

- There aren't **many shops** along this street.
- Were there **many people** at the meeting?
- There isn't **much water** in the bottle.
- Does the newspaper have **much information**?

### Affirmative with `a lot of` / `lots of`

- He's got **lots of friends** here.
- **A lot of time** is needed to learn a foreign language.

### Affirmative with `a large number of` / `a great deal of`

- He has got **a large number of** English books.
- They have invested **a great deal of** money in education.

## Notes & exceptions

- `many` and `much` ARE used in affirmative sentences after `too`, `so`, `as` and `very`, and `a lot of` cannot replace them there:
  - There is **too much** salt in my soup. (not ~~too a lot of~~)
  - We had **so many** exercises to do. (not ~~so a lot of~~)
  - I haven't got **as much** patience as I thought. (not ~~as a lot of~~)
  - Thank you **very much** for your nice gift.
- After `a lot of` / `lots of` the verb agrees with the following noun: *A lot of time **is** needed* / *A lot of people **are** waiting.*

## Common mistakes

- ❌ He has got many friends here. (unnatural in a plain affirmative) → ✅ He has got **a lot of / lots of** friends here.
- ❌ There is too a lot of salt in my soup. → ✅ There is **too much** salt in my soup.
