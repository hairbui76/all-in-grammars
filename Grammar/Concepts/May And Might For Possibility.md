# May And Might For Possibility

> [!abstract] In one line
> `May` and `might` say that something is possibly true; each sentence can be rewritten with `Perhaps + clause`.

## Form

| Time reference | Structure | Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| now, in progress | `S + may / might be + V-ing` | `Perhaps + present continuous clause` |
| past, completed | `S + may / might have + PII` | `Perhaps + past simple clause` |
| past, in progress | `S + may / might have been + V-ing` | `Perhaps + past continuous clause` |

## Examples — present

- What is he doing? — I don't know for certain but he **may be playing** sports in the club. / Perhaps he is playing ...
- They **may be hiding** in the wood. = Perhaps they are hiding in the wood.
- They **may be looking** forward to hearing from you.
- They **might be waiting** for someone.

## Examples — past

- A sea monster **might have attacked** the ship.
- Lan was late for school this afternoon. She **might have overslept**. / Her bike **might have broken** on half-way.
- He **might have stolen** the money. = Perhaps he stole the money.
- She **might have misunderstood** you.
- They **may have changed** their plans. = Perhaps they changed their plans.

## Examples — past continuous

- They **might have been playing** sports this time yesterday. = Perhaps they were playing sports.
- What was she doing then? — I'm not sure. But she **might have been cooking** dinner.

## Notes & exceptions

- `could have + PII` is a synonym of `may / might have + PII` in this meaning: *They **might / could have taken** this road.* = Perhaps they took this road.
- `could have + PII` has a second, different meaning (an unused past opportunity) → see [[Could Have Done]].

## Contrast with

- [[Must For Deduction]] — the speaker is certain it is true
- [[Cannot For Negative Deduction]] — the speaker is certain it is not true
