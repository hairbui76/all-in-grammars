# Modal Changes in Reported Speech

> [!abstract] In one line
> Only a few modals change when speech is reported; those that are already past forms, and those expressing advice or supposition, stay as they are.

## Form

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
|---|---|
| will | would |
| shall | should / would |
| can | could |
| may | might |
| must (obligation) | had to |
| needn't | didn't have to |
| could, might, ought to, used to, should, shouldn't | unchanged |

## When to use

- Apply the changes only when the reporting verb is in a past tense
- `must`, `can`, `may` and `will` shift; the rest are already remote enough to stay

## Examples

- His father said to him, "You **must** study harder." → His father told him that he **had to** study harder.
- Jane said, "You **needn't** water the flowers because it rained last night." → Jane said that he **didn't have to** water the flowers because it had rained the day before.
- The doctor said to Tom, "You **should** stay in bed." → The doctor told Tom that he **should** stay in bed.
- "I **will** be in Paris on Monday," she said → She said she **would** be in Paris on Monday.

## Notes & exceptions

- `must` expressing deduction or a permanent rule does not change: *He said the room **must** be empty.*
- `should` and `shouldn't` giving advice, and `ought to`, `used to`, `could`, `might` never change form.
- `You'd better ...` is usually reported not as a modal but with `advise (sb) (not) to do` → see [[Reported Speech - Commands]].
