# Non-Defining Relative Clauses

> [!abstract] In one line
> A non-defining relative clause adds extra information about an already identified noun and is always separated by commas.

## Form

`Antecedent, + who/whom/which/whose + clause, + ...`

| | Subject | Object | Possessive |
|---|---|---|---|
| For persons | who | whom / who | whose |
| For things | which | which | whose / of which |

## When to use

- The antecedent is already fully identified: a proper noun, a possessive adjective + noun, or a demonstrative + noun.
- The clause can be deleted without damaging the sense of the sentence.

## Examples

- Newton, **who invented the theory of gravitation**, was a famous English scientist.
- President Ho Chi Minh, **whom we admire**, was an outstanding leader.
- James Watt, **whose invention of the steam engine is famous throughout the world**, was a Scottish inventor.
- My dictionary, **which has 150,000 words**, is very useful to my studies.
- English, **which we are learning**, is the most international language in the world.
- My television, **whose case is made of wood**, was made in Russia.

## Notes & exceptions

- `that` is **never** used in a non-defining clause.
- The relative pronoun can **never** be omitted, even when it is the object.
- `which` can also refer back to a whole preceding clause → see [[Which Referring to a Whole Clause]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Newton, that invented the theory of gravitation, ... → ✅ Newton, **who** invented ...
- ❌ President Ho Chi Minh, we admire, was ... → ✅ President Ho Chi Minh, **whom** we admire, was ...
