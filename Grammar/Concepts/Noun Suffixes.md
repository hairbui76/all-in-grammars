# Noun Suffixes

> [!abstract] In one line
> These endings turn verbs and adjectives into abstract nouns.

## Form

`adjective / verb + noun suffix` → abstract noun

The endings are: `-y, -cy, -ty, -ity, -al, -ion, -sion, -tion, -ation, -dom, -sis, -ence, -ance, -ure, -ship, -ness, -age, -ment, -ery`.

## From adjectives

| Adjective | Noun |
|---|---|
| difficult | difficulty |
| clear | clarity |
| fluent | fluency |
| popular | popularity |
| free | freedom |
| dark | darkness |
| urgent | urgency |

## From verbs

| Verb | Noun |
|---|---|
| refuse | refusal |
| divide | division |
| admire | admiration |
| diagnose | diagnosis |
| attend | attendance |
| invent | invention |
| introduce | introduction |
| differ | difference |
| fail | failure |
| use | usage |
| improve | improvement |
| rob | robbery |

## From nouns

| Noun | Noun |
|---|---|
| friend | friendship |

## Examples

- The doctor's **diagnosis** was that I had bronchitis.
- His **failure** to become a minister was a shock to him.
- I had **difficulty** in improving my English speaking skill.
- What is the **difference** between these two books?
- I don't understand his **refusal** to help us.
- The American **usage** of this word is different from the British.
- The teacher was pleased at the **improvement** in his students' work.
- A book sometimes sells better if it has an **introduction** written by a famous person.
- This is a matter of great **urgency**. It must be solved immediately.
- She could see nothing in the **darkness**.

## Notes & exceptions

- The right ending is fixed by the root — you cannot predict it: *difficult → difficulty* but *clear → clarity*, *popular → popularity*.
- Spelling often changes at the join: `divide → division`, `admire → admiration`, `diagnose → diagnosis`.
- `-ion`, `-ity` and `-ence` move the stress; `-ment`, `-ness`, `-ship`, `-age`, `-dom` do not → see [[Suffixes That Shift Stress to the Previous Syllable]].
