# Order of Adjectives

> [!abstract] In one line
> When several adjectives modify one noun they follow a fixed sequence and are normally not joined by `and`.

## Form

```
Determiner - Value - Size - Age - Shape - Colour - Origin - Material - Compound - NOUN
```

A common memory aid for the middle columns: **O**pinion, **S**ize, **A**ge, **S**hape, **C**olour, **O**rigin, **M**aterial, **P**urpose.

## When to use

- Two or more adjectives before the same noun: put them in the order above and leave out `and`.

## Examples

- He has a **small white** car. (size + colour)
- She has an **expensive wool** coat. (value + material)
- It's a **small, shiny, black leather** handbag. (size + value + colour + material)
- their huge circular **swimming** pool
- my Swedish **wooden salad** bowl
- Jane's pretty Victorian **writing** desk
- his charming whitewashed **country** cottage
- a priceless nineteenth-century impressionist **painting**

## Notes & exceptions

- `and` **is** used when two or more adjectives describe the same characteristic — the same colour dimension, the same material, the same quality:
  - It's a **yellow and black** sports car.
  - That is a **concrete and glass** factory.
- Commas may separate several adjectives in the same category; no comma is used between the last adjective and the noun.
- The last item before the noun is often itself a noun functioning as a classifier (`swimming pool`, `salad bowl`, `garden seat`) — it stays closest to the head noun.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ a white small car → ✅ a **small white** car
- ❌ a small and white car → ✅ a **small white** car
