# Order of Adverbs

> [!abstract] In one line
> When several adverbials end a clause they follow the sequence **manner – place – time**, and no adverb may separate a verb from its object.

## Form

```
S + V + (object) + MANNER + PLACE + TIME
```

## Examples

- She sang **very well** (manner) **at the concert** (place) **last night** (time).
  [not: ~~last night at the concert~~]
- He works **in the garage** (place) **on Tuesdays** (time).
  [not: ~~on Tuesdays in the garage~~]
- Let's go **to bed** (place) **early** (time).
  [not: ~~early to bed~~]

## Notes & exceptions

- Never put an adverb between the verb and its object:
  - They speak English **very well**. [not: ~~speak very well English~~]
  - Can you do your work **now**? [not: ~~do now your work~~]
- Adverbs of frequency are the exception — they go before the main verb, not at the end → see [[Adverbs of Frequency]].
- Manner adverbs may move in front of the verb when the object is long → see [[Adverbs of Manner]].
- A time adverb may also be fronted for emphasis: *Yesterday I had my car serviced.*

## Common mistakes

- ❌ She sang last night at the concert very well. → ✅ She sang **very well at the concert last night**.
- ❌ I like very much this song. → ✅ I like this song **very much**.
