# Participles as Adjectives

> [!abstract] In one line
> A present participle before a noun has an active meaning; a past participle before a noun has a passive meaning.

## Form

```
V-ing + Noun          → the noun DOES the action
V-ed / Irregular + Noun → the noun RECEIVES the action
```

## Examples

### Present participle — active

- a **sleeping** child (the child is sleeping)
- a **lying** dog
- **dripping** water
- a **laughing** girl
- a **talking** girl
- a **floating** house

### Past participle — passive

- **boiled** water (the water has been boiled)
- a **broken** leg
- **fried** rice
- **boiled** chicken
- **grilled** lamb chops
- **steamed** sticky rice

## Notes & exceptions

- The same verb gives two different adjectives: `boiling water` (water that is boiling) vs `boiled water` (water that has been boiled).
- Longer participle phrases usually go **after** the noun instead: `the dog **lying on the floor**`, `the man **arrested by the police**` — see [[Reduced Relative Clauses]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ a boiling egg for breakfast (when you mean one that has been cooked) → ✅ a **boiled** egg
- ❌ a sleeped child → ✅ a **sleeping** child
