# Passive Infinitive

> [!abstract] In one line
> `to be + past participle` is the passive infinitive, used wherever an active infinitive could stand but the subject receives the action instead of performing it.

## Form

- Simple: `to be + P2` — `to be done`, `to be made`, `to be written`
- Perfect: `to have been + P2` — `to have been stolen`

## Examples

- I want you to repair the car now. → I want the car **to be repaired** (by you) now.
- I'd like someone to take me to the Opera House. → I'd like **to be taken** to the Opera House.
- This calculator is small enough **to be kept** in your pocket.
- He is said **to have been arrested** last night.

## Notes & exceptions

- After `want`, `would like`, `expect`, `ask` etc., the object of the active becomes the subject of the passive infinitive.
- With `adjective + enough / too + adjective`, the passive infinitive is used when the subject is affected by the action: `small enough to be kept`.

## Contrast with

- [[Passive Gerund]] — `being + P2`, after prepositions and `-ing` verbs
- [[Impersonal Passive]] — `be said to be / to have been`, a major use of the passive infinitive
