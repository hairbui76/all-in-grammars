# Passive Reporting Structures

> [!abstract] In one line
> Verbs of saying and thinking can be made passive with a personal subject followed by an infinitive whose form shows the time of the reported action.

## Form

`People say that + S + V ...` → `S + be said + to-infinitive`

The form of the infinitive matches the tense of the original clause:

| Original clause | Infinitive used |
|---|---|
| present simple | `to + V` |
| present continuous | `to be + V-ing` |
| present perfect / past simple | `to have + PII` |
| present perfect continuous | `to have been + V-ing` |

## Reporting verbs used this way

say · think · believe · report · expect · understand · know · suppose ·
presume · assume · acknowledge · allege

## Examples

- People say that he works for an international organization. → He **is said to work** for an international organization.
- People say that he is studying abroad. → He **is said to be studying** abroad.
- They **are said to be working** for the enemies.
- People say that Pham Xuan An was a perfect spy. → Pham Xuan An **is said to have been** a perfect spy.
- People think that Columbus discovered America before others. → America **is thought to have been discovered** by Columbus before others.
- People say that he has been playing football since he was a child. → He **is said to have been playing** football.
- They think that he has been working for an international organization for a long time. → He **is thought to have been working** ...

## Notes & exceptions

- The alternative impersonal pattern keeps `It` as subject: *It is said that he works for an international organization.*
- The subject of the passive sentence is the subject of the original `that`-clause, not its object.
