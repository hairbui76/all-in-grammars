# Passive with Modal Verbs

> [!abstract] In one line
> After a modal or a modal-like auxiliary the passive is formed with `be + past participle`; the modal itself never changes.

## Form

`S + modal + be + P2 + (by + Agent)`

Modals and auxiliaries that take this pattern:

`can` · `could` · `may` · `might` · `must` · `ought to` · `should` · `had better` ·
`have/has to` · `used to`

Active: `S + modal + V + Object` → Passive: `S + modal + be + P2 + by + Agent`

## Examples

- He **can't repair** my bike. → My bike **can't be repaired** by him.
- You **should pay** more attention to your study. → More attention **should be paid** to your study.
- You **must wash** your hands. → Your hands **must be washed**.
- Children **should treat** old men with respect. → Old men **should be treated** with respect by children.
- Our teacher **used to forbid** us to talk in class. → We **used to be forbidden** to talk in class by our teacher.
- Mr Brown **will appoint** Tom the director tomorrow. → Tom **will be appointed** the director tomorrow by Mr Brown.
- They **must report** it to the police. → It **must be reported** to the police.

## Notes & exceptions

- The negative particle stays with the modal: `can't be repaired`, `mustn't be touched`.
- `used to + V` becomes `used to be + P2` — the form `used to` is not conjugated.
- With perfect modals the pattern extends to `modal + have been + P2`: `He must have been arrested.`
