# Past Perfect

> [!abstract] In one line
> The "past before the past": an action finished before another point or action in the past.

## Form

`(+) S + had + V3/V-ed`
`(-) S + had not (hadn't) + V3/V-ed`
`(?) Had + S + V3/V-ed ?`

`had` is used for all persons. For spelling of the `-ed` form see [[Spelling Rules for -ED Forms]].

## When to use

- An action completed **before** another past action (the later action is in [[Past Simple]]).
- An action completed **before** a stated past time (`by 2010`, `by the time he arrived`).
- With `after`, `before`, `by the time`, `as soon as`, `when`, `until` → see [[Clauses of Time]].
- With `already`, `just`, `never`, `yet`, `for`, `since` shifted into the past.
- After `It was the first/second time ...` (past version of [[This Is The First Time]]).
- In the if-clause of the [[Third Conditional]] and after [[Wish and If Only]] for past regrets.
- As the backshifted form of [[Past Simple]] and [[Present Perfect]] in [[Reported Speech]] — see [[Backshift of Tenses]].

## Examples

- The train **had left** before we **reached** the station.
- By 2015 she **had published** three novels.
- I **had never seen** snow until I moved to Canada.
- He said he **had lost** his passport.
- It was the first time I **had eaten** durian.

## Notes & exceptions

- With `before` and `after` the time order is already clear, so [[Past Simple]] is often acceptable in both clauses: *He left before I arrived / had arrived.*
- Do not use the past perfect just because something happened long ago. It needs a **second, later past reference point**.
- `When + past perfect` = the first action is finished; `when + past simple` may mean the two are almost simultaneous.
  - *When I **had finished** dinner, I went out.* (finished first)
  - *When I **saw** him, I waved.* (at that moment)
- Passive: `S + had been + V3` → see [[Passive Voice in Nine Tenses]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I had gone to Hue last year. → ✅ I **went** to Hue last year. (no second past point)
- ❌ After he had went out ... → ✅ After he **had gone** out ...
- ❌ If I had knew ... → ✅ If I **had known** ...

## Contrast with

- [[Past Simple]] — a single finished past action, or the later of two actions
- [[Past Continuous]] — an action in progress around a past moment
- [[Past Perfect Continuous]] — emphasises the **duration** leading up to the past point
- [[Present Perfect]] — links the past to **now**, not to another past moment
- [[Future Perfect]] — the same "before-a-reference-point" idea, moved into the future
