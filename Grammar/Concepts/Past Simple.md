# Past Simple

> [!abstract] In one line
> Reports an action finished at a definite time in the past, with no attention to its present result.

## Form

```
(+) S + V-ed / irregular past form
(-) S + didn't + V(bare)          exception: was/were → wasn't/weren't
(?) Did + S + V(bare)?            exception: Was/Were + S ...?
```

Regular verbs add `-ed` (see [[Spelling Rules for -ED Forms]]); irregular verbs must be learnt by heart.

## When to use

- An action that happened and ended in the past, with no result reaching the present
- A chain of actions happening one after another in the past
- Historical events
- The interrupting action inside a longer past action

## Examples

- In my childhood, I **lived** in a small village.
- Seven years ago, you **were** 17 and I **was** 20.
- This morning I **got** up at 5 o'clock. I **did** morning exercise for 15 minutes. I **brushed** my teeth and **washed** my face.
- President Ho Chi Minh **read** the Declaration of Independence at Ba Dinh Square on 2nd September 1945.
- They were playing football when it **rained**.

## Notes & exceptions

- It goes with finished-time markers: *yesterday, the day before yesterday, last week, last month, last year, in 1975, 20 years ago, once upon a time, when I was a child*.
- The speaker is thinking only about the past and is not interested in how things stand now: *I **lost** my key.* / *I **lived** in Thai Nguyen for 10 years* (I no longer do).
- Verbs of instantaneous change (*start, begin, buy, become, escape, die, marry*) are not used with `since`/`for` in a perfect tense; use the past simple instead: *I **bought** this car two months ago* (not *I have bought this car for two months*).

## Contrast with

- [[Present Perfect]] — no definite past time, result still relevant
- [[Past Continuous]] — an action in progress at a past moment
- [[Past Perfect]] — an action before another past action
