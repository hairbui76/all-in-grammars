# Past Subjunctive in Reported Speech

> [!abstract] In one line
> Structures built on the unreal past — `wish`, `as if / as though`, `would rather`, `it's time`, `had better` — keep their form when they are reported.

## Form

```
"... wish + past / past perfect ..."     ->  ... wished + past / past perfect ...
"... as if / as though + past ..."       ->  ... as if / as though + past ...
"... would rather + S + past ..."        ->  ... would rather + S + past ...
"... it's time + S + past ..."           ->  ... it was time + S + past ...
"... had better ..."                     ->  ... had better ... (or advise sb to do)
```

Only the reporting verb, the pronouns and the time words change.

## When to use

- Whenever the quoted words contain a subjunctive past form
- The past form is already unreal, so backshifting it would add nothing and would change the meaning

## Examples

- "I wish I **knew** the answer," she said → She said that she **wished** she **knew** the answer.
- "I'd rather you **repaired** the car now," the boss said → The boss said that he'd rather I **repaired** the car **then**.
- "He treats me as if I **were** a slave," she said → She said he treated her as if she **were** a slave.
- "She'd better not work for him," Mary said → Mary said that she'**d better** not work for him.

## Notes & exceptions

- `You'd better ...` addressed to someone is more naturally reported with `advise sb (not) to do smth`:
  - "You'd better teach less," the doctor said → The doctor **advised me to teach** less.
  - "You'd better not invest all your savings in gold," she said → She **advised me not to invest** all my savings in gold.
- The same principle covers type 2 and type 3 conditionals → see [[Reported Speech - Conditionals]].
