# Past Subjunctive

> [!abstract] In one line
> The past subjunctive looks like the simple past, except that `be` becomes `were` for all persons; it signals unreality rather than past time.

## Form

```
S + V2/V-ed        (same as the simple past)
I/he/she/it + were (in expressions of doubt or unreality)
```

Past subjunctives are often called **unreal pasts**, because they refer to something imaginary rather than to past time.

## Examples

- He behaves as though he **were** the owner. (But he is not the owner.)
- If I **were** you, I wouldn't do that.
- I'd rather you **spoke** English during the lesson.
- It's time we **went**.
- I wish I **knew** his address.

## Where the past subjunctive appears

- after `as if` / `as though` — see [[As If and As Though]]
- after `it is (high) time` — see [[It Is Time]]
- after `would rather` — see [[Would Rather]]
- after `wish` and `if only` — see [[Wish and If Only]]
- in Type 2 and Type 3 conditionals — see [[Second Conditional]] and [[Third Conditional]]

## Notes & exceptions

- With `be`, both `was` and `were` occur, but `were` is more usual when doubt or unreality is expressed.
- The past subjunctive never changes in reported speech: *"I wish I lived nearer my work," he said* → He said he wished he **lived** nearer his work.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ He behaves as though he is the owner. (when he clearly is not) → ✅ ... as though he **were** the owner.
