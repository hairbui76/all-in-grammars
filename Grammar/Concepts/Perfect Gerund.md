# Perfect Gerund

> [!abstract] In one line
> `having + past participle` replaces the simple gerund when the gerund action happened and was completed before the action of the main verb.

## Form

`having + PII`

## When to use

- To make it explicit that the gerund action is earlier than the main verb.
- Especially common after `deny`, and after verbs of accusing and confessing.

## Examples

- He was accused of **deserting** his ship. *or* He was accused of **having deserted** his ship.
- She confessed to **correcting** / **having corrected** the document.
- He denied **having stolen** the car but admitted **having borrowed** it.
- He denied **having been** there.

## Notes & exceptions

- In most contexts the simple present gerund is preferred; the perfect gerund is used mainly when the time difference matters or after `deny`.
- The passive equivalent is `having been + PII` → see [[Passive Gerund]].
  - The safe showed no signs of **having been touched**.

## Contrast with

- [[Gerunds]] — the simple `-ing` form, neutral as to time
- [[Perfect Participle]] — `having + PII` used adverbially, not as a noun
- [[Perfect Infinitive]] — `to have + PII`, the infinitive equivalent
