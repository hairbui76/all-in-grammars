# Perfect Participle

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Having + past participle` replaces the present participle when the first action was clearly finished before the second one began.

## Form

```
Having + V3 (past participle)
Not having + V3          (negative)
Having been + V3         (passive)
```

## When to use

Use `Having + V3` instead of a plain `V-ing` when you want to show that:

- the first action was **completed** before the second one started
- there was a **long interval** between the two actions
- the first situation lasted a **long time** before the second event

## Examples

- **Having read** the instructions, he took two tablets.
- **Having had** a big breakfast, he doesn't eat anything at lunchtime.
- **Having failed** twice, she doesn't want to try again.
- **Having seen** that film many times, she doesn't want to go to the cinema with me.
- **Having been** his own boss for a long time, he finds it hard to accept orders from another.
- **Having lived** in Thai Nguyen since I was born, I don't want to move anywhere else.

## Notes & exceptions

- The subject of the participle and the subject of the main clause are normally the same person.
- When the two actions follow each other immediately, a simple present participle is enough: `Opening the drawer, he took out a gun.`

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Having read the instructions, two tablets were taken. → ✅ **Having read** the instructions, **he took** two tablets.
- ❌ Have read the instructions, he took two tablets. → ✅ **Having read** ...
