# Personal Pronouns

> [!abstract] In one line
> The eight pronouns that stand at the head of a clause and act as its subject.

## The set

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st (the speaker) | I | we |
| 2nd (the person addressed) | you | you |
| 3rd (the person or thing spoken about) | he, she, it | they |

## When to use

- As the subject of a verb — they normally stand at the beginning of the clause.

## Examples

- **We** are all learning English.
- **They** live in Ha Noi.
- **He** speaks English fluently.

## Notes & exceptions

- English has three persons but only eight personal pronoun forms, because `you` covers both singular and plural.
- After `be` in careful/formal English the subject form is used (`It is I`), but the object form is normal in speech (`It's me`) — see [[Object Pronouns]].
