# Phrasal Verbs - Telephoning and Communication

> [!abstract] In one line
> Phrasal verbs used for making and ending phone calls, passing on information and getting a message across.

## Reference list

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `call around` | phone many different places or people | We **called around** but we weren't able to find the car part we needed. |
| `call someone back` | return a phone call | I **called** the company **back** but the offices were closed for the weekend. |
| `call someone up` | phone someone | Give me your phone number and I will **call** you **up** when we are in town. |
| `call something off` | cancel | Jason **called** the wedding **off** because he wasn't in love with his fiancée. |
| `call on someone` | ask for an answer or an opinion | The professor **called on** me for question 1. |
| `call on someone` | visit someone | We **called on** you last night but you weren't home. |
| `hang on` | wait a short time (informal) | **Hang on** while I grab my coat and shoes! |
| `hold on` | wait a short time | Please **hold on** while I transfer you to the Sales Department. |
| `hang up` | end a phone call | He didn't say goodbye before he **hung up**. |
| `get something across / over` | communicate, make understandable | I tried to **get** my point **across/over** to the judge but she wouldn't listen. |
| `keep something from someone` | not tell someone | We **kept** our relationship **from** our parents for two years. |
| `give something away` | ruin a secret | My little sister **gave** the surprise party **away** by accident. |
| `give someone away` | reveal hidden information about someone | His wife **gave** him **away** to the police. |
| `make something up` | invent, lie about something | Josie **made up** a story about why we were late. |
| `point someone/something out` | indicate with your finger | I'll **point** my boyfriend **out** when he runs by. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `call on someone` has two unrelated meanings; only context distinguishes "ask in class" from "pay a visit".
- `hang on` and `hold on` are interchangeable on the phone, but only `hang on` is used to mean "wait" in informal speech generally.
- Separable verbs (`call someone back`, `make something up`) put a pronoun object **before** the particle: *call **her** back*, never *call back her*.
