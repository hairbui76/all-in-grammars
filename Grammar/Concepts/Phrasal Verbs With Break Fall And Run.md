# Phrasal Verbs With Break Fall And Run

> [!abstract] In one line
> Three base verbs that combine with particles to express breakdown, failure, collapse and chance encounters.

## Examples — break

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| break down | stop functioning (vehicle, machine) | Our car **broke down** at the side of the highway. |
| break down | become emotionally upset | The woman **broke down** when the police told her that her son had died. |
| break something down | divide into smaller parts | Our teacher **broke** the final project **down** into three separate parts. |
| break in | force entry to a building | Somebody **broke in** last night and stole our stereo. |
| break in | interrupt | The TV station **broke in** to report the news. |
| break into something | enter forcibly | The firemen had to **break into** the room to rescue the children. |
| break off | end (a relationship); stop speaking | They **broke off** their engagement. |
| break out | begin suddenly (fire, war); escape | A fire **broke out** in the hospital last night. |
| break out in something | develop a skin condition | I **broke out in** a rash after our camping trip. |
| break up | end a relationship | My boyfriend and I **broke up** before I moved to America. |
| break up | (school) close for the holidays | When does your school **break up**? |

## Examples — fall

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| fall behind | fail to keep up | He **fell behind** with his payments. |
| fall for | fall in love with; be deceived by | The whole family **fell for** the new house as soon as they saw it. |
| fall in with | agree to; happen to meet | She **fell in with** our plans at once. |
| fall off | decrease | Attendance has **fallen off** since Christmas. |
| fall out | quarrel | Jim and Mary **fall out** every few weeks, but their quarrels never last. |
| fall through | fail to happen, come to nothing | The plan **fell through** when it proved too costly. |
| fall back on | rely on as a last resort | If I lose my job I can **fall back on** my savings. |

## Examples — run

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| run across / run into | meet by chance | I **ran into** an old friend when I was going shopping yesterday. |
| run after | chase | The dog **ran after** the postman. |
| run away from | escape from | He **ran away from** home at sixteen. |
| run back | return | She **ran back** to fetch her umbrella. |
| run down | reduce; criticise | The factory is being **run down**. |
| run out of | have no more of | I've **run out of** coffee, will you have tea? |
| run over | knock down with a vehicle | The child was **run over** by a lorry. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `break down` has one transitive separable sense (*break a project down*) and two intransitive senses (a machine, a person).
- `fall through` (a plan fails) is easily confused with `fall out` (people quarrel) and `fall off` (an amount decreases).
- `run out` alone means *be used up* (*the petrol ran out*), but with an object it needs `of` (*we ran out of petrol*).

## Common mistakes

- ❌ We ran out petrol. → ✅ We **ran out of** petrol.
- ❌ The plan fell out. → ✅ The plan **fell through**.
