# Phrasal Verbs With Bring Call And Carry

> [!abstract] In one line
> Three base verbs used for causing things to happen, contacting people and continuing an activity.

## Examples — bring

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| bring about | cause to happen | The war **brought about** enormous social change. |
| bring someone down | make unhappy | This sad music is **bringing** me **down**. |
| bring in | introduce (a law, a system) | They **bring in** a new law about wearing seat belts in cars. |
| bring off | succeed in doing something difficult | Against all odds, he **brought** it **off**. |
| bring on | cause (usually something bad) | The cold weather **brought on** her illness. |
| bring out | publish, produce | The company is **bringing out** a new model. |
| bring round | make someone regain consciousness | They **brought** her **round** with cold water. |
| bring someone up | raise a child | My aunt **brought up** four children. |
| bring something up | start talking about a subject | My mother walks out of the room when my father **brings up** sports. |

## Examples — call

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| call at | pay a short visit to a place | The ship **calls at** Naples on the way. |
| call for | require; come to collect | This achievement **calls for** a celebration. |
| call in / call on | visit briefly | We'd better **call on** his father next Saturday. |
| call off | cancel | They'll have to **call off** the match if it's going to rain. |
| call up | telephone; summon for military service | Please **call** me **up** tomorrow morning. |

## Examples — carry

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| carry off | win, achieve successfully | She **carried off** the first prize. |
| carry on | continue | The baker has **carried on** business here for many years. |
| carry out | perform, put into practice | The repairs were **carried out** last week. |
| carry over | transfer to a later time or account | The balance is **carried over** to the next page. |

## Examples — other B-verbs

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| back somebody up | support | The whole team **backed** her **up**. |
| bear up | keep going bravely under strain | She is **bearing up** well after the accident. |
| bear out | confirm, support (a statement) | The figures **bear out** his claim. |
| bear on | be relevant to, affect | These facts **bear on** the decision. |
| become of | happen to | Whatever **became of** your cousin? |
| blow out | extinguish by blowing | He **blew out** all the candles. |
| blow down | knock down by blowing | The gale **blew down** three trees. |
| blow over | pass without serious effect | The scandal will soon **blow over**. |
| burn away | burn until nothing is left | The candle had **burnt away** completely. |
| burn out | stop burning; exhaust oneself | The fire **burnt out** during the night. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `bear out` (confirm) and `bear up` (endure) are unrelated to `bear on` (be relevant to).
- `blow over` is used figuratively of trouble that passes, not only of weather.
- `bring up` has two very common senses: raise a child, and raise a topic in conversation. The noun form of the first is *upbringing*.
- `call off` = `put off` only in the sense of cancelling; `put off` more usually means *postpone to a later date*.
- `carry on` = `go on` = continue; `carry out` = execute a plan or order, and is often used in the passive.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ They called off the match to next week. → ✅ They **put off** the match until next week.
- ❌ She was brought by her grandmother up. → ✅ She was **brought up** by her grandmother.
