# Phrasal Verbs With Check Clean Clear And Cut

> [!abstract] In one line
> A group of C-verbs whose particles express inspecting, tidying, reducing and removing.

## Examples — check, clean, clear

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| check in | complete the formalities on arrival at a hotel or airport | We **checked in** two hours before the flight. |
| check out | pay and leave a hotel | Guests must **check out** before eleven. |
| check up (on) | investigate, make sure about | The company **checked up on** his references. |
| clean out | clean thoroughly; empty completely | I **cleaned out** the garage at the weekend. |
| clean up | tidy, make clean | **Clean up** this mess before your father gets home. |
| clear away | remove, take out of the way | Please **clear away** the plates. |
| clear up | make clear, solve; (weather) become fine | The police soon **cleared up** the mystery. |

## Examples — close, cut, cross

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| close down | shut permanently (a factory, a business) | The mine was **closed down** in 1998. |
| close in | come nearer, gradually surround | The enemy troops were **closing in**. |
| close up | move closer together; shut completely | The shops **close up** at six on Sundays. |
| cut back on / cut down on | reduce | You should **cut down on** salt. |
| cut in | interrupt | She kept **cutting in** while I was speaking. |
| cut something out of | remove by cutting | He **cut** the article **out of** the newspaper. |
| cut off | disconnect; isolate | The village was **cut off** by the snow. |
| cut up | cut into small pieces | **Cut up** the vegetables before frying them. |
| cross out | delete by drawing a line through | **Cross out** the wrong answer. |

## Examples — catch, chew

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| catch on | become popular; understand | The new fashion never really **caught on**. |
| chew over = think over | consider carefully | Let me **chew** the offer **over** for a day. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `cut down on` and `cut back on` are three-part verbs and are never separated → see [[Three Part Phrasal Verbs]].
- `cut off` is used in the passive far more often than in the active: *We were cut off* (telephone or by weather).
- `check in` / `check out` are intransitive at a hotel, but transitive with luggage: *check in your bags*.
- `clear up` describes both solving a problem and the weather improving.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ You should cut down the sugar. → ✅ You should **cut down on** sugar.
- ❌ The line was cut of. → ✅ The line was **cut off**.
