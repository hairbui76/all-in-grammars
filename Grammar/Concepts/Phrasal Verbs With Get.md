# Phrasal Verbs With Get

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Get` forms one of the largest phrasal-verb families in English, covering movement, recovery, relationships and progress.

## Examples

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| get by | manage, survive | We can't **get by** on such a small income. |
| get down | depress someone | This difficult situation is **getting** me **down**. |
| get down to | start working seriously on | It's time to **get down to** business. |
| get in | arrive; be elected | The train **got in** ten minutes late. |
| get into | enter, board (a car) | She **got into** the taxi and drove away. |
| get off | leave a bus, train or plane | Excuse me, I have to **get off** at the next stop. |
| get on | board a bus, train or plane | Did the man **get on** at the last stop? |
| get on with | have a good relationship with | Does she **get on with** her aunt? |
| get out of | avoid (a duty) | He always tries to **get out of** doing the washing-up. |
| get over | recover from | It took him one month to **get over** his operation. |
| get through | finish, complete | I **got through** all my work before lunch. |
| get through to | make contact with | I rang for an hour but couldn't **get through to** him. |
| get up | rise from bed | My father is used to **getting up** early in the morning. |
| get ahead | make progress, do better than others | She works hard because she wants to **get ahead**. |
| get away with | escape punishment for | It's amazing what his mother lets him **get away with**. |
| get back | return | We **got back** from Spain last Sunday. |
| get at | imply, hint | I don't understand what you're **getting at**. |
| get across | make something understood | The teacher **got** her point **across** clearly. |
| get on someone's nerves | irritate someone | That noise is **getting on** my **nerves**. |
| get round to | find time to do | I never **got round to** answering her letter. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `get on with` and `get along with` are interchangeable in the sense of having a good relationship.
- `get on` / `get off` are used with public transport (bus, train, plane); with a car or taxi English uses `get into` / `get out of`.
- `get down to` and `get round to` end in the preposition `to`, so the next verb is a gerund: *get down to **working***.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I got on the taxi. → ✅ I **got into** the taxi.
- ❌ I must get down to study. → ✅ I must **get down to studying**.
