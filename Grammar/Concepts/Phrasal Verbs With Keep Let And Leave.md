# Phrasal Verbs With Keep Let And Leave

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Keep` idioms are about maintaining or preventing; `let` and `leave` idioms are about allowing, releasing and omitting.

## Examples — keep

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| keep away from = keep off | stay at a distance from | **Keep away from** the edge of the platform. |
| keep out of | stay out of; not get involved in | **Keep out of** other people's arguments. |
| keep somebody back from | prevent somebody from advancing or doing | Illness **kept** him **back from** his studies. |
| keep somebody from = stop somebody from | prevent | Nothing will **keep** me **from** telling the truth. |
| keep people together | keep a group united | Hard times **kept** the family **together**. |
| keep up | maintain, continue at the same level | **Keep up** the good work. |
| keep up with | stay level with | I can't **keep up with** the rest of the class. |
| keep on + V-ing | continue doing something | He **kept on** asking the same question. |

## Examples — let

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| let somebody down | disappoint, fail to support | Don't **let** me **down** this time. |
| let somebody in / out | allow to enter or leave; release | The guard **let** us **in** through the side gate. |
| let somebody off | excuse, not punish | The judge **let** him **off** with a warning. |

## Examples — lay, leave, lie, live, lock

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| lay down | put down (weapons); set out (rules) | The rebels finally **laid down** their arms. |
| lay out | arrange, set out, plan | She **laid out** the plans on the table. |
| leave somebody off = dismiss | dismiss somebody from a job | Fifty workers were **left off** when the plant closed. |
| leave out = omit | not include | You have **left out** an important detail. |
| lie down | rest, especially on a bed | I need to **lie down** for half an hour. |
| live up to | reach the standard expected | The hotel did not **live up to** its reputation. |
| live on | survive on (money, food) | Students often **live on** very little. |
| lock up | lock securely; imprison | Remember to **lock up** before you leave. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `keep on` is followed by a gerund, never an infinitive: *keep on **trying***.
- `keep somebody from doing` and `stop / prevent somebody from doing` are interchangeable → see [[Prevent and Stop Somebody From Doing]].
- `let` in these idioms keeps its causative sense of *allow* and takes a bare infinitive elsewhere → see [[Bare Infinitive]].
- `lay` (lay – laid – laid) takes an object; `lie` (lie – lay – lain) does not: *lay the book down* / *lie down*.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ He kept on to ask. → ✅ He **kept on asking**.
- ❌ I want to lay down for a while. → ✅ I want to **lie down** for a while.
