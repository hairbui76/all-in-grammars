# Phrasal Verbs With Let And Draw

> [!abstract] In one line
> Two small but high-frequency phrasal verb families: `let` for releasing and revealing, `draw` for approaching and drafting.

## Phrasal verbs with let

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| let **on** | reveal a secret | Don't **let on** that we know. |
| let **up** | become less intense, ease off | The rain finally **let up**. |
| let sb **off** | excuse sb, not punish | The judge **let** him **off** with a fine. |
| let sb **down** | disappoint, fail to keep a promise to | Don't **let** me **down** again. |

## Phrasal verbs with draw

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| draw **on** | come nearer in time, approach | As winter **drew on**, the days got shorter. |
| draw sth **up** | draft, prepare a document or plan | The lawyer **drew up** the contract. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `let sb off` and `let sb down` are **separable**: the object goes between the verb and the particle when it is a pronoun (`let him off`, never `let off him`) → see [[Separable Phrasal Verbs]].
- `let on` and `let up` take no object, so nothing can be inserted → see [[Inseparable Phrasal Verbs]].
- `draw up` also means "come to a stop" when used of a vehicle: `A taxi **drew up** outside.`

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Don't let down me. → ✅ Don't **let me down**.
- ❌ He let on the secret to me. → ✅ He **let on** that he knew the secret.
