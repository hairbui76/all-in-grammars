# Phrasal Verbs With Make Do And Give

> [!abstract] In one line
> Three everyday verbs whose particles produce meanings of inventing, managing without, and surrendering.

## Examples — make

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| make for | move quickly towards | After the concert, the crowd **made for** the nearest door. |
| make off with | steal and run away with | The thieves **made off with** the jewels. |
| make out | manage to see or understand | I can't **make out** the meaning of this poem. |
| make over | transfer (property, an account) | He **made over** the house to his daughter. |
| make up | invent; apply cosmetics; be reconciled | He **made up** a story to explain his coming home so late. |
| make up for | compensate for | Nothing can **make up for** the years he lost. |
| make up to | flatter in order to gain something | She is always **making up to** the manager. |

## Examples — do

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| do away with | abolish, get rid of | They should **do away with** this old rule. |
| do up | fasten; redecorate | She **did up** her coat and went out. |
| do with | need, want | I could **do with** a cup of coffee. |
| do without | manage in the absence of | If there's no sugar, we'll have to **do without**. |

## Examples — give

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| give away | give free; reveal (a secret) | He **gave away** all his books to the library. |
| give back | return | Please **give** me **back** my pen. |
| give in | surrender, yield | After a long argument he finally **gave in**. |
| give off | emit (a smell, heat) | The flowers **give off** a sweet scent. |
| give out | distribute; be used up | Our supplies **gave out** after three days. |
| give up | abandon, stop doing | The doctor told Jim to **give up** sweets to lose weight. |
| give way to | yield to, make room for | You must **give way to** traffic from the right. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `make up` is one of the most polysemous phrasal verbs in English: invent a story, put on cosmetics, become friends again after a quarrel, and form a total.
- `do with` (need) and `do without` (manage without) form a natural pair; `do with` in this sense is normally used with `could`.
- `give in` (surrender) is intransitive; `give in something` (hand in a paper) is a different, separable verb.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I could do a cup of tea. → ✅ I could **do with** a cup of tea.
- ❌ He gave up to the pressure. → ✅ He **gave in to** the pressure.
