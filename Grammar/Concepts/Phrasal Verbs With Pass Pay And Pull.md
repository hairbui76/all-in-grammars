# Phrasal Verbs With Pass Pay And Pull

> [!abstract] In one line
> P-verbs whose particles express passing on, settling a debt and pulling something down or aside.

## Examples — pass

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| pass away | die (polite) | Her grandfather **passed away** peacefully. |
| pass by | go past | We **passed by** the old school on our way home. |
| pass on to = hand down to | transmit to someone else or to a later generation | The story was **passed on to** each new generation. |
| pass out | faint, lose consciousness | He **passed out** in the heat. |

## Examples — pay and point

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| pay somebody back | return money owed; take revenge | I'll **pay** you **back** at the end of the month. |
| pay up | pay all the money owed | The company finally **paid up** after a court order. |
| point out | draw attention to, indicate | She **pointed out** the mistake in my calculation. |

## Examples — pull

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| pull back | withdraw, retreat | The army **pulled back** across the border. |
| pull down = knock down | demolish | The old cinema was **pulled down** last year. |
| pull in to | (a train) arrive at a station | The express **pulled in to** platform four. |
| pull something out | take something out | The dentist **pulled** the tooth **out**. |
| pull over | (a vehicle) stop at the side of the road | The police made him **pull over**. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `pass away` = die; `pass out` = faint. They look similar but are unrelated.
- `pay back` has a literal sense (money) and a figurative one (revenge): *I'll pay him back for that remark*.
- `pull down` and `knock down` both mean *demolish*, but only `knock down` also means *hit with a vehicle*.
- `pay somebody back` and `pull something out` are separable; with a pronoun object separation is compulsory: *pay **him** back*.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ His grandmother passed out last week. (meaning: died) → ✅ His grandmother **passed away** last week.
- ❌ I'll pay back you tomorrow. → ✅ I'll **pay you back** tomorrow.
