# Phrasal Verbs With Save See And Send

> [!abstract] In one line
> S-verbs used for putting money aside, taking care of something, saying goodbye and summoning people.

## Examples — save and see

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| save up | put money aside for a purpose | They are **saving up** for a house. |
| see about = see to | deal with, take care of | I'll **see about** the tickets tomorrow. |
| see somebody off | go with somebody to say goodbye at a station or airport | We **saw** her **off** at the airport. |
| see through somebody | recognise somebody's true character | I **saw through** him at once. |
| see over = go over | inspect, look round (a house, a building) | We **saw over** the flat before signing. |

## Examples — send and ring

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| send for | send a message asking somebody to come | They **sent for** an ambulance immediately. |
| send somebody to | send into (hospital, prison) | The boy was **sent to** hospital with a broken arm. |
| send back | return something to where it came from | The meal was cold, so we **sent** it **back**. |
| ring back | return a telephone call later | I'm busy now — can I **ring** you **back**? |
| ring off | end a telephone call | He **rang off** without saying goodbye. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `see to` and `see about` both mean *deal with*, but `see to it that ...` introduces a whole clause: *See to it that the door is locked*.
- `see somebody off` (say goodbye) is different from `see through somebody` (understand their real motive) and `see something through` (finish it despite difficulty).
- `ring off` is British English; American English prefers `hang up` → see [[Phrasal Verbs - Telephoning and Communication]].
- `send back`, `see off` and `ring back` are separable and must be split around a pronoun: *see **her** off*.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ We sent to the doctor. → ✅ We **sent for** the doctor.
- ❌ I'll ring back you tonight. → ✅ I'll **ring you back** tonight.
