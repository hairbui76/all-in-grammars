# Predicative-Only Adjectives

> [!abstract] In one line
> A closed set of adjectives — most of them beginning with `a-` — can only follow a linking verb and can never stand in front of a noun.

## Form

```
✅ Subject + be/look/feel/seem + adjective
❌ Determiner + adjective + Noun
```

## The list

| Adjective | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ablaze | on fire |
| asleep | sleeping |
| afraid | frightened |
| alike | the same |
| alight | burning |
| awake | not sleeping |
| alone | by oneself |
| aware | conscious of |
| alive | living |
| afloat | floating |
| ashamed | feeling shame |
| awash | flooded with water |

Also predicative-only, without `a-`: `ill` (sick), `well` (healthy), `content` (satisfied), `lit` (given light), `exempt` (excused), `unable` (not able).

## Examples

- The girl is **afraid** when she is alone. [not: an ~~afraid girl~~]
- Was there anyone **alive** after the accident? [not: ~~alive people~~]
- A cigarette set the dry grass **alight**.
- The two brothers look **alike**.
- In a moment most of the house was **ablaze**.
- He feels **ashamed** when he has made a careless mistake.
- Is the boy **awake** or **asleep**?
- The boat stuck on a sandbank but we soon got it **afloat** again.
- Tom is feeling quite **content**.
- The candle is **lit**.
- The boy isn't **well**. He looks **ill**.

## Notes & exceptions

- To express the same idea before a noun, use a different word: `a healthy man` (not ~~a well man~~), `sick people` (not ~~ill people~~).
- `contented` and `lighted` are the attributive counterparts of `content` and `lit`, and can be used in any position: *a contented smile*, *a lighted candle*.
- `alive` before a noun becomes `living`: `living people`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ an afraid girl → ✅ a **frightened** girl
- ❌ a well man → ✅ a **healthy** man
- ❌ ill people → ✅ **sick** people
