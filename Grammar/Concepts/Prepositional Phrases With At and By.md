# Prepositional Phrases With At and By

> [!abstract] In one line
> `At` phrases mostly express a state or a degree; `by` phrases mostly express a means, a manner or an accident.

## Examples — at

| Phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| at times | occasionally | **At times** he can be very rude. |
| at hand | near, within easy reach | Keep a dictionary **at hand** while you read. |
| at heart | in one's true nature, deep down | He seems severe, but he is kind **at heart**. |
| at once | immediately | Come here **at once**! |
| at length | in detail; at last | She described the accident **at length**. |
| at a profit | making money on a deal | They sold the flat **at a profit**. |
| at a moment's notice | with almost no warning | A good doctor must be ready to leave **at a moment's notice**. |
| at present | now, at this time | He is **at present** working in Japan. |
| at all costs | whatever it takes | We must finish the report **at all costs**. |
| at war | fighting a war | The two countries were **at war** for a decade. |
| at a pinch | if it is absolutely necessary | **At a pinch**, this car will seat five people. |
| at ease | relaxed, comfortable | I never feel **at ease** in his company. |
| at rest | not moving; comfortable | The engine is quiet when the machine is **at rest**. |
| at least | not less than | It will cost **at least** fifty pounds. |
| at most | not more than | She is twenty **at most**. |

## Examples — by

| Phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| by sight | recognising someone without knowing them | I know him only **by sight**. |
| by chance | accidentally, not planned | I met my old teacher quite **by chance**. |
| by mistake | in error, not intentionally | I took your umbrella **by mistake**. |
| by heart | from memory | She learnt the whole poem **by heart**. |
| by oneself | alone, without help | The child crossed the road **by himself**. |
| by all means | certainly, of course | **By all means**, take the car. |
| by degrees | gradually, little by little | **By degrees** the pain wore off. |
| by land | overland, not by sea or air | They travelled to Turkey **by land**. |
| by no means | not at all, definitely not | The result is **by no means** certain. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `at least` / `at most` set a lower and an upper limit and always come before the number.
- `by chance` = `by accident` = accidentally; but `by mistake` stresses that the wrong thing was chosen.
- `by no means` is a negative adverbial: when it opens a sentence it forces inversion → see [[Inversion with Negative Phrases]].
- `by + means of transport` takes no article: *by land*, *by sea*, *by air*, *by bus* → see [[Prepositions Of Movement]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I learnt it by my heart. → ✅ I learnt it **by heart**.
- ❌ He came in at chance. → ✅ He came in **by chance**.
