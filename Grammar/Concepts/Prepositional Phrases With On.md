# Prepositional Phrases With On

> [!abstract] In one line
> A closed set of fixed adverbial phrases formed with `on` + noun, each learnt as a single unit.

## Form

`on + (the / one's) + noun`

## Examples

| Phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| on second thoughts | after reconsidering | **On second thoughts**, I'll stay at home. |
| on the contrary | the opposite is true | He isn't lazy; **on the contrary**, he works very hard. |
| on the average | taking a typical figure | **On the average**, she reads two books a month. |
| on one's own | alone, without help | She built the whole website **on her own**. |
| on foot | walking | The village can only be reached **on foot**. |
| on purpose | deliberately | I'm sure he broke it **on purpose**. |
| on time | punctually, at the arranged moment | The train left exactly **on time**. |
| on the whole | generally speaking | **On the whole**, the trip was a success. |
| on fire | burning | The old factory was **on fire** all night. |
| on and off | now and then, not continuously | It rained **on and off** all weekend. |
| on the spot | immediately, there and then | He was offered the job **on the spot**. |
| on sale | offered at a reduced price; available to buy | These coats are **on sale** until Sunday. |
| on duty | at work, in charge for a period | Which nurse is **on duty** tonight? |

## Notes & exceptions

- `on time` (punctual) contrasts with `in time` (early enough) → see [[Prepositional Phrases With In]].
- `on one's own` = `by oneself` = alone → see [[Prepositional Phrases With At and By]].
- `on and off` = `from time to time` = `at times` — three ways of saying *occasionally*.
- `on sale` (reduced price, or on the market) is not the same as `for sale` (available to be bought).

## Common mistakes

- ❌ He did it by purpose. → ✅ He did it **on purpose**.
- ❌ In the whole, it was fine. → ✅ **On the whole**, it was fine.
