# Prepositional Phrases With Out Of Under and Within

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Out of + noun` says that something is missing, finished or beyond limits; `under` and `within` mark the opposite states.

## Form

`out of + noun` · `under + noun` · `within + noun`

## Examples — out of

| Phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| out of work | unemployed | He has been **out of work** since the factory closed. |
| out of date | old-fashioned; no longer valid | My passport is **out of date**. |
| out of reach | too far away to be reached | Keep medicines **out of reach** of children. |
| out of money | having no money left | We were **out of money** by the third day. |
| out of danger | no longer at risk | After the operation she was **out of danger**. |
| out of use | no longer used | That spelling has been **out of use** for years. |
| out of the question | impossible, not worth discussing | Lending you more money is **out of the question**. |
| out of order | not working properly | The lift is **out of order** again. |

## Examples — under, within, from

| Phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| under control | being managed successfully | The fire is now **under control**. |
| under arrest | held by the police | The suspect is **under arrest**. |
| within reach | near enough to be reached | The shops are **within reach** of the hotel. |
| from time to time | occasionally | **From time to time** I still write to her. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `out of reach` and `within reach` are exact opposites; so are `in danger` and `out of danger` → see [[Prepositional Phrases With In]].
- `out of order` describes a machine; a person who behaves badly can also be *out of order* in informal British English.
- `from time to time` = `at times` = `on and off` = occasionally.
- After `out of`, a verb takes the `-ing` form: *out of doing* → see [[Gerund After Prepositions]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ The lift is out of the order. → ✅ The lift is **out of order**.
- ❌ It is out of question. → ✅ It is **out of the question**. (impossible)
