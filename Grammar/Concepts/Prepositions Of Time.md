# Prepositions Of Time

> [!abstract] In one line
> `At` marks a clock time or festival, `on` marks a day or date, and `in` marks a longer period such as a part of the day, a month, a season or a century.

## AT — clock times, festivals, fixed phrases

- at 5 o'clock / 11.30 / midnight / night / lunchtime / sunset / sunrise / dawn / noon
- at the weekend / Easter / Christmas (Americans and Australians say *on the weekend*)
- at present / at the moment / at this (that) time / at the same time
- at the end / beginning of (this month), at the age of (25)

Examples:
- They arrived **at** 2 o'clock yesterday.
- My father often works **at** night.
- We got up **at** dawn and reached the summit **at** noon.
- Will you be here **at / on** the weekend?
- Tom and Peter arrived **at the same time**.
- **At the end of** the holiday, I needed another holiday.
- He came to live in London **at the age of** twenty-eight.

## ON — days of the week, dates, particular days

- on Saturday, on 7th February, on this (that) day
- on Sunday evenings, on Christmas (Easter) Day, on New Year's Day, on my birthday

Examples:
- They arrived **on** Saturday.
- I was born **on** 7th February.
- He'll be at home **on** Friday morning.
- We met **on** Easter Day and went for a walk across the hills.
- We always go out for a meal on our wedding anniversary **on** 18th September.

## IN — parts of the day, months, seasons, years, decades, centuries

- in the morning / afternoon / evening, in the Easter holiday, in the summer term
- in August, in (the) summer, in 2002, in the 1990s, in the 20th century, in the Middle Ages, in the 3rd millennium

Examples:
- He always gets up early **in** the morning.
- Most students look for temporary jobs **in** the summer holidays.
- Our football season often begins **in** August every year.
- It's always cold **in (the)** winter.
- This church was built **in** 1910.
- We have seen many important changes in science **in** the last five decades.
- There will be a lot of scientific achievements **in** the twenty-first century.

`In` also expresses a period of time in the future, and the phrase `in the end` (= finally, at last):
- Peter will be here **in** a moment.
- They will be coming **in** a few minutes.
- Could you run thirty miles **in** a day?
- Daisy has gone away. She'll be back **in** a week.
- My car used a lot of petrol. **In the end** I sold it and bought another one.
- Peter got more and more angry. **In the end** he walked out of the room.

## Other prepositions of time

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `until / till` | up to a point in time | I'll be in Hanoi **till** Saturday. |
| `from ... to` | from one point to another | Every morning he plays tennis **from** 6 **to** 7. |
| `by` | before or at a point in time | You must be home **by** half past seven. |
| `for` | how long something lasts | I lived in Paris **for** three months. |
| `since` | from a starting point up to now | I've been waiting for her **since** half past six. |
| `during` | at some point within a period | I slept for 20 minutes **during** the play. |

- You can borrow this book **until** Thursday.
- I watched TV **from** 9 **to** 12 last night.
- She'll have to return the book **by** next Monday.
- The shop will be closed **for** ten days.
- She has lived here **since** 1996.
- You have to be quiet **during** the performance.

## Common mistakes

No preposition is used before `all, each, some, this, next, last, every, today, yesterday, tomorrow`.

- ❌ in all morning → ✅ He hasn't been here **all morning**.
- ❌ on next Saturday → ✅ She's going to Oslo **next Saturday**.
- ❌ on tomorrow → ✅ Goodbye. See you **tomorrow**.
- ❌ in last week → ✅ He came to see me **last week**.
- ❌ on yesterday → ✅ The weather was beautiful **yesterday**.
