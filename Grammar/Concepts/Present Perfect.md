# Present Perfect

> [!abstract] In one line
> Links a past action to the present: the time is not stated, but the result or the period still matters now.

## Form

```
(+) S + have/has + past participle
(-) S + have/has + not + past participle
(?) Have/Has + S + past participle?
```

## When to use

- An action completed in the past when no definite past time is mentioned
- An action that began in the past, has lasted up to now, and has either just finished or is still going on (typically with stative verbs)
- In the `if`-clause of a real present condition (Type 1)
- After a time conjunction (`when, by the time, after, till/until, as soon as`) in a sentence about the future, to stress that one action finishes before the next begins

## Examples

- I **have done** my homework. — They **have redecorated** the living room.
- He **has been** to many cities in Viet Nam.
- I **have been** a teacher for 10 years. — We **have known** each other since our schooldays.
- This car **has belonged** to my family for 5 years.
- If you **haven't eaten** anything, I will cook something for you.
- When I **have taken** the exam, I will go on holiday.

## Notes & exceptions

- It goes with unfinished-period markers: *recently, lately, so far, up to now, today, this week, this month*.
- Two ways of expressing the same span: *I **haven't seen** him for 5 years* = *It **is** 5 years since I last saw him* = *The last time I saw him was 5 years ago* = *I last saw him 5 years ago*.
- Verbs of instantaneous change (*start, begin, buy, become, escape, die, marry*) do not take `since`/`for` in this tense — use [[Past Simple]]: *He **became** a teacher in 1996*, not *He has become a teacher since 1996*.

## Contrast with

- [[Past Simple]] — definite past time, no present relevance
- [[Present Perfect Continuous]] — stresses the continuity, not the result
- [[Been To and Gone To]]
