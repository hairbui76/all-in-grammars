# Prevent and Stop Somebody From Doing

> [!abstract] In one line
> The standard pattern for saying that one thing makes another thing impossible: `prevent / stop / keep + object + from + V-ing`.

## Form

```
S + prevent / stop / keep + somebody or something + from + V-ing
S + be + prevented / stopped / kept + from + V-ing   (passive)
```

## When to use

- To say that an obstacle makes an action impossible.
- `prevent` is the most formal, `stop` the most common, `keep` suggests continuous effort.

## Examples

- The heavy rain **prevented us from going** out.
- Nothing will **stop him from telling** the truth.
- Her illness **kept her from attending** the meeting.
- The fence **prevents the sheep from escaping**.
- The crowd **was kept from entering** the stadium.

## Notes & exceptions

- With `stop`, `from` is usually left out: *You can't stop me leaving.* With `prevent` it may be omitted only in formal British English: *prevent him (from) leaving*.
- `from` is a preposition, so the verb after it must be a gerund, never an infinitive → see [[Gerund After Prepositions]].
- The related pattern with a to-infinitive uses a different verb: *forbid somebody **to do** something*.
- `stop somebody doing` (prevent) is not the same as `stop to do something` (interrupt an activity in order to do something else) → see [[Verbs Changing Meaning With Gerund Or Infinitive]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ The rain prevented us to go out. → ✅ The rain **prevented us from going** out.
- ❌ He stopped me from to leave. → ✅ He **stopped me from leaving**.
