# Pronunciation of -ED Endings

> [!abstract] In one line
> The regular past ending `-ed` is read `/ɪd/`, `/t/` or `/d/`, decided by the final sound of the base verb.

## Rule

| Ending is read | After base verbs ending in | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| `/ɪd/` | `/t/` or `/d/` | want**ed**, need**ed**, add**ed**, provid**ed**, invit**ed**, permitt**ed**, post**ed**, exhibit**ed**, hesitat**ed**, plant**ed**, predict**ed** |
| `/t/` | `/s/` (`ce, ss`), `ch`, `sh`, `x`, `k`, `p`, `/f/` (`f, gh`) | pass**ed**, miss**ed**, danc**ed**, plac**ed**, watch**ed**, snatch**ed**, wash**ed**, finish**ed**, mix**ed**, fix**ed**, work**ed**, cook**ed**, stopp**ed**, kidnapp**ed**, laugh**ed**, cough**ed** |
| `/d/` | all other sounds | plough**ed**, lov**ed**, arriv**ed**, rais**ed**, plann**ed**, water**ed**, learn**ed**, liv**ed**, mov**ed**, climb**ed** |

## Notes & exceptions

- Only `/ɪd/` adds an extra syllable; `/t/` and `/d/` do not.
- A few adjectives keep an old `/ɪd/` pronunciation even though the verb does not: *aged*, *learned* (= scholarly), *blessed*.
