# Questions in the Passive Voice

> [!abstract] In one line
> How a question turns passive depends on whether the question word is the subject or the object of the active sentence.

## Form

**A. The question word is the SUBJECT** (no auxiliary in the active question; the verb is third person singular).

`Who/What + V + O?` → `By whom + be + S + P2?` or `Who/What + be + S + P2 + by?`

Apply the ordinary passive formula for the tense concerned.

**B. The question word is the OBJECT** (the active question needs an auxiliary).

`Wh- + auxiliary + S + V?` → build the ordinary affirmative passive of that tense, with the question word now standing as the passive subject.

## Examples

Question word as subject:

- **Who loves** her? → **By whom is** she **loved**? / **Who is** she **loved by**?
- **Who has broken** the glass window? → **By whom has** the glass window **been broken**?
- **What ruined** Oxford city? → **By what was** Oxford city **ruined**?
- **Who will solve** the problem? → **By whom will** the problem **be solved**?

Question word as object:

- **Who(m) does** she **love**? → **Who is loved** by her?
- **Who have** the police **arrested**? → **Who has been arrested** by the police?
- **Which wine did** they **order**? → **Which wine was ordered** by them?
- **What can** we **do** to protect our environment? → **What can be done** to protect our environment?

## Notes & exceptions

- `Who ... by?` is the informal order; `By whom ...?` is formal.
- In pattern A the answer to the original question is always `S + auxiliary`, which is a useful test for recognising it.
