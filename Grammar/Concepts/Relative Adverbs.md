# Relative Adverbs

> [!abstract] In one line
> `where`, `when` and `why` introduce relative clauses in place of an adverbial of place, time or reason.

## Form

| Relative adverb | Replaces | Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| `where` | adverbial of place | `in which` / `at which` |
| `when` | adverbial of time | `in which` / `on which` / `at which` |
| `why` | reason after `the reason` | `for which` |

A relative adverb is always followed by a full clause; it can never be the subject of the clause.

## Examples

`where`:

- I love Vietnam. I was born and grew up in Vietnam/there. → I love Vietnam **where/in which I was born and grew up**.
- Do you remember the place **where/at which you first met**?
- He went to Hollywood **where/in which he made a film**.
- In 1919 he went to the Italian Front **where he was badly wounded**.
- This is the school **where/at which I used to study**.
- A birthplace is a place **where you were born**.
- A cinema is a place **where films are shown**.
- A cemetery is a place **where dead people are buried**.
- A laboratory is a room **where experiments are done**.

`when`:

- 1975 is the year **when/in which I was born**.
- It was the time **when we lived happily**.
- I must find a time **when I can tell you the truth**.
- I still remember a time **when/at which cars were rarely seen in the village**.
- I still remember a time **when people had to queue**.

`why`:

Pattern: `The reason (why) + clause + is/was + that + clause` or `+ to + V`

- The reason **why** I don't like him **is that** he is dishonest.
- The reason **why** we come here **is to learn** English.
- The reason **why** I don't like that house **is that** it hasn't got a garden.
- The reason **why** I like working as a teacher **is that** I love children.
- The reason **why** he was late for work **was that** his car broke down on the way.

## Notes & exceptions

- `where` cannot be the subject of its clause; if the place is the subject, use `which`/`that`.
- `why` is often omitted after `the reason`.
- The complement after `The reason ... is` should be `that + clause`, not `because`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ The reason why he was late was because his car broke down. → ✅ The reason why he was late was **that** his car broke down.
