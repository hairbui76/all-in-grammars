# Reported Speech - Questions

> [!abstract] In one line
> A reported question loses its question word order and its question mark; the tense, pronoun and time changes are the same as for statements.

## Form

```
Yes/No question:  S + asked (+ O) + if / whether + S + V
Wh- question:     S + asked (+ O) + wh-word + S + V
```

The subject comes **before** the verb, and no auxiliary `do/does/did` is used.

## When to use

- `if` or `whether` when the original question had no question word
- The original question word (`what`, `where`, `how long`, `who`, `which` ...) when it did

## Examples

Yes/No questions:

- "Did you go out last night?" he asked → He asked me **if / whether I had gone** out the previous night.
- "Is anyone there?" Mary asked → Mary asked **if / whether anyone was** there.
- "Do you know Bill?" he said → He asked **if / whether I knew** Bill.
- "Are you doing anything at the moment?" she asked → She asked me **if / whether I was doing** anything at that moment.

Wh- questions:

- "How much do you think it would cost?" she said → She asked me **how much I thought** it would cost.
- "How long does it take to get to Edinburgh by coach?" asked Ann → Ann asked me **how long it took** to get to Edinburgh by coach.

## Notes & exceptions

- `if` and `whether` are usually interchangeable, and `if` is the more frequent.
- Prefer `whether` when a real choice is stressed: *"Do you want to go by air or sea?" the travel agent asked → The travel agent asked **whether** I wanted to go by air or by sea.*
- `whether ... or not` is the fixed pattern for "whether or not": *He asked **whether or not** I wanted to insure my luggage.* / *He asked **whether** I wanted to insure my luggage **or not**.*
- After `wonder` and `want to know`, `whether + to-infinitive` is possible: *"Shall I wait for them or go now?" he wondered → He wondered **whether to wait** for them or go on.*
- Use `whether`, not `if`, when the reported question contains a conditional clause, to avoid two `if`s: *"If you get the job, will you move to York?" Bill asked → Bill asked **whether** I would move to York **if** I got the job.*
- The reporting verb may be `ask`, `want to know`, `wonder`, `inquire`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ He asked me where did I live. → ✅ He asked me **where I lived**.
- ❌ She asked if did I know Bill. → ✅ She asked **if I knew** Bill.
