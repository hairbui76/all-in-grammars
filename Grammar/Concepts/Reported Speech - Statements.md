# Reported Speech - Statements

> [!abstract] In one line
> A statement is reported with `say/tell (that) + clause`, keeping the meaning but changing the words to the reporter's point of view.

## Form

```
Direct:   S + said, "..."
Indirect: S + said (that) + clause
          S + told + O + (that) + clause
```

`that` may be omitted. `say` takes no personal object; `tell` must have one.

## When to use

- To pass on what someone stated, rather than quoting the exact words
- Direct speech keeps the original words inside quotation marks; indirect speech does not

## Examples

- Mary said, "I have never been to Paris." → Mary **said that she had never been** to Paris.
- "We are learning lesson five today," said Tom. → Tom **said that they were learning** lesson five **that day**.
- "I'm looking for a job," Jane says. → Jane **says that she is looking** for a job.
- "This is the first time I have eaten this kind of food," he says. → He **says that this is** the first time **he has eaten** this kind of food.

## Notes & exceptions

- **Present-tense reporting verb** (`says`, `is saying`, `will say`): the tense of the quoted verb does not change; only pronouns and other referring words are adjusted.
- **Past-tense reporting verb** (`said`, `told`): the quoted verb is normally backshifted → see [[Backshift of Tenses]].
- **No backshift for permanent truths**, natural laws and unchanging habits:
  - "Two and two makes four," he said → He said two and two **makes** four.
  - "The sun rises in the east," the teacher said → The teacher said that the sun **rises** in the east.
  - "Ho Chi Minh City is bigger than Hanoi," my father said → My father said that Ho Chi Minh City **is** bigger than Hanoi.
  - "I always have bread and eggs for breakfast," he said → Peter said that he always **has** bread and eggs for breakfast.
