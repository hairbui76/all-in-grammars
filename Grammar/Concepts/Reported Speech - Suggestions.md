# Reported Speech - Suggestions

> [!abstract] In one line
> Sentences that propose an action are reported with `suggest` (four possible patterns) or with `advise`, never with `suggest + to-infinitive`.

## Form

Direct forms that make a suggestion:

```
Let's + V(bare)
Shall we + V(bare)?
What about + V-ing?
How about + V-ing?
Why don't you + V(bare)?
Why not + V(bare)?
```

Reported with:

```
1. suggest (to sb) that + S + (should) + V(bare)
2. suggest that + S + V
3. suggest + sb's + V-ing
4. suggest + V-ing
```

## When to use

- `suggest` when the speaker proposes a shared or general course of action
- `advise sb to do smth` when the proposal is aimed at one person as advice

## Examples

Direct suggestion forms:

- **Let's** go out for a change. / **Let's** eat out tonight.
- **Shall we** have a short break? / **Shall we** have a barbecue?
- **What about** going to the concert tonight?
- **How about** travelling by train? / **How about** inviting the Browns to the party?
- **Why don't you** ask Jack for help? / **Why not** ask Jack for help?

Reported:

- "Shall we go swimming this afternoon?" he said → He **suggested that we should go** swimming that afternoon.
- "Let's stop our lesson here and finish it later," said the teacher → The teacher **suggested that we should stop** our lesson there and finish it later.
- "Why don't you take off your coat?" he said → He **advised me to take off** my coat.

## Notes & exceptions

- `suggest` is never followed by an object + to-infinitive: ❌ *He suggested me to go.*
- After `suggest that`, `should` may be dropped, leaving the bare infinitive (the mandative subjunctive).
- `Why don't you ...?` and `You'd better ...` are usually reported with `advise`, because they address one person.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ He suggested us to go swimming. → ✅ He **suggested (that) we should go** swimming.
