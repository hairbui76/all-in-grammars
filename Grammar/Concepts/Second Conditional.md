# Second Conditional

> [!abstract] In one line
> An unreal or unlikely situation in the present or future, and its imagined result.

## Form

`If + S + V(past simple / past subjunctive), S + would/should/could/might + V(bare)`

The past tense here is not past time — it signals unreality.

## When to use

- Situations that are objectively contrary to present fact
- Situations the speaker merely imagines and does not expect to happen
- Giving advice with `If I were you, ...`

## Examples

- If I **were** you, I **would marry** her.
- If I **had** a lot of money, I **would travel** around the world.
- If I **knew** the answer, I **would tell** you.
- If pigs **had** wings, they **would fly**.
- She **would be** fitter if she **took** exercise regularly.
- What **would** you **do** if you **were** rich?

## Notes & exceptions

- `were` is used for all persons in the hypothetical use: *If I **were** a bird, I would like to be a dove.* (`was` is heard informally: *If my great grandfather **was/were** alive ...*)
- Continuous version for an imagined action at the moment of speaking:
  `If + S + were + V-ing, S + would be + V-ing`
  *If I **were not studying** English here now, I **would be watching** TV at home.*
- `could` in the if-clause expresses an ability contrary to fact: *If I **could speak** English well, I would apply for that job.*
- `were to + V` in the if-clause makes a future hypothesis more remote, often with `might/could`: *If Wind **were to come** next Monday, I **might have** time to see him.*

## Common mistakes

- ❌ If I would have more time, I would learn the piano. → ✅ If I **had** more time, ...
