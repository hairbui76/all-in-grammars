# Sentence Connector Punctuation

> [!abstract] In one line
> A sentence connector joins two INDEPENDENT clauses, so the clauses must be separated by a semicolon or a full stop — never by a comma alone.

## Form

```
Independent clause; connector, independent clause.
Independent clause. Connector, independent clause.
```

The connector belongs to the SECOND clause and is normally followed by a comma.

## Examples

- Mary wanted it**;** ***nevertheless***, Stephen gave it away.
- Mary wanted it**.** ***Nevertheless***, Stephen gave it away.
- I thought the plane would be delayed**;** ***therefore***, I bought a book to read.
- I thought the plane would be delayed**.** ***Therefore***, I bought a book to read.

## Position of the connector

The connector does not have to stand first. It can also sit in the middle or at the end of its clause, marked off by commas.

- Mary was happy. **However**, Alice was very sad.
- Mary was happy. Alice, **however**, was very sad.
- Mary was happy. Alice was very sad, **however**.

## Notes & exceptions

- After `otherwise` the comma is often omitted: *You have to press the button. **Otherwise** the machine won't work.*
- `Instead` may stand at the beginning or at the very end of its clause → see [[Connectors of Alternative]].
- Short connectors such as `so`, `thus`, `yet` frequently appear without the following comma.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Mary wanted it, nevertheless Stephen gave it away. (comma splice)
- ✅ Mary wanted it; nevertheless, Stephen gave it away.
