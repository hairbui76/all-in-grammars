# Should Have Done

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Should / ought to / had better + have + past participle` criticises the past: the right thing was not done, or the wrong thing was.

## Form

`S + should / had better + have + PII` — it was advisable but was not done
`S + shouldn't + have + PII` — it was done but should not have been
`S + ought to + have + PII` — same meaning as `should have + PII`
`S + should + have been + V-ing` — it ought to have been going on at that moment but wasn't

## Examples — positive criticism

- You **should have asked** your parents for permission before you went out last night.
- You **should have told** her the truth.
- He **had better have had** his eyes tested a long time ago.
- You **should have made** us speak English during the lesson.
- My boss was angry with me because I hadn't done the work. I **ought to have done** it the previous week.

## Examples — negative criticism

- They **shouldn't have played** truant.
- They **shouldn't have taken** my motorbike away without asking me for permission.
- You **shouldn't have forced** us to learn by heart too many words at one time.

## Examples — continuous form

- When I arrived, he was watching TV. He **shouldn't have been watching** TV. He **should have been doing** his homework.

## Notes & exceptions

- `ought to have + PII` is fully equivalent to `should have + PII`.
- The present-time counterpart is `should be + V-ing` for something that ought to be happening now: *He **should be wearing** a warm coat.*
