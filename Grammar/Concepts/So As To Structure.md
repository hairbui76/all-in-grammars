# So As To Structure

> [!abstract] In one line
> `So + adjective + as + to-infinitive` says that someone was foolish, stupid or kind *enough* to do something — and it is most common in polite requests.

## Form

`S + be + so + adj + as to + V`

Request form: `Would you be so + adj + as to + V ...?` = `Would you be + adj + enough + to V ...?`

## Examples

- She was **so foolish as to leave** her bicycle unlocked.
- He was **so stupid as to turn down** such a good job.
- She was **so stupid as to turn down** his marriage proposal.
- Would you be **so kind as to forward** my letter?
- Would you be **kind enough to forward** my letter?
- Would you be **so good as to move** your bike? It's in my way.

## Notes & exceptions

- In statements this pattern usually carries a critical or evaluative tone ("she was silly enough to ...").
- In questions it is a very polite request formula, interchangeable with `adjective + enough + to V`.

## Contrast with

- [[Enough With Infinitive]] — `kind enough to V` is the everyday equivalent
- [[Too With Infinitive]] — expresses excess making an action impossible
