# Stress and Vowel Sounds

> [!abstract] In one line
> If you can see the transcription of a word, the vowel qualities themselves tell you where the stress falls.

## Rule 1 — stress never falls on /ə/ or /əʊ/

A syllable containing the schwa `/ə/` or the diphthong `/əʊ/` is never the stressed syllable.

| Word | Transcription | Stress | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| mother | /ˈmʌðə/ | 1st | 2nd syllable has /ə/ |
| hotel | /ˌhəʊˈtel/ | 2nd | 1st syllable has /əʊ/ |

**Exception to keep in mind:** when a word contains *both* `/ə/` and `/əʊ/`, the stress goes to the part with `/əʊ/`.

| Word | Transcription | Stress |
|---|---|---|
| suppose | /səˈpəʊz/ | 2nd |
| ago | /əˈɡəʊ/ | 2nd |
| opponent | /əˈpəʊnənt/ | 2nd |

## Rule 2 — stress prefers long vowels, diphthongs and heavy endings

Stress usually falls on a syllable with a long vowel, a diphthong, or one that ends in more than one consonant.

| Word | Transcription | Stress | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| disease | /dɪˈziːz/ | 2nd | long vowel /iː/ |
| explain | /ɪksˈpleɪn/ | 2nd | diphthong /eɪ/ |
| comprehend | /ˌkɒmprɪˈhend/ | 3rd | ends in two consonants /nd/ |

## Rule 3 — all-short-vowel words take first-syllable stress

If every vowel in the word is short, the stress falls on the first syllable.

| Word | Transcription | Stress |
|---|---|---|
| happy | /ˈhæpi/ | 1st (/æ/ and /i/ both short) |
| animal | /ˈænɪml/ | 1st (/æ/, /ɪ/, /ə/ all short) |

## Notes & exceptions

- These rules are read off the transcription; they are the fallback when no suffix or prefix rule applies.
- Affix rules override them → see [[Suffixes That Take Stress]] and [[Suffixes That Shift Stress to the Previous Syllable]].
