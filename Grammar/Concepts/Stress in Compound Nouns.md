# Stress in Compound Nouns

> [!abstract] In one line
> In a compound noun the primary stress falls on the first element, whatever the two parts are made of.

## Rule

`COMPOUND NOUN` → stress the **first** word

## Patterns

| Pattern | Stress | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Noun + Noun | 1st noun | **bir**thday /ˈbɜːθdeɪ/, **air**port /ˈeəpɔːt/, **gate**way /ˈɡeɪtweɪ/, **boy**friend /ˈbɔɪfrend/, **green**house /ˈɡriːnhaʊs/, **sea**food /ˈsiːfuːd/, **tooth**paste /ˈtuːθpeɪst/ |
| Adjective + Noun | the adjective | **black**board /ˈblækbɔːd/, **green**grocer /ˈɡriːnˌɡrəʊsə/, **grand**parents /ˈɡrænˌpeərənts/ |
| Gerund + Noun | the gerund | **wash**ing machine /ˈwɒʃɪŋməˌʃiːn/, **wait**ing room /ˈweɪtɪŋrʊm/ |
| Noun + Gerund | the noun | **hand**writing /ˈhændˌraɪtɪŋ/, **day**dreaming /ˈdeɪdriːmɪŋ/ |
| Noun + Verb + -er | the noun | **goal**keeper /ˈɡəʊlkiːpə/, **hol**idaymaker /ˈhɒlɪdeɪˌmeɪkə/ |

## Notes & exceptions

- `greenhouse` /ˈɡriːnhaʊs/ (a glass building) contrasts with the free phrase `green house` (a house painted green), which is stressed on `house`.
- The rule is the opposite of compound verbs and most compound adjectives → see [[Stress in Compound Verbs and Adjectives]].
