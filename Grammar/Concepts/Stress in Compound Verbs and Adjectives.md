# Stress in Compound Verbs and Adjectives

> [!abstract] In one line
> Compound verbs and most compound adjectives put the primary stress on the **second** element, unlike compound nouns.

## Compound verbs

`compound verb` → stress the second word

- become /bɪˈkʌm/
- understand /ˌʌndəˈstænd/

## Compound adjectives

| Pattern | Stress | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Noun + Adjective | the **noun** (1st) | **home**sick /ˈhəʊmsɪk/, **air**tight /ˈeətaɪt/, **trust**worthy /ˈtrʌstˌwɜːði/ |
| Noun + V3 (past participle) | the **participle** | hand**made** /hændˈmeɪd/ |
| Adjective + Adjective | the **2nd** adjective | red-**hot** /redˈhɒt/, dark**blue** /ˌdɑːkˈbluː/ |
| Adjective + Noun + -ed | the **2nd** element | bad-**tem**pered /ˌbædˈtempəd/, old-**fash**ioned /əʊldˈfæʃənd/ |
| Adjective + Noun | the **adjective** (1st) | **long**-distance /ˈlɒŋˌdɪstəns/, **high**-quality /ˌhaɪˈkwɒlɪti/ |
| Adjective + V3 | the **2nd** element | clear-**cut** /klɪəˈkʌt/, ready-**made** /ˌrediˈmeɪd/ |
| Adverb + Gerund | the **2nd** element | far-**reach**ing /fɑːˈriːtʃɪŋ/, hard-**work**ing /ˌhɑːdˈwɜːkɪŋ/ |

## Notes & exceptions

- The two patterns that keep the stress on the first element are `Noun + Adjective` (homesick) and `Adjective + Noun` (long-distance).
- The general habit is easy to remember as: **compound noun → front; compound verb/adjective → back**.
