# Structures For Time and Money

> [!abstract] In one line
> Two interchangeable patterns for saying how much time or money an activity costs: `It takes somebody ... to do` and `somebody spends ... doing`.

## Form

```
It + take(s)/took + somebody + amount of time + to-infinitive
S + spend(s)/spent + amount of time or money + V-ing
S + spend(s)/spent + amount of time or money + on + noun
S + waste(s)/wasted + time or money + V-ing
```

## When to use

- `It takes ... to do` puts the focus on the task; the person is the object.
- `spend ... doing` puts the focus on the person, who is the subject.
- `on + noun` replaces the gerund when the complement is a thing, not an action.
- `waste` replaces `spend` when the time or money produced nothing useful.

## Examples

- **It takes** me five minutes **to get** to school.
- **It took** him ten minutes **to do** this exercise yesterday.
- I **spend** two hours **reading** books a day.
- She **spent** all of her money **on** clothes.
- Don't **waste** your time **arguing** with him.

## Notes & exceptions

- The two patterns convert into each other: *It takes me an hour to cook dinner* = *I spend an hour cooking dinner*.
- After `take` the verb is a to-infinitive; after `spend` and `waste` it is a gerund → see [[Verb Patterns With Gerund and Infinitive]].
- `It` here is an empty preparatory subject, not a pronoun with a referent → see [[Infinitive As Subject]].
- With no person mentioned, the object is simply dropped: *It takes three hours to fly there*.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I spend two hours to read books. → ✅ I **spend** two hours **reading** books.
- ❌ It takes me five minutes going to school. → ✅ It **takes** me five minutes **to go** to school.
- ❌ She spent all her money for clothes. → ✅ She **spent** all her money **on** clothes.
