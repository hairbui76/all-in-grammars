# Subjunctive in That Clauses

> [!abstract] In one line
> Verbs of advising, demanding and proposing take `that + subject + (should) + bare infinitive`; when `should` is dropped, the bare infinitive alone carries the subjunctive.

## Form

```
S + V + that + S + (should) + V(bare) ...
```

## Verbs used with this pattern

`advise, agree, arrange, ask, beg, command, decide, demand, determine, insist, order, propose, recommend, request, stipulate, suggest, urge, prefer, decree`

Also the expressions `be anxious`, `be determined`.

## Examples

- She advised **that we should keep** the gate locked.
- She advised **that the gate should be kept** locked. (also: *She advised keeping the gate locked* / *advised us to keep it locked*.)
- They agreed / decided **that the roof should be repaired**. (also: *They agreed to repair the roof*.)
- He arranged **that I should go** abroad. (also: *He arranged for me to go abroad*.)
- They arranged **that the minister should be met** at the airport.
- He suggested **that a petition (should) be drawn up**.

## Notes & exceptions

- `that ... should` is **more formal** than the gerund or infinitive alternative, and implies less direct contact between the person advising and the person who will act.
- `recommend` sounds even more formal than `advise` in this pattern.
- When the infinitive is `be`, `should` is often omitted, leaving a bare subjunctive: `He suggested that a petition **be drawn up**.`

## Common mistakes

- ❌ He suggested that a petition is drawn up. → ✅ He suggested that a petition **(should) be drawn up**.
- ❌ He suggested me to draw up a petition. → ✅ He suggested **that I (should) draw up** a petition.
