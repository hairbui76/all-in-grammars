# Subjunctive with Impersonal Expressions

> [!abstract] In one line
> After `It is / was + adjective`, a `that`-clause with `should` (or a bare subjunctive) replaces the `for + object + to`-infinitive construction.

## Form

```
It is/was + adjective + that + S + should + V(bare)
```

## Two groups of adjectives

### 1. Adjectives of necessity and desirability

`advisable, better, desirable, essential, imperative, important, natural, necessary`; also `fair (= just), just, right` (often preceded by `only`), and `reasonable`.

- It is advisable **that everyone should have** a map.
- It is better for him to hear it from you. / It is better **that he should hear** it from you.
- It is essential for him to be prepared for this. / It is essential **that he should be prepared** for this.
- It is only right **that she should have** a share.
- `should` may be dropped before `be`: It is essential **that he be prepared**.

### 2. Adjectives of surprise and judgement

`absurd, amazing, annoying, ludicrous, odd, ridiculous, strange, surprising` and similar adjectives. Here `that ... should` is an alternative to `that` + present or past tense.

- It is ridiculous **that we should be** short of water in a country where it is always raining. (= that we are)
- It is amazing **that she should have said** nothing about the murder. (= that she said)

## Notes & exceptions

- With group 2, a **perfect infinitive** (`should have + V3`) is used when referring to past events.
- The `for + object + to`-infinitive version is less formal: *It is essential **for him to be** prepared.*

## Common mistakes

- ❌ It is essential that he is prepared. → ✅ It is essential that he **should be / be** prepared.
- ❌ It is amazing that she should said nothing. → ✅ It is amazing that she **should have said** nothing.
