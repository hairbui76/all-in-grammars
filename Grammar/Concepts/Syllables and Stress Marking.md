# Syllables and Stress Marking

> [!abstract] In one line
> Every English word of two or more syllables has one syllable pronounced louder, higher and longer than the rest — that syllable carries the primary stress.

## What a syllable is

- A syllable is one unit of pronunciation built around a vowel sound (`/ʌ/`, `/æ/`, `/ɑː/`, `/ɔɪ/`, `/ʊə/` …), with or without consonants around it.
- A word may have one, two, three or more syllables.

| Word | Transcription | Syllables |
|---|---|---|
| quickly | /ˈkwɪkli/ | 2 |
| beautiful | /ˈbjuːtɪfʊl/ | 3 |

## How stress is marked

- In phonetic transcription the primary stress is shown by `ˈ` placed **before and above** the stressed syllable.
- A secondary stress is shown by `ˌ` below the line.

`ˈ` + syllable → that syllable is stressed

| Word | Transcription | Stressed syllable |
|---|---|---|
| happy | /ˈhæpi/ | 1st |
| clever | /ˈklevər/ | 1st |
| arrange | /əˈreɪndʒ/ | 2nd |
| engineer | /ˌendʒɪˈnɪə/ | 3rd |

## Why stress matters

- Native speakers stress every polysyllabic word; wrong stress makes a word hard to recognise even if every sound is correct.
- Stress alone can change word class and meaning: `desert` /ˈdezət/ (n) *a dry region* vs `desert` /dɪˈzɜːt/ (v) *to abandon* → see [[Noun Verb Stress Pairs]].
