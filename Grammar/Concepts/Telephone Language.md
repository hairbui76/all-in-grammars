# Telephone Language

> [!abstract] In one line
> The fixed formulas of a telephone call: asking for a person, identifying yourself, holding the line, and leaving or taking a message.

## Useful patterns

| Stage | Caller | Person answering |
|---|---|---|
| Asking for someone | `Could I speak to Ann?` · `May I speak to the manager?` | `Who's calling, please?` |
| Identifying yourself | `This is Daisy speaking.` · `Daisy here.` | `Speaking.` |
| Holding | `I'll hold, thanks.` | `Just a moment, please.` · `Hold on a moment.` |
| Person unavailable | — | `I'm afraid he is not in.` · `I'm sorry. Ann's not in.` · `I'm afraid she's not here at the moment.` |
| Message | `Can I leave a message then?` · `May I leave a message for Ms Davis?` | `Can I take a message?` · `Would you like to leave a message?` |
| Confirming | — | `Yes, I'll make sure she gets it.` |
| Enquiry call | `Hi, I'd like some information about driving lessons.` | `Sure, could I have your name and address?` |
| Closing | `Thank you. Goodbye.` | `Goodbye.` |

## When to use

- On the phone English uses `This is ... speaking`, never `I am ...`.
- Use `Could I ...?` or `May I ...?` to ask for a person; `Can you ...?` would ask the other person to do something else.
- The unavailability formula is `He's not in` / `She's not here at the moment` — always softened with `I'm afraid` or `I'm sorry`.
- Whoever raises the message depends on the roles: the caller *leaves* a message, the person answering *takes* one.

## Examples

- "**Could I speak to Ann?**" — "**I'm sorry. Ann's not in.**"
- "**May I speak to the manager?**" — "**I'm afraid he is not in. Can I take a message?**"
- "Sorry, the manager is not here." — "**Can I leave a message then?**"
- "**May I leave a message for Ms Davis?**" — "**Yes, I'll make sure she gets it.**"

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "Could I speak to Ann?" — "This is Daisy speaking." (identifies the wrong person; the caller asked for Ann)
- ❌ "Sorry, the manager is not here." — "Can I speak to the manager, please?" (the answer has already been given)
- ❌ Mixing up *leave* and *take* a message.
