# Third Conditional

> [!abstract] In one line
> An unreal condition in the past and the past result that therefore never happened.

## Form

`If + S + had (not) + V3/V-ed, S + would/should/might/could + have + V3/V-ed`

## When to use

- Regret or criticism about the past
- Speculating about how the past could have turned out differently
- Explaining that a past result depended on a condition that was not met

## Examples

- If you **had worked** hard, you **would have passed** the exam.
- If my car **hadn't broken down**, I **should have met** her.
- If they **had left** earlier, they **would have caught** the train.
- It **would have been** better if they **hadn't come**.
- If the storm **hadn't swept** through, the crop **wouldn't have been** a failure.
- What **would** they **have done** if they **hadn't been** arrested?

## Notes & exceptions

- Past perfect continuous can replace past perfect in the if-clause when the unreal condition was an action in progress at a definite past moment:
  *If it **hadn't been raining**, we could have gone out.*
  *If I **hadn't been sleeping** then, I would have heard the phone ring.*
  *If she **had been wearing** her seat belt, she wouldn't have been hurt so badly.*
- Formal variant with `were to have + V3`: *If I **were to have known** that you were ill, I would ...* (= If I had known ...)

## Common mistakes

- ❌ If you would have worked hard, you would have passed. → ✅ If you **had worked** hard, ...
