# Three Part Phrasal Verbs

> [!abstract] In one line
> A verb followed by an adverb *and* a preposition; the three words behave as a single unit and the object always comes last.

## Form

`V + adverb + preposition + object`

The object can never be moved inside the group, not even a pronoun:
*I can't put up with **it**.* (not ~~put it up with~~)

## Examples

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| put up with | tolerate | I can't **put up with** her another day, she never stops complaining. |
| catch up with | reach the same level as | He spent six months **catching up with** his studies. |
| come up with | think of, suggest | I hope you can **come up with** a better plan than this. |
| come up against | face, meet (a difficulty) | Sorry to be late, but we **came up against** some unexpected delays. |
| come up to | reach the standard of | The play **came up to** our expectations. |
| get on with | have a good relationship with | Does she **get on with** her aunt? |
| get away with | escape punishment for | It's amazing what his mother lets him **get away with**. |
| look forward to | anticipate with pleasure | I'm **looking forward to** meeting you again. |
| look down on | despise | The whole school **looks down on** such behaviour. |
| look up to | respect | Every child needs someone to **look up to**. |
| live up to | reach the standard set by | Children will always **live up to** your expectations of them. |
| do away with | abolish | The government decided to **do away with** the tax. |
| cut down on | reduce | The doctor advised Peter to **cut down on** smoking. |
| run out of | have no more of | I've **run out of** coffee, will you have tea? |
| go down with | fall ill with | Our holiday was fine until Tom **went down with** a bad cold. |
| go along with | agree to | I can't **go along with** your childish behaviour any longer. |
| face up to | confront | You must **face up to** the fact that you were wrong. |
| fall back on | rely on as a last resort | If the plan fails we can **fall back on** our savings. |
| keep up with | stay level with | I can't **keep up with** the rest of the class. |
| stand up for | defend | We'll **stand up for** you in spite of difficulties. |
| make up for | compensate for | Nothing can **make up for** the time we lost. |
| get through to | make contact with | I couldn't **get through to** her all morning. |
| put up for | ask someone to give a bed to | They **put** us **up for** the night. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `look forward to`, `object to`, `get down to` and `take to` end in the preposition `to`, so a verb after them must be a gerund: *I look forward to **hearing** from you.* → see [[Gerund After Prepositions]].
- A few of these have close two-word partners with different meanings: `come up` (arise) vs `come up with` (invent); `put up` (build) vs `put up with` (tolerate).

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I look forward to meet you. → ✅ I look forward to **meeting** you.
- ❌ I can't put it up with. → ✅ I can't **put up with** it.
