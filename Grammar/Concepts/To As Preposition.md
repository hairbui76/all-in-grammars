# To As Preposition

> [!abstract] In one line
> `to` is sometimes part of an infinitive (`to + V`) and sometimes a true preposition, in which case it must be followed by a gerund, a noun or a pronoun.

## Form

- `to` as part of the infinitive: `to + V(bare)`
- `to` as a preposition: `to + V-ing / noun / pronoun`

## When to use

**1. `to` is part of the infinitive** after the auxiliaries `be, have, ought, used` and after `going` (`be going to`). Here `to` is often left standing alone to avoid repeating the verb, especially after `hate, hope, intend, would like/love, mean, plan, try, want`.

- Did you buy cheese? ~ No, I meant **to** (buy some) but the shop was shut.
- Would you like to go to the cinema with me tonight? ~ I'd like **to** / I'd love **to**.
- He sings more beautifully than he used **to**.
- Have you fed the cat yet? ~ No, but I'm going **to**.

**2. `to` is a preposition** in fixed expressions such as `look forward to`, `take to`, `be accustomed to`, `be/get used to`, `object to`, `confess to`. It is then followed by a gerund, noun or pronoun.

- I am looking forward **to** my holidays / **to** next weekend / **to** it.
- I am looking forward **to seeing** you.
- I am used **to** heat / **to** hard work.
- I am used **to standing** in queues / **to** it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I'm used to get up early. → ✅ I'm used **to getting** up early.
- ❌ I look forward to hear from you. → ✅ I look forward **to hearing** from you.
