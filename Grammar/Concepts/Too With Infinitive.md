# Too With Infinitive

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Too` is a degree adverb placed before an adjective or another adverb; with a following infinitive it means "so ... that it is impossible".

## Form

`S + be / V + too + adj / adv + (for sb) + to V`

`too + adj + a/an + N + (for sb) + to V`

## The infinitive refers to the subject of the sentence

- She is **too young to get married**.
- She's **too tired to go on working**.
- I am **too weak to lift** the stone.
- I'm **too short to touch** the ceiling.
- He is **too poor to buy** even an old bicycle.
- He ran **too slowly to reach** the destination first.
- He sings **too badly to become** a singer.

## The infinitive refers to the object of a verb

Here the object is not repeated after the infinitive.

- The cup of tea is **too hot (for me) to drink**.
- The ceiling is **too high for me to touch**.
- The exercise is **too difficult for her to do**.
- You are speaking **too softly for me to hear**.
- The case is **too heavy for her to carry**.

## The infinitive refers to the object of a preposition

The preposition stays at the end.

- The grass is **too wet (for us) to sit on**.
- You are walking **too fast for me to keep up with**.
- The tiger is **too dangerous to play with**.
- The light is too dim **to read by**.

## With a noun: too + adj + a/an + N

- He is **too shrewd a businessman to be cheated**.
- It's **too marvellous an opportunity for us to miss**.
- It's **too dirty a restaurant for me to eat in**.

## Related transformations

The same idea can be expressed with `so ... that` and `such ... that`:

- This cake is **too hard for me to eat**.
- This cake is **so hard that** I can't eat it.
- This is **such a hard cake that** I can't eat it.
- It is **too hard a cake for me to eat**.
- It is **so hard a cake that** I can't eat it.
- **So hard is the cake that** I can't eat it.
- **Such is the hardness of the cake that** I can't eat it.
- The hardness of the cake is **such that** I can't eat it.
