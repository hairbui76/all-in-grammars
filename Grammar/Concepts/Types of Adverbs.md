# Types of Adverbs

> [!abstract] In one line
> Adverbs are grouped by the question they answer — how, where, when, how often, how much — plus the interrogative, relative and sentence adverbs.

## The eight classes

| Class | Answers | Typical members |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adverbs of manner | How? | bravely, fast, happily, hard, quickly, well |
| Adverbs of place | Where? | by, down, here, there, near, up |
| Adverbs of time | When? | now, soon, still, then, today, yet, yesterday |
| Adverbs of frequency | How often? | always, never, occasionally, often, twice |
| Interrogative adverbs | in questions | where, when, why |
| Relative adverbs | join clauses | where, when, why |
| Sentence adverbs | comment on the whole clause | certainly, definitely, luckily, surely |
| Adverbs of degree | To what extent? | fairly, quite, rather, hardly, too, very |

## Examples

- She danced **beautifully**. (manner)
- Bill is **upstairs**. (place)
- I'll wait till **tomorrow**. (time)
- She **rarely** goes to the cinema on Sundays. (frequency)
- **Where** did you put it? (interrogative)
- I looked **where** he pointed. (relative)
- **Luckily**, nobody was hurt. (sentence)
- The exam was **pretty** difficult. (degree)

## Notes & exceptions

- The same word can belong to more than one class: `where` and `when` are both interrogative and relative adverbs.
- Sentence adverbs modify the whole clause, not the verb, and are usually separated by a comma.
