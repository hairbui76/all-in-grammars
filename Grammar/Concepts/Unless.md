# Unless

> [!abstract] In one line
> `Unless` means "if ... not"; it introduces the one condition that would prevent the result.

## Form

`Unless + S + V(affirmative), main clause`

`Unless` already contains the negative, so the verb after it stays positive.

## When to use

- To rewrite an `if ... not` clause more compactly
- To state the single exception under which the main clause will not hold

## Examples

- **Unless** it **rains**, we will go sightseeing. (= If it doesn't rain, we will go sightseeing.)
- **Unless** you **work** hard, you will fail the exam. (= If you don't work hard, ...)
- I shan't wake **unless** I **hear** the alarm.
- My dog never starts a fight. He never growls **unless** the other dog **growls** first.

## Notes & exceptions

- When the original if-clause is **affirmative**, rewriting with `unless` requires the **main clause** to be negated instead:
  *If you promise to drive carefully, I will lend you my car.*
  → **Unless** you promise to drive carefully, I **won't** lend you my car.
- The same reversal applies to unreal conditionals:
  *If it had more rain, my crop would grow faster.* → **Unless** it **had** more rain, my crop **wouldn't grow** faster.
- The two clauses of an `unless` sentence are always opposite in polarity to the corresponding `if` sentence.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ Unless you don't work hard, you will fail. → ✅ **Unless you work hard**, you will fail.
