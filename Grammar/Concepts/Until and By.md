# Until and By

> [!abstract] In one line
> `until` = up to a point and continuing; `by` = at or before a point, one moment — Vietnamese translates both as "đến", which is why they get swapped.

## The core difference

| | `until` / `till` | `by` |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | the situation **continues** up to that time, then stops | the event happens **at or before** that time, once |
| Shape in time | a **line** ending at the point | a **point**, a deadline |
| Verb it goes with | durative: `wait`, `stay`, `sleep`, `remain`, `keep`, `be open` | punctual/completive: `finish`, `arrive`, `submit`, `leave`, `have done` |
| Vietnamese | "cho đến (tận)…" | "trước / chậm nhất là…" |

- I will **wait until** six o'clock. *(the waiting fills the whole time)*
- I will **finish by** six o'clock. *(one moment, no later than six)*

A quick test: if you can insert **"and not before"** the word is `by`; if you can insert **"the whole time"** it is `until`.

## Grammar of each word

`until` works two ways:

- **Preposition** + noun / time expression: *The shop is open **until** midnight.*
- **Conjunction** + clause: *Stay here **until** I **come** back.*
  The clause takes the [[Present Simple]] for future time, never `will` → see [[Clauses of Time]].

`by` is **only a preposition** — it always needs an object (`by Friday`, `by then`, `by the end of the month`). To put a clause after it you must use `by the time`:

- *I will have packed **by the time** you **arrive**.*

`till` is the same word as `until`, just more informal; both are correct. Avoid the spelling `til`.

## By then and until then

`then` here means "that time" and points back to a time already mentioned, so neither expression can be the first mention of the time.

- The train leaves at six. **By then** we will have finished packing. *(finished, no later than six)*
- The train leaves at six. **Until then** we will be packing. *(packing continues right up to six)*

## Not ... until

`not ... until` means the event happens only at that time and not earlier:

- He **didn't** call me **until** midnight. *(the call came at midnight)*
- The results **won't be** published **until** Monday.

Fronting it forces inversion: *__Not until__ midnight **did he** call me.* → see [[Inversion with Not Until]].

## Until and before

Both can end a period, but `before` only fixes the boundary while `until` insists the situation lasted the whole way:

- I lived in Huế **until** 2020. *(the living continued right up to 2020)*
- I moved out **before** 2020. *(one event, some time earlier)*

## Notes & exceptions

- `until now` normally implies a change has just happened: *__Until now__ we have used paper forms.* (we are stopping). For a neutral "so far", prefer `so far` or `up to now`.
- `from ... until / to`: *The exhibition runs **from** March **until/to** June.*
- Distance and quantity take `up to`, not `until`: ✗ *until 50 kilometres* → ✓ ***up to** 50 kilometres*.
- `by` also has unrelated meanings — agent in the [[Passive Voice]] (*written **by** Tolkien*), and means of transport (*travel **by** train*). Only the time sense contrasts with `until`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I will have finished the report until Friday → ✅ I will have finished the report **by** Friday
- ❌ Please wait here by I get back → ✅ Please wait here **until** I get back
- ❌ I will stay here by tomorrow → ✅ I will stay here **until** tomorrow
- ❌ Submit the form until 5 p.m → ✅ Submit the form **by** 5 p.m
- ❌ We will leave until she arrives → ✅ We **won't leave until** she arrives
- ❌ Stay here until I will come back → ✅ Stay here until I **come** back
- ❌ By the time he will arrive, we will have eaten → ✅ **By the time he arrives**, we will have eaten
