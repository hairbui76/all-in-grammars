# Verb Patterns With Gerund and Infinitive

> [!abstract] In one line
> English verbs fall into four complementation patterns: `V + V-ing`, `V + to V`, `V + O + V(bare)` and `V + O + to V`.

## Form

```
V + V-ing          e.g. enjoy swimming
V + to V           e.g. decide to leave
V + O + V(bare)    e.g. make him work
V + O + to V       e.g. advise him to work
```

## The four lists

| `V + V-ing` | `V + to V` | `V + O + V(bare)` | `V + O + to V` |
|---|---|---|---|
| appreciate | afford | make | advise |
| admit | appear | let | allow |
| avoid | arrange | have | encourage |
| can't help | decide | hope | force |
| can't stand | fail | catch | invite |

| consider | happen | watch | persuade |
| deny | intend | feel | remind |
| dislike | learn | find | teach |

## Notes & exceptions

- `look forward to`, `object to`, `be/get used to` end in the preposition `to`, so the `-ing` form follows → see [[To As Preposition]].
- After the `V + O + V(bare)` verbs of perception (`see, watch, hear, feel, find, catch, observe`), a gerund is also possible with a change of aspect: *I saw him **cross** the road* (complete) vs *I saw him **crossing** the road* (in progress).
- In the passive, `make` takes the `to`-infinitive: *He was made **to work** harder.*
