# Verbs Followed By For

> [!abstract] In one line
> A family of verbs that take the fixed preposition `for`, usually adding the idea of *a thing wanted, requested or accounted for*.

## Form

`V (+ object) + for + noun / pronoun / V-ing`

## Examples

| Combination | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| account for | explain; make up a proportion of | How do you **account for** the missing money? |
| allow for | take into account in advance | **Allow for** heavy traffic on Friday evening. |
| answer for | be responsible for | One day you will have to **answer for** what you did. |
| ask for | request | He **asked for** a glass of water. |
| care for | like; look after | Would you **care for** a cup of tea? / She **cares for** her elderly mother. |
| call for | require; come to collect | The situation **calls for** immediate action. |
| count on someone for something | rely on someone to supply something | You can **count on** me **for** a lift home. |
| cry for something | demand or call out for | The baby was **crying for** its mother. |
| cry for the moon | ask for something impossible | Wanting a pay rise now is **crying for the moon**. |
| die for | want something desperately | I'm **dying for** a cold drink. |
| fill in for someone | do someone's work while they are away | Can you **fill in for** me on Monday? |
| go for | try hard to obtain | She is **going for** the top prize. |
| go in for | take part in; be keen on | He **goes in for** long-distance running. |
| look for | try to find | I've been **looking for** my keys all morning. |
| look out for | watch carefully in order to spot | **Look out for** a blue van outside the bank. |
| make up for | compensate for | Nothing can **make up for** the years we lost. |
| pay for | give money in exchange for | Who is going to **pay for** the damage? |
| send for | send a message asking someone to come | They **sent for** the doctor at once. |
| jump for joy | be extremely happy | She **jumped for joy** when the results came out. |

## Notes & exceptions

- `care for` has two very different senses: *like / want* (usually in offers and questions) and *look after*.
- `look for` = search; `look out for` = keep watching so that you notice something → see [[Phrasal Verbs With Look]].
- `go in for` = `take part in`; `go for` = try to win or obtain → see [[Phrasal Verbs With Come And Go]].
- A verb after `for` must be a gerund: *account for **losing** the money* → see [[Gerund After Prepositions]].

## Common mistakes

- ❌ He asked a glass of water. → ✅ He **asked for** a glass of water.
- ❌ I am looking my keys. → ✅ I am **looking for** my keys.
