# Verbs of Perception with V-ing and Bare Infinitive

> [!abstract] In one line
> After `see, hear, watch, notice, feel, smell, listen to`, a present participle shows part of an action in progress, while a bare infinitive shows the whole action from start to finish.

## Form

```
smell / see / hear / notice / watch / listen to / feel + sb + V-ing   (action in progress)
smell / see / hear / notice / watch / listen to / feel + sb + V(bare) (complete action)
```

## Examples with `V-ing` — part of an action

- I **see him passing** my house every day.
- I suddenly **felt an insect crawling** up my leg.
- He **smelt something burning** and **saw smoke rising**.
- Did you **hear the clock striking**?

## Examples with the bare infinitive — the whole action

- I **saw him come** into the room, walk to the table, open the drawer, take out a document, photograph it and put it back.
- Have you ever **listened to Michael Jackson sing** "Heal the World"?
- He **heard the clock strike** 6 o'clock and knew that it was time for him to go.
- I **saw him get** on the car and drive away.

## Notes & exceptions

- The infinitive is used **without `to`** after these verbs in the active voice.
- Some sentences accept both forms with a small difference in meaning: `Did you hear the clock striking/strike?`
- In the passive, `to` reappears: *He was seen **to enter** the building.*

## Common mistakes

- ❌ I saw him to come into the room. → ✅ I saw him **come** into the room.
- ❌ I felt an insect to crawl up my leg. → ✅ I felt an insect **crawling** up my leg.
