# Wish and If Only

> [!abstract] In one line
> `wish` and `if only` express a desire for something that is not true, using a past form for present wishes and a past perfect for past regrets.

## Form

```
A. wish (that) + S + V(past simple)          -> wish about the present / future
B. wish (that) + S + would + V(bare)         -> wish about someone else's behaviour
C. wish (that) + S + had + V3                -> regret about the past
```

`If only` replaces `wish` with the same patterns and a stronger, more emotional force.

## When to use

- A: the present situation is the opposite of what you want
- B: you are annoyed and want an action changed or started; the subject of `wish` cannot be the subject of `would`
- C: you regret something that did or did not happen in the past

## Examples

Present wishes:

- I **wish** I **knew** his address. (= I'm sorry I don't know his address.)
- **If only** you **could drive** a car.
- I **wish** he **were** coming with us.
- She **wishes** it **didn't rain** much in London.
- **If only** you **were** here now.

Wishes with `would`:

- The radio has been playing since 6 o'clock. I **wish** someone **would turn** it off.
- I **wish** he **would write** more often.
- I **wish** prices **would come** down.
- I **wish** they **would stop** making bombs.

Past regrets:

- I **wish** I **had bought** that house. (= I regret not buying that house.)
- He **wishes** he **hadn't married** her.
- I **wish** I **had been** born rich.
- Do you wish you **had learnt** English instead of Russian?

## Notes & exceptions

- `were` is used for all persons: *I wish I **were** rich and famous.*
- `I wish I would ...` is impossible, because `would` requires a different subject from `wish`.
- `wish + would` asks for a change of **action**, not of a situation.
- `wish + would` works only with actions the subject **can control**, and often expresses interest in that person's willingness or unwillingness: *I wish he **would wear** a coat* (= I'm sorry he refuses to wear one).
- `I wish you would ...` is a possible request form, but it usually carries reproach: *I wish you would help me* often implies "You should have offered to help me"; *I wish you would stop interrupting* shows irritation. As a reply to an offer it carries no such feeling: *Shall I help you check the accounts? ~ I **wish you would**.*
- `if only` can replace `wish + would` in the same way, but not in requests; it is more dramatic: *If only he **would join** our party!*
- `wished` can replace `wish` without changing the subjunctive: *I **wished** I hadn't spent so much money.*
- Equivalent expressions: `I'm sorry (that) ...`, `What a pity ...`, `It is a pity ...`.
- After a past reporting verb, `wish` is not backshifted: *"I wish I knew the answer," she said → She said that she **wished** she **knew** the answer.*
