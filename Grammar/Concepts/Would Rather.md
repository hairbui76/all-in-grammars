# Would Rather

> [!abstract] In one line
> `would rather` (= `would sooner`) expresses preference: a bare infinitive for your own action, a past form when you want someone else to act.

## Form

Same subject — bare infinitive:

```
S + would rather/sooner (not) + V(bare)
S + would rather/sooner + V(bare) + than + V(bare)
S + would rather/sooner (not) have + V3
S + would rather/sooner have + V3 + than have + V3
```

Different subject — past subjunctive:

```
S + would rather + S2 + V(past simple)      -> preference about the present/future
S + would rather + S2 + had + V3            -> preference about the past (unreal)
```

## When to use

- Bare infinitive: you state what **you** prefer to do
- Past simple after a second subject: you want **somebody else** to do or not do something now
- Perfect forms (`have + V3` / `had + V3`): the preference concerns the past and is contrary to fact

## Examples

Own action:

- I feel a bit tired. I'**d rather go** out for a change.
- I'**d rather not go** out tonight.
- I'**d rather live** alone **than marry** such a girl.
- I'**d rather be** out of work **than work** for him.
- I'**d rather travel** by train **than go** by car.
- I'**d rather drive than be driven**.

Own action, past and unreal:

- I'**d rather have said** goodbye to everybody before I left. (= I wish I had said ...)
- I'**d rather not have bought** this second-hand car. (in fact I bought it)
- I'**d rather have bought** a new car **than have had** my old one repaired.

Another person's action:

- I'**d rather he repaired** the car now.
- I'**d rather** you **didn't smoke** in this room. (= I wish you wouldn't smoke in this room.)
- I'**d rather she cooked** dinner now.
- I'**d rather she hadn't taken** my bicycle away without asking my permission.
- I'**d rather** you **spoke** English during the lesson.
- He'**d rather she apologised** to her.
- I'**d rather** you **had asked** me for permission before you had a Friday afternoon off.
- I'**d rather he hadn't written** that letter.
- I'**d rather** you **hadn't played** truant last time.

## Notes & exceptions

- `would sooner` is interchangeable with `would rather`.
- The past tense after a second subject is a subjunctive, not past time.
- These forms are **not** backshifted in reported speech: *"I'd rather you repaired the car now," the boss said → The boss said he'**d rather** I **repaired** the car then.*
- `would like to have + V3` expresses the same unreal wish about the past:
  - I'**d like to have lived** in the war time.
  - I'**d like to have bought** a house in the city.
  - Would you **like to have learned** English instead of Russian?
- `would like to have + V3` has a second meaning — an achievement wanted by a future deadline:
  - Paolo would **like to have travelled** all the countries in Asia by the time he is 40.
  - I would **like to have saved** enough money to buy a car by the end of next year.
