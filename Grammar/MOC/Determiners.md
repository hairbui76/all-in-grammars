# Determiners

> [!abstract] In one line
> Words that introduce and quantify a noun: all, every, each, both, few, much.

## Notes in this area

- [[All Most Some and Half]] — `all, most, some` and `half` stand directly before a noun for a general meaning, but need `of` before a determ
- [[Both Either and Neither]] — These three determiners are used only for two people or two things: `both` = the two, `either` = one or the ot
- [[Countable and Uncountable Nouns]] — Countable nouns have a plural and take `a/an` and numbers; uncountable nouns do not
- [[Enough With Infinitive]] — `Enough` follows an adjective or adverb and, with a following infinitive, means "sufficiently ... to be able t
- [[Every and Each]] — Both take a singular noun and a singular verb, but `every` looks at the group as a whole while `each` looks at
- [[Few A Few Little and A Little]] — `a few` and `a little` mean "some, enough" with a positive feeling, while `few` and `little` mean "hardly any,
- [[Many Much and A Lot Of]] — `many` and `much` express large quantity mainly in negatives and questions, while `a lot of` and `lots of` are
- [[No and None]] — `no` is a determiner that must be followed by a noun, while `none` is a pronoun that stands alone or takes `of
- [[Order of Adjectives]] — When several adjectives modify one noun they follow a fixed sequence and are normally not joined by `and`
- [[Position of Adjectives]] — An adjective either stands in front of the noun it describes (attributive) or follows a linking verb (predicat
- [[Possessive Adjectives]] — Words placed before a noun to show who the noun belongs to
- [[Possessive Case With Apostrophe S]] — `'s` (or a bare apostrophe) marks the owner, normally a person, animal or group
- [[Possessive Pronouns]] — A possessive pronoun replaces "possessive adjective + noun" so the noun does not have to be repeated
- [[Quantifiers]] — Determiners that say **how much** or **how many** — the choice depends on the countability of the noun
- [[Relative Clauses with Whose]] — `whose` replaces a possessive adjective (`my`, `her`, `his`, `their`, `its`) and is always followed directly b
- [[Time and Place Changes in Reported Speech]] — Words that point to the speaker's "here and now" have to be re-anchored to the moment of reporting
