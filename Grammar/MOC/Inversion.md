# Inversion

> [!abstract] In one line
> Putting the auxiliary before the subject for emphasis or after negative adverbials.

## Notes in this area

- [[Cleft Sentences]] — A sentence is split in two so that one element is highlighted between `It is / was` and `that`
- [[Concessive Inversion With As and Though]] — Moving an adjective, adverb or verb to the front of the clause before `as` or `though` gives an emphatic conce
- [[Emphatic Structures]] — `Do / does / did` can be added before the bare infinitive to insist that an action really is or was performed
- [[Inversion after Adverbials of Place]] — When a sentence opens with an adverbial of place or direction, an intransitive verb can be moved in front of i
- [[Inversion in Comparisons]] — In formal written English the subject and verb may be inverted after the comparison words `as` and `than`
- [[Inversion in Conditionals]] — `If` can be dropped and the auxiliary `should`, `were` or `had` moved to the front of the clause — a formal eq
- [[Inversion with Negative Adverbs]] — When a negative or near-negative adverb is fronted for emphasis, the subject and the auxiliary swap places, ex
- [[Inversion with Negative Phrases]] — Fronting a noun phrase or prepositional phrase containing `no` forces question word order in the main clause
- [[Inversion with Neither Nor and So]] — `So`, `neither` and `nor` at the start of a clause mean "the same is true of X" and are followed by inverted w
- [[Inversion with No Sooner and Hardly]] — Fronting `no sooner` or `hardly / barely / scarcely` says that one event happened immediately after another, a
- [[Inversion with Not Only But Also]] — When `not only` opens the sentence, the first clause inverts; the second clause keeps normal word order and ad
- [[Inversion with Not Until]] — Fronting `not until / not till + time expression` inverts the main clause and means "only at that moment did i
- [[Inversion with Only]] — A fronted phrase beginning with `only` restricts the statement and triggers question word order in the main cl
- [[Inversion with So and Such]] — A `so ... that` or `such ... that` result sentence can be made emphatic by fronting the `so`/`such` element an
- [[Question Tags With Negative Adverbs]] — A sentence containing a negative-meaning word counts as negative, so the tag is **positive**
- [[Too With Infinitive]] — `Too` is a degree adverb placed before an adjective or another adverb; with a following infinitive it means "s
- [[Used To And Would For Past Habits]] — `Used to` and `would` describe a habit or state that existed in the past but no longer exists now
