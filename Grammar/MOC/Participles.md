# Participles

> [!abstract] In one line
> Present, past and perfect participles, and the clauses they build.

## Notes in this area

- [[Compound Nouns With Gerund]] — A gerund combines with a noun in either order to build compound nouns, and the order changes the relationship 
- [[Gerunds]] — A gerund is a verb form ending in `-ing` that keeps its verbal meaning but works as a noun in the sentence
- [[Participle Clauses]] — A subordinate clause can be shortened by deleting its subject and conjunction and turning its verb into a part
- [[Participle Clauses Replacing Main Clauses]] — When two main clauses share a subject, one of them can be reduced to a present participle to show simultaneous
- [[Participles as Adjectives]] — A present participle before a noun has an active meaning; a past participle before a noun has a passive meanin
- [[Passive Voice in Nine Tenses]] — The passive is always `be + past participle`; only the form of `be` changes from tense to tense
- [[Passive of Perception Verbs]] — Verbs of perception take a bare infinitive or an `-ing` form in the active; in the passive the bare infinitive
- [[Past Participle]] — The third form of the verb (`V-ed` or an irregular form), used with a passive or completed meaning
- [[Perfect Participle]] — `Having + past participle` replaces the present participle when the first action was clearly finished before t
- [[Present Participle]] — The `-ing` form of a verb, used with an active meaning: as part of continuous tenses, as an adjective, or as t
- [[Reduced Relative Clauses]] — When the relative pronoun is the subject of its clause, the clause can be shortened to a present participle, a
- [[Verb Patterns with V-ing]] — A group of common verbs is regularly followed by an `-ing` form, either directly or after an object
- [[Verbs of Perception with V-ing and Bare Infinitive]] — After `see, hear, watch, notice, feel, smell, listen to`, a present participle shows part of an action in prog
