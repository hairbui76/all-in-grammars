# Verb Tenses

> [!abstract] In one line
> The English tense system: how form and time reference combine across present, past and future.

## Notes in this area

- [[Active and Passive Voice]] — The active sentence puts the doer of the action in subject position; the passive puts the receiver there and m
- [[Backshift of Tenses]] — When the reporting verb is in the past, the verb of the quoted words moves one step further back in time
- [[Be Going To]] — `am/is/are going to + V` — a plan already decided, or a prediction based on present evidence
- [[Been To and Gone To]] — `have been to` = went there and came back; `have gone to` = went there and is still away
- [[Clauses of Time]] — A time clause says **when** the action of the main clause happens, and it never uses a future tense
- [[Expressions With Do]] — `Do` is used for performing an action, activity, task or job, and for vague general activity
- [[Future Continuous]] — `will be + V-ing` — an action that will be in progress at a definite moment in the future
- [[Future Perfect]] — `will have + V3` — an action that will already be finished before a given time in the future
- [[Future Perfect Continuous]] — `will have been + V-ing` — how long an action will have been going on by a point in the future
- [[Future Simple]] — `will + bare infinitive` — a decision, prediction, promise or offer made about the future
- [[Passive Voice in Nine Tenses]] — The passive is always `be + past participle`; only the form of `be` changes from tense to tense
- [[Past Continuous]] — The past equivalent of the present continuous: an action in progress at a particular moment in the past
- [[Past Perfect]] — The "past before the past": an action finished before another point or action in the past
- [[Past Perfect Continuous]] — An action that had been going on for a period of time up to a moment in the past
- [[Past Simple]] — Reports an action finished at a definite time in the past, with no attention to its present result
- [[Prepositions Of Time]] — `At` marks a clock time or festival, `on` marks a day or date, and `in` marks a longer period such as a part o
- [[Present Continuous]] — Describes a temporary action in progress around the moment of speaking
- [[Present Perfect]] — Links a past action to the present: the time is not stated, but the result or the period still matters now
- [[Present Perfect Continuous]] — Stresses how long an action has been going on from the past up to now
- [[Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous]] — The perfect points at the RESULT reached by now; the perfect continuous points at the ACTIVITY that produced i
- [[Present Simple]] — States facts, habits and permanent situations that are true around the present time
- [[Stative Verbs]] — Verbs describing states rather than actions; they are not normally used in any continuous tense
- [[This Is The First Time]] — After `This is the first/second/third ... time`, the verb that follows goes into the present perfect
- [[Used To And Would For Past Habits]] — `Used to` and `would` describe a habit or state that existed in the past but no longer exists now
